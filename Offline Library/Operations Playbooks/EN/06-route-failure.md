# Primary Route Failure Plan

Plan the decision to abandon a route before you are physically committed to it.

### Draw three things
On an offline map or paper, mark the normal path, one genuinely different alternative, and a safe decision point where you can still change direction. Two routes that share the same bridge, tunnel or low crossing are not independent alternatives.

### Failure triggers
Write observable reasons to reject the primary path: official closure, floodwater, smoke/fire direction, structural damage, unsafe crowding, inaccessible stairs, stalled traffic with no safe turnaround, or another local hazard. Avoid vague triggers such as “if it looks bad.”

### Communications
Tell the household which route name/code means what. If someone changes route, define how they update the out-of-area contact or meeting point without expecting continuous mobile data.

### Practice the reversal
During a tabletop exercise, introduce a closure after departure has supposedly begun. Make the group choose from the last safe decision point rather than inventing a shortcut. The important capability is reversing an outdated plan without sunk-cost thinking.
