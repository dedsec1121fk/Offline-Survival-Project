# Emergency Information Discipline

During fast events, an accurate old fact and an unverified new claim can both mislead a decision.

### Every critical note gets three fields
Write the statement, its source, and the time it was obtained. If the source is another person, record whether they directly observed it or repeated it. Keep “unknown” as a valid state rather than filling gaps with inference.

### Separate the board
Maintain three short lists: VERIFIED NOW, UNCONFIRMED, and SUPERSEDED. When an official update changes a route, warning area or service status, move the old item out of the active list instead of leaving conflicting versions side by side.

### Verification budget
Do not spend battery and attention refreshing every channel continuously. Select authoritative sources relevant to the incident and set review times. Social posts may provide leads but should not silently become operational facts.

### Decision link
For any major action, write which information justified it and when that information must be reviewed. If the underlying fact expires—wind direction, road status, restoration estimate—the decision becomes due for review too.

After the incident, keep only records that have recovery value and dispose of sensitive scratch notes appropriately.
