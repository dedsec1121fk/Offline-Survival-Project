# Document Recovery Map

The objective is to know what exists and where it can be recovered without turning the project into a vault of unnecessary secrets.

### Build the map
For each important identity, medical, insurance, property, vehicle, education/employment or legal record, note: who owns it, where the physical original is kept, whether a protected offline copy exists, and which organization could reissue it. Record reference numbers only when genuinely useful and acceptable for your privacy model.

### Test a loss scenario
Pretend the home copy is inaccessible. Can you identify the issuing authority, contact path and evidence needed to begin replacement? Then reverse the scenario: assume the phone is lost. Can paper/offline information still point you toward recovery?

### Prioritize after disruption
Mark documents as “needed immediately,” “needed for recovery,” or “replace later.” This prevents a minor record from competing with identity, medical or housing documentation during the first stressful hours.

Review the map after moving home, renewing identification, changing insurance or replacing a device. Delete obsolete copies deliberately so contradictory versions do not accumulate.
