# Preparedness Maintenance Cycle

Prepared equipment is a condition that must be demonstrated, not a label that lasts forever.

### Convert objects into due dates
For each critical item, record what proves readiness: visual inspection, battery capacity check, manufacturer test, rotation date, software/offline-file refresh, physical access check or professional service. Then set the next date or usage trigger.

### Evidence column
Avoid “checked” as the only result. Write an observable outcome such as “power bank accepted charge and reached 96%,” “paper contact list matches phone,” or “alternate exit unobstructed.” If a check cannot be performed safely, record who should perform it.

### Batch intelligently
Group low-risk tasks by month or season so they are less likely to be forgotten, but keep safety-critical manufacturer intervals intact. Rotate food, water and consumables according to their actual labels/storage conditions rather than a universal calendar rule.

### Close overdue work
Once a month, open the Maintenance register and resolve the oldest overdue high-priority task first. If it is repeatedly deferred, decide whether the equipment should still count as part of the emergency plan.
