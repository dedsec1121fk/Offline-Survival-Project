# Thermal Shelter Operations

Heat and cold plans should be built around people, room conditions and safe equipment use rather than a single thermostat number.

### Choose the working zone
Identify the room that best supports the current hazard: shade and controllable ventilation during heat, or a smaller conservable space during cold. Keep exits usable and consider bathroom/accessibility needs before moving everyone into one area.

### Observe conditions
Record indoor temperature if you have a reliable thermometer, time of day, power status and how vulnerable household members are actually doing. Trend matters: a room that is steadily worsening may require earlier relocation even before it reaches a predefined threshold.

### Power hierarchy
Reserve limited electricity for medically necessary or safety-critical equipment first, then communications and lighting. Cooling/heating devices must be used only as designed; never replace safe heating with indoor use of outdoor combustion equipment.

### Relocation trigger
Pre-identify a safer heated/cooled destination and the transport needed to reach it. The plan should state who decides to move and what observable condition triggers the decision, particularly for older adults, infants, people with illness or anyone with impaired temperature awareness.
