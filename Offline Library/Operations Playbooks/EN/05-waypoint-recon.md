# Waypoint Recon Worksheet

A useful waypoint is more than a latitude/longitude pair. Reconnaissance should answer whether the point can actually be recognized and approached under degraded conditions.

### At the location
Record coordinates twice if possible: once from the chosen device and once by a second device/map reference. Add a plain-language landmark visible without a screen. Note which direction you approached from, whether the path is usable in darkness or poor weather, and any gate, stair, flood-prone dip, construction zone or access restriction.

### Verify the purpose
Write why this waypoint exists: meeting point, water source, alternate exit, charging site, medical service, transport pickup or another role. Then state the condition that would invalidate it. A meeting point beside a road closure or a water point under a contamination notice should not remain “valid” merely because its coordinates are correct.

### Before relying on it
Check the point at a different time of day if that changes access. Export or print the coordinates with the landmark description. Set a review date so temporary construction or service changes do not silently make the waypoint obsolete.
