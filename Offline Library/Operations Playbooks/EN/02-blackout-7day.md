# Seven-Day Outage Continuity

A week without normal utility service requires a routine, not seven repetitions of the first emergency day.

### Divide the week into cycles
Choose fixed times for water accounting, food decisions, charging, sanitation inspection, official-information checks and household handover. Fewer scheduled checks often preserve battery and attention better than constant monitoring.

### Track trends
Each day record four numbers: potable water remaining, approximate food days remaining, usable stored electrical energy, and fuel/transport reserve if applicable. Compare today's figures with yesterday's actual use. A falling reserve should change the plan before it reaches zero.

### Protect human performance
Assign rest periods and rotate stressful roles. Record one person who is backup for every essential task. Fatigue creates missed labels, forgotten messages and poor route choices long before supplies are exhausted.

### Day-three review
Ask which part of the home can no longer support normal routines: hygiene, temperature, refrigeration, medical continuity, communications or access. If the answer points toward relocation, begin that decision while transport, daylight and communication options still exist rather than waiting for total failure.
