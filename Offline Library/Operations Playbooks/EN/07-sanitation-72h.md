# 72-Hour Sanitation Board

Sanitation failures become health and habit problems quickly when water or plumbing is limited.

### Map the flow
Write where clean hand-washing water comes from, where used cleaning water goes if locally appropriate, how toilet needs will be managed under the actual building conditions, and where sealed waste waits for lawful disposal. Keep food-preparation items physically separate from waste-handling supplies.

### Consumables count
Physically count soap, gloves if used, toilet supplies, bags/liners, disinfectant products and cleaning water. Record what each product label says about safe use; do not mix cleaning chemicals or invent concentrations.

### Inspection twice a day
Morning check: leaks, odors, overflowing containers, hand-hygiene station and remaining consumables. Evening check: waste sealed, surfaces that need attention, clean/dirty tools still separated and enough capacity for the night.

### Escalation
If sewage backs up, water becomes contaminated, ventilation is unsafe or the arrangement cannot be maintained hygienically, treat that as a change in shelter viability rather than simply “working around it” indefinitely.
