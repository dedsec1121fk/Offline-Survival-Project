# Return & Re-entry Decision Board

The desire to go home is not evidence that home is ready for occupation.

### Gate 1 — travel permission and access
Record the source and timestamp of any official re-entry instruction. Confirm the route is open; a general “area reopened” notice may not describe every street, building or transport line.

### Gate 2 — stop outside
Before entering, look from a safe position for visible structural damage, fire/smoke, standing water, fallen lines, chemical odors, unstable trees/debris or signs that authorities have restricted the property. Any serious concern ends the self-check and moves the decision toward qualified assessment.

### Gate 3 — services
Do not assume electricity, gas, water, lifts, alarms or refrigeration are safe simply because they appear to work. Follow provider/authority instructions and professional inspection requirements appropriate to the incident.

### Gate 4 — living viability
Ask whether the home currently supports safe sleep, sanitation, medication storage, drinking water and necessary accessibility. If not, continued temporary accommodation may be the better recovery decision.

Write one explicit “leave again” trigger before entering so returning does not become psychologically irreversible.
