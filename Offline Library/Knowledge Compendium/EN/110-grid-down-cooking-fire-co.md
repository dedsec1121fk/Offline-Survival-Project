# Cooking Without Grid Power: Fire, Fuel and Carbon Monoxide Boundaries

## Choose equipment by safe location
Camping stoves, grills and generators can create carbon monoxide and fire hazards when brought indoors, into garages or near openings. Use combustion equipment only in the environment specified by its manufacturer and official fire-safety guidance.

## Do not improvise household backfeed
Using an outdoor portable burner is different from feeding a home's wiring through a receptacle. Generator connection to household wiring requires an appropriate transfer arrangement and competent installation/use. Keep generators outdoors and well away from windows, doors and vents.

## Stabilize the cooking area
Use a stable noncombustible surface away from dry vegetation, children, animals and flammable liquids. Keep a clear path around boiling water and fuel. Outdoor flames may be prohibited or unsafe during wildfire restrictions.

## Handle fuel deliberately
Use approved containers and the correct fuel. Do not refuel hot equipment, and keep spare fuel away from ignition and living spaces. Never place fuel in drink bottles.

## Reduce energy demand through meal design
Prioritize foods requiring little or no cooking during prolonged outages. Coordinate with cold-chain triage so perishable foods are handled safely. Batch cooking only helps if cooked food can subsequently be stored safely.

## Keep food hygiene separate from fuel handling
Use safe water, clean hands/surfaces and separate raw from ready-to-eat food. Never prepare food in the same dirty zone used for fuel or waste.

## Treat CO alarms as protection, not permission
A working carbon-monoxide alarm is an additional layer, not permission to run unsafe equipment indoors. If it alarms or exposure is suspected, move to fresh air and seek emergency guidance.

## Cool equipment before storage
Shut down according to instructions and allow equipment, ash and coals to cool in a safe location. Never bring burning charcoal indoors for heat.

## Keep a no-flame food option
Ready-to-eat shelf-stable meals prevent pressure to use unsafe combustion when weather, restrictions, injury or lack of outdoor space makes cooking unsafe.
