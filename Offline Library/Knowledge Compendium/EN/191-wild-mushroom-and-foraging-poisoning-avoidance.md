# Wild Mushrooms and Foraging: Poisoning-Avoidance Rules

Hunger and supply disruption can make wild food look like an easy reserve. The safest survival rule is that a plant or mushroom is not food merely because an app, photo, folklore test, animal behavior or one visual feature appears to identify it.

## Identification must be stronger than resemblance
Some toxic mushrooms and plants closely resemble edible species. Cooking, drying or freezing does not reliably make an unknown poisonous species safe. “Silver spoon,” color-change and insect/animal-eating myths are not reliable toxicity tests. If expert-level identification is not already a practiced skill, an emergency is a poor time to begin eating unknown species.

## Separate learning specimens from food
If you collect specimens for identification, keep them physically separate from known food. Use a paper container/label where appropriate, record location and photographs, and wash hands/tools when plant sap or unknown mushrooms are handled. Children and pets should not have access to the collection.

## If ingestion occurs
Do not wait for symptoms before seeking poison/emergency advice when a potentially toxic species was eaten. Preserve a sample/photo if safe because identification can help treatment. Do not induce vomiting unless specifically instructed by a medical/poison professional. Different toxins can produce delayed symptoms, so feeling well immediately is not proof of safety.

## Preparedness alternative
Build calories through shelf-stable staples, verified local crops and food-preservation skills before relying on wild foods. If foraging is part of your plan, learn a small number of locally distinctive species from qualified local experts during normal times and learn their dangerous look-alikes just as thoroughly.
