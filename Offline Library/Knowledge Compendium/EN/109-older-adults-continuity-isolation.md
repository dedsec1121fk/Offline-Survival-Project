# Older Adults: Continuity, Isolation and Evacuation Timing

## Plan around actual function
Older adults vary widely in mobility, cognition, hearing, medicine dependence, temperature tolerance and social support. Record the person's real dependencies rather than treating age as a diagnosis.

## Identify hidden infrastructure dependencies
High-rise residents can lose access when elevators fail. Upper floors may lose water if pumps stop. Powered beds, stair lifts, building entry systems and remote alarms can also fail. Add these dependencies to the risk board.

## Prevent isolation from becoming invisibility
Set regular check-in times with more than one contact when practical. A missed check-in should trigger a defined sequence rather than vague concern. Keep a printed contact card because a dead or locked phone hides its address book.

## Move medicines and sensory aids together
Pack medicines with glasses, hearing aids, batteries, dentures/care products, mobility aids and written instructions. A medicine bag without the ability to hear instructions or reach the toilet is incomplete.

## Use earlier relocation triggers
Waiting until heat, cold, smoke or stair access becomes intolerable makes movement harder. People with limited mobility or medical dependence may benefit from conservative, earlier relocation thresholds consistent with official guidance.

## Preserve autonomy
Use clear written or visual instructions, repeat critical facts calmly and confirm understanding. Support decision-making without automatically transferring control to younger relatives.

## Verify destination capability
Check accessible toilets, sleeping arrangements, power/refrigeration and any needed care. Bring the person's own mobility device when feasible. A roof and electricity do not automatically make a destination accessible.

## Monitor temperature and hydration risk
Older adults can be more vulnerable to temperature extremes and some medicines affect heat response. Do not change medicines without clinical guidance. Relocate if safe indoor temperature cannot be maintained.

## Reassess the home after return
New trip hazards, broken rails, elevator outages or disrupted services can make a previously manageable home unsafe. Inspect the actual route between bed, toilet, kitchen and exit.
