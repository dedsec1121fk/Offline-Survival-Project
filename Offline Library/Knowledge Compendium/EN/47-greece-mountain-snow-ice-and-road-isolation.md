# Greece: mountain snow, ice, and road isolation

## Isolation can begin before snow reaches the house
Mountain households can lose safe road access, deliveries and power while indoor conditions still look normal. Monitor official weather and road information and act before a steep or shaded route becomes icy. If travel is not necessary, avoiding a deteriorating mountain road may be the most effective preparedness action.

## Vehicle winter margin
Tyres, chains or traction equipment where required, battery condition, antifreeze/coolant, washer fluid, lights and fuel matter more in cold mountain travel. Practice fitting required traction equipment in good weather; unopened equipment in the boot is not a capability. Carry warm layers, water, food, light, phone power and a method to make the vehicle visible if stranded.

## If stranded
Stay with the vehicle when it provides the safest shelter and follow local emergency guidance. Keep exhaust outlets clear of snow before running an engine and avoid creating carbon-monoxide risk. Use fuel conservatively and make the vehicle visible without exhausting the battery. Share precise location and road direction with emergency services.

## House heating and outage safety
Cold-induced improvisation creates fire and carbon-monoxide hazards. Use heaters only as designed, maintain ventilation requirements and never use outdoor grills or generators indoors. Keep one warm room, dry clothing, bedding and a way to check indoor temperature and vulnerable people.

## Supply horizon
Mountain closure can delay food, medicine and fuel resupply after the weather improves. Maintain a reserve appropriate to local isolation history and household medical needs. Record days remaining for prescription medicines, animal feed, heating fuel and drinking water separately; the shortest critical reserve drives the resupply decision.
