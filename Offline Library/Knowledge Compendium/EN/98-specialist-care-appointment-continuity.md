# Specialist care and scheduled treatment continuity: plan the interruption before the appointment is missed

## Some care cannot simply be postponed
Dialysis, infusion, wound care, oxygen supply, device support, prenatal follow-up and other scheduled services can have different urgency. The person and treating team should define what happens if the normal site is closed, transport fails or the appointment is cancelled. Do not invent a universal delay limit.

## Keep the care pathway offline
Record clinic/service name, phone numbers, treating team, normal schedule, referral/prescription information and backup location if one has been assigned. Keep the minimum necessary medical summary with the person, not only in an online portal.

## Watch transport and utility threats early
A wildfire warning, ferry cancellation, snowstorm or long power outage may disrupt care before the appointment date. Contact the provider early when disruption is likely. For island/rural patients, travel may need to occur before the last normal transport window.

## Prepare equipment and consumables for transfer
Use the manufacturer/clinical plan for packing and power. Keep chargers, prescribed supplies and documentation together. Do not alter therapy settings to extend battery life unless the clinical plan explicitly instructs it.

## Know the emergency threshold
Ask the treating team which symptoms, device failures or missed treatments require emergency evaluation. Put that answer in the medical continuity card. The survival project stores the plan; it does not determine clinical urgency.

## Reconcile after service returns
Confirm the next appointment, replacement prescriptions/supplies and any missed-care follow-up. Update the contingency if the backup provider, travel time or equipment requirement proved different from the plan.
