# Tornado Shelter Location and Post-Impact Exit

Tornado survival depends heavily on reaching the best available shelter before the wind arrives. Decide the location in normal time; do not wait for a warning to debate which room is safest.

## Choose the best available shelter
A purpose-built safe room or storm shelter designed to recognized standards is preferred where available. Otherwise use a small interior room on the lowest level of a sturdy building, away from windows and exterior walls. In a high-rise, identify an interior room or protected corridor on the lowest level you can safely reach before conditions deteriorate. Mobile/manufactured homes require an external sturdy shelter plan rather than treating the home as adequate tornado protection.

## Protect from debris
Use sturdy shoes and keep helmets or other head protection accessible if your household plan includes them. Get beneath sturdy protection where appropriate and cover head/neck. Do not spend warning time opening windows. Keep a flashlight and whistle in the shelter because power and exits may fail after impact.

## After the wind
Do not rush outside into downed lines, broken glass, leaking gas or unstable walls. Check people first, use the safest known exit, and avoid entering visibly damaged sections. Send a short status/location message when communications work. Expect secondary hazards and additional storms.
