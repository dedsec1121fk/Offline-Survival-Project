# High Surf and Rocky Coasts: Sneaker Waves, Spray, and Escape Routes

A calm-looking shoreline can change within seconds. Large sets, reflected waves, slippery rock, rising tide and narrow escape routes make rocky coasts dangerous even without a named storm. The safe plan starts by staying out of the impact zone rather than relying on reaction speed.

## Read the coastline before approaching
Identify wet rock lines, seaweed/debris marks, channels where waves run inland, cliffs that remove escape options, and whether the tide is rising. Observe several wave sets from a dry protected position; one quiet minute is not evidence that the platform is safe.

## Keep separation from the edge
Do not stand on low exposed rocks to photograph surf or retrieve equipment. A “sneaker” wave can reach farther than preceding waves and knock a person into turbulent water. Children and animals need an even larger buffer because they may not recover balance or respond to a warning quickly.

## If someone is swept in
Call emergency rescue services and use shore-based rescue aids such as a lifebuoy, throw bag, or floating object if available and if you can deploy them without entering the water. Untrained entry into high surf or rocky water can create a second victim. Keep visual contact and point continuously for responders.

## Plan the exit before fishing or exploring
Know at least one route that remains usable as tide and waves rise. Do not allow the group to be cut off behind a headland or on a rock shelf. Wear suitable footwear and flotation where activity/local guidance requires it.

## After a wave event
Cold-water exposure, impact injury and aspiration may need medical assessment even when the person walks out. Track breathing, consciousness and injuries rather than assuming rescue ends at the shoreline.
