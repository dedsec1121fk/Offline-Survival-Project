# Pharmacy and Refill Continuity When Away From Home

A medicine reserve is useful only if the household can replace it when evacuation lasts longer than planned. Pharmacy continuity therefore includes identification, prescription information, insurance/payment information where applicable, prescriber contacts, storage needs, and a method for explaining exactly what was lost or left behind.

## Prepare a refill packet before disruption
Keep a current medication list plus pharmacy name/phone, prescriber contacts, relevant insurance identifiers, allergy information, and a copy or photo of prescription labels where lawful and appropriate. Do not store the only copy online. If controlled or specially regulated medicines are involved, expect stricter refill rules and seek professional guidance early rather than assuming an emergency override exists.

## Ask for the smallest correct decision
When contacting a pharmacy, describe the situation precisely: evacuation, medicine inaccessible, supply damaged, cold-chain failure, prescription transferred, or normal pharmacy closed. Provide the label information instead of asking staff to identify a medicine from appearance. Record who was contacted, time, what they advised, and the location where a replacement can actually be collected.

## Prevent duplicate supply from becoming duplicate dosing
If multiple pharmacies or clinics issue replacement stock, reconcile it into the medication list and mark the superseded container. Keep receipts and paperwork because they may also be useful for reimbursement or continuity at the next location. Never combine loose pills into an unlabeled emergency container unless a qualified professional has specifically instructed an appropriate method.

## Verification anchor
- Local pharmacy, prescriber, insurer/health system, and applicable national/local medicine regulations.
