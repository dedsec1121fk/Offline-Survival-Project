# Electrical Shock: Scene Safety Before First Aid

Electricity can injure the rescuer before the original casualty is reached. The first action is therefore not touching the person: it is controlling the electrical source and making sure the scene cannot energize you, wet ground, metal objects, or other people.

## Do not become part of the circuit
If the person is still in contact with a live source, do not touch them with bare hands. Shut off power at a safe disconnect, breaker, plug, or utility-controlled point when you can do so without entering danger. High-voltage lines, substations, damaged service drops, energized floodwater, and unknown industrial equipment are professional-responder/utility hazards; keep well away and call emergency services/utility operators.

## Treat water as a major uncertainty
Floodwater or wet structures can conceal energized conductors. Do not wade into water to rescue someone if electricity may be present. Have the supply isolated by the competent authority first. A fallen overhead line should be treated as energized even if it is quiet, not sparking, or appears insulated.

## After power is confirmed isolated
Check responsiveness and breathing. If the person is unresponsive and not breathing normally, begin CPR and use an AED when available, following training/device prompts. Electrical injury can produce burns at contact points, internal injury, falls, heart-rhythm problems, muscle damage, or delayed symptoms even when the skin injury looks small.

## Escalation and monitoring
Emergency medical assessment is appropriate after significant electrical shock, loss of consciousness, chest pain, abnormal heartbeat sensation, breathing problems, neurologic symptoms, major burns, high-voltage exposure, or a fall associated with the shock. Keep the person at rest and monitor for deterioration while help is arranged.

## Preserve the incident details
Record voltage/source if reliably known, whether the supply was AC/DC, wet/dry conditions, duration/contact circumstances, loss of consciousness, burns, fall height, CPR/AED use, and who isolated the power. Do not re-energize damaged circuits until appropriately inspected.
