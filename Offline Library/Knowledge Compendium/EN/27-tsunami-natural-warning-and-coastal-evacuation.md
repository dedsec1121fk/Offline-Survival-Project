# Tsunami: natural warnings, vertical urgency, and coastal evacuation

## Do not wait for a phone alert when nature is already warning you
In a tsunami-prone coastal area, strong or long earthquake shaking, a sudden unusual rise or fall of the sea, or a loud ocean roar can be natural warnings. If a locally recognized natural warning occurs, move away from the coast and toward the designated safer area immediately when you can do so safely. An official message may arrive later or communications may be degraded.

## Elevation and distance matter more than possessions
The household plan should identify the locally designated tsunami evacuation zone and route before an earthquake. After a natural warning, do not spend time packing normal belongings or going to the shoreline to observe. Use the route instructed by local authorities; where walking is recommended, it can avoid traffic gridlock. Keep mobility devices, essential medicines, footwear and a small water supply positioned so a vulnerable person does not require a long preparation phase.

## One wave is not the end
A tsunami is a series of waves, and dangerous currents can persist for hours. Do not return after the first wave or because the sea appears calm. Remain outside the hazard zone until authorities say it is safe. Harbours, river mouths and low-lying channels can remain dangerous even when nearby land looks undamaged.

## If you are already offshore
Marine guidance depends on vessel location, harbour conditions and official maritime instructions; do not apply a household land-evacuation rule to a boat without appropriate maritime authority information. The safest action for someone on shore is generally to move inland/uphill as directed, not to launch a vessel.

## Greece-specific preparation
For Greek coastal and island locations, identify the route from each frequent location—home, work, school, beach or port—to higher or safer ground and note which route still works if a narrow waterfront road is blocked. Keep 112 alerts enabled and follow local Civil Protection, Coast Guard and municipal instructions.
