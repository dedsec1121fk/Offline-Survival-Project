# Rainwater Capture: First-Flush, Storage, and Potable Separation

Rainwater can extend nonpotable supplies, but a roof and tank do not automatically create drinking water. Dust, bird/animal waste, roofing materials, smoke/ash and maintenance chemicals can enter the collection stream.

## Define the intended use first
Label the system clearly as potable or nonpotable according to how it is designed, treated and regulated locally. A nonpotable rainwater system can support cleaning, toilet flushing or other approved uses while protecting known-safe drinking water. Do not allow cross-connections where someone can accidentally drink from an untreated line.

## Keep the collection path inspectable
Clean gutters and screens, exclude animals/insects where practical, and divert the dirtiest initial roof runoff with a suitable first-flush design when applicable. After wildfire ash, chemical deposition or roofing work, suspend collection until the catchment has been assessed rather than assuming normal treatment is sufficient.

## Protect stored water
Use covered, cleanable containers that prevent light/debris entry and mosquito access. Keep an overflow path that does not undermine foundations. Mark the collection date/batch if you rotate stored water. Do not use a container that previously held unknown or hazardous chemicals for water storage.

## Drinking-water boundary
If rainwater is intended for drinking, treatment and testing must match local public-health guidance and the specific contamination risks. Clear appearance is not proof of microbiological or chemical safety.
