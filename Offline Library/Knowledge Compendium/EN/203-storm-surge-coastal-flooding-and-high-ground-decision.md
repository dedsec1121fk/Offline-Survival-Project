# Storm Surge and Coastal Flooding: High-Ground Decision

Storm surge is not simply “large waves.” Wind and pressure can push seawater inland, raising water level over roads, buildings and evacuation routes. The safest decision often occurs before water is visible.

## Know the elevation problem
Use official evacuation/surge maps before the season and identify a destination outside the mapped zone. A nearby building that is slightly taller is not automatically a safe substitute for official evacuation. Bridges, causeways and coastal roads may become unusable well before the worst conditions.

## Do not use the shoreline as an observation point
Large waves, debris and rapidly rising water can overtake people who approach to watch. Keep away from beaches, seawalls, harbors, river mouths and flooded roads during warnings. Never drive through water covering a roadway; depth, current and pavement damage cannot be judged reliably from the vehicle.

## Protect the return decision
After water retreats, salt water, sewage, fuel, electrical hazards and structural damage can remain. Do not treat receding water as an all-clear. Wait for access guidance, avoid downed lines and use protective footwear/gloves appropriate to cleanup tasks.
