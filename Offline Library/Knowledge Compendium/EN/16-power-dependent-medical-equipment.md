# Power-dependent medical equipment continuity

## Treat runtime as a medical dependency, not a gadget specification
For equipment that supports breathing, mobility, communication, feeding, suction, refrigeration of medicine, or other health needs, battery runtime is part of the person’s care plan. Record the device model, normal power draw, battery count, realistic tested runtime, charging time, supplier, and whether the device can safely operate from an inverter or alternative power source. Manufacturer guidance takes precedence over generic electrical calculations.

## Test the real backup before an emergency
A battery labelled for many watt-hours may deliver less under the actual load, temperature, age, or inverter losses. Run a supervised test under safe conditions and record the result. Check whether alarms remain audible, whether the device restarts automatically after power loss, and whether a caregiver knows how to switch to backup without changing clinical settings.

## Build a relocation trigger
Do not plan to remain at home until the final battery is exhausted. Define a conservative trigger such as loss of mains power plus a specific remaining runtime, failure of the charging source, extreme indoor temperature, or loss of caregiver support. Identify powered medical shelters, hospitals, clinics, or other appropriate destinations according to local emergency planning.

## Generator use adds carbon-monoxide and electrical risk
If a generator is part of the approved backup plan, it must be used outside and away from openings according to current safety guidance. CDC specifies more than 20 feet from windows, doors, and vents, with a battery-powered or battery-backup carbon-monoxide detector inside. Never connect a generator to household wiring through an improvised “backfeed” cable.

## Keep one paper handover sheet
If the person must be moved, a short sheet with device name, power needs, battery status, accessories, medications, communication needs, and supplier contacts reduces delays. It should travel with the person, not remain in the evacuation binder at home.
