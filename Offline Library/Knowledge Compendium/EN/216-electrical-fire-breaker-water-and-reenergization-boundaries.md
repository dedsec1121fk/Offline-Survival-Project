# Electrical Fire: Breaker, Water, and Re-Energization Boundaries

Electrical faults can ignite wiring, appliances, batteries or extension leads. Electricity also changes how a fire can be approached: water and conductive tools may create shock hazards.

## Escape if the fire is not immediately controllable
Sound the alarm, get people out and call emergency services. Only attempt extinguisher use if the fire is small, you have the correct extinguisher/training, smoke is limited and a clear escape remains behind you. Never let “saving the inverter” block the exit.

## Disconnect power only from a safe position
If a known breaker/disconnect can be operated without entering smoke, water or the fire area, isolation may reduce hazard. Do not touch a wet electrical panel, exposed conductors or equipment in standing water. Do not pull a burning plug by grabbing damaged cable.

## Do not re-energize by trial
After fire, flooding, arcing or heat damage, a breaker staying on does not prove the circuit is safe. Burned insulation and hidden connections can fail later. Keep affected circuits/equipment isolated until inspected by a qualified electrician or authority.

## Record what happened
Note circuit, device, smell/sound, breaker behavior, water exposure and photographs from a safe position. Do not dismantle critical evidence before insurance/fire/electrical review when that review is required.
