# Wildlife and Food Storage: Camp Separation and Attractant Control

Food, rubbish, toiletries and animal feed can attract wildlife long before a person sees the animal. Good camp hygiene is therefore a prevention system: control odors and attractants, follow the location-specific storage rule, and avoid conditioning animals to associate people with food.

## Learn the local storage requirement
Some areas require bear-resistant containers, food lockers, vehicle storage, or a specific hanging method; others prohibit certain techniques. Store the local rule offline before travel. A method that works for small rodents may be inadequate for bears, and a vehicle may not be acceptable in every region.

## Treat more than food as an attractant
Cooking ingredients, garbage, toothpaste, scented cosmetics, pet food, wrappers, cooking grease and sometimes medication containers can carry odor. Keep the sleeping area separate from cooking/food storage when local wildlife guidance calls for it. Never store snacks in a sleeping bag or tent because it is convenient overnight.

## Keep a clean cooking zone
Prepare only the amount needed, collect crumbs and scraps, strain food particles from wash water where required, and pack out waste according to local rules. Do not burn food packaging or bury scraps as a disposal shortcut.

## If wildlife approaches
Give the animal space, protect escape routes and follow species-specific local instructions. Do not feed it, approach for photographs, or place yourself between an adult and young. An animal that obtains food may become more persistent and dangerous to later visitors.

## Household/pet integration
Keep pets controlled and their feed secured. Record wildlife restrictions in the route card so every group member follows the same system rather than moving attractants between tents and vehicles.
