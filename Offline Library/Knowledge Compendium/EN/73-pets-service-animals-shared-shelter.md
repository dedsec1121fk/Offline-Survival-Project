# Pets and service animals in shared sheltering: separate care needs without separating the plan

## Know the facility policy before arrival where possible
Emergency shelters may handle pets and service animals differently. Service animals have a different role from pets, and applicable local rules govern access. Identify pet-friendly options, veterinary contacts and a backup caregiver before the incident instead of assuming every destination can house every animal.

## Keep identification with the animal
Use current tags/microchip information and carry a recent photo plus ownership/veterinary records. A carrier, leash/harness and familiar handling equipment should fit the actual animal. Label medicines and special diets clearly.

## Create an animal zone that does not compromise human operations
Where a shelter provides a pet area, keep it away from food preparation and sanitation routes. Control waste promptly, prevent loose animals from obstructing exits and avoid contact between unfamiliar animals. Never tether an animal where it can become trapped by moving equipment or crowds.

## Preserve service-animal function
Do not pet, feed or separate a service animal from its handler without permission except where immediate safety requires action. Ask the handler what assistance is actually needed. Backup supplies should include the animal’s food, water, waste control and any equipment needed for its work.

## Plan for stress and escape risk
Noise, smoke, strange people and other animals can cause normally calm pets to bolt or become defensive. Use secure containment and avoid opening carriers in uncontrolled areas. Keep a secondary contact who can receive the animal if the main shelter option fails.

## Recovery includes the animal’s environment
After flood, fire or chemical incidents, do not allow animals to roam through contaminated yards, ash, standing water or debris before the area is safe. Veterinary advice is appropriate for exposure, injury or loss of normal feeding/medication continuity.
