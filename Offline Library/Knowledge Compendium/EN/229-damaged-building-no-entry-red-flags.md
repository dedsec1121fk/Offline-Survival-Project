# Damaged Building Re-Entry: No-Entry Red Flags

Returning home after a disaster can expose people to collapse, electricity, gas, fire, sewage, mold and contaminated water. A familiar building is not automatically a safe building. Use a deliberate outside-to-inside decision and stop when evidence exceeds household capability.

## Inspect from outside before approaching
Look for leaning walls, shifted foundations, sagging rooflines, cracked or displaced structural elements, detached balconies/chimneys, downed wires, leaking fuel, strong gas odor, active smoke/fire, flowing water from broken pipes, or ground movement. If authorities have tagged or restricted the building, obey the restriction.

## Conditions that should stop entry
Do not enter when there is visible structural instability, active fire/smoke, suspected gas accumulation, electrical arcing or energized floodwater, significant chemical release, deep/fast water, or an official prohibition. Leave immediately if you hear unusual shifting, cracking, hissing, see increasing deformation, or develop dizziness/headache in a potentially contaminated atmosphere.

## If limited entry is considered safe
Use daylight when possible, sturdy footwear, gloves and a clear exit path. Work with another accountable person rather than alone. Do not switch electrical equipment on/off if gas is suspected. Avoid touching wet electrical systems. Keep children and animals outside the work zone until hazards are controlled.

## Utilities need their own decision
A shutoff is not automatically permission to restore. Damaged gas, electrical, solar/battery, water or sewage systems may require utility or qualified technical inspection. Photograph positions and labels before altering them when documentation is useful and safe.

## Separate recovery from rescue
Retrieving documents or food is never worth entering a structure that may collapse or contain toxic atmosphere. Establish a no-entry boundary and wait for qualified assessment. Record what is inaccessible so recovery planning does not repeatedly pressure people to “just go in quickly.”
