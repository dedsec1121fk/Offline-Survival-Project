# Medication continuity during evacuation and outages

## Build the plan before the pharmacy is unreachable
Medication continuity requires more than a list of drug names. Record the exact product, strength as printed on the label, normal schedule, prescriber, pharmacy, refill date, storage requirement, and the reason the medicine is important. Keep the original labelled containers when practical. This lets a clinician or pharmacist understand what the person normally uses without relying on memory during an emergency.

## Refrigerated or temperature-sensitive medicine needs a separate power plan
Do not assume a household refrigerator is an adequate backup during a long outage. Identify which medicines are temperature-sensitive and obtain manufacturer/pharmacist guidance in advance on acceptable storage, transport, and what to do after temperature excursions. Use a dedicated cooler or monitoring method only as directed; freezing some medicines can be as harmful as warming them.

## Create redundancy in access, not in self-prescribing
Know an alternate pharmacy, how to reach the prescriber, and what local emergency prescription programs exist. A backup copy of the prescription record can help, but the project should never tell a person to double a dose, stretch a controlled medicine, or substitute another drug without qualified advice. If access is interrupted, contact a pharmacist, clinician, emergency service, or official assistance channel as appropriate.

## Power-dependent care needs an evacuation threshold
Oxygen concentrators, suction devices, dialysis-related equipment, powered mobility systems, refrigerated medicine, and other health technologies can turn a power outage into a medical emergency. Record battery runtime, charging options, supplier emergency contact, and the condition that triggers relocation to a powered facility. Do not wait until the last battery reaches zero before acting.

## Protect privacy while keeping the information usable
A medication card can be life-saving, but it contains sensitive health information. Keep one copy accessible to the person or caregiver and protect full digital backups. The redacted project template intentionally excludes personal state; a full export should be handled as private medical/emergency data.
