# Urban Debris: Glass, Nails, and a Safer Foot Route

After earthquake, wind, explosion or fire, a familiar street can become an injury field of glass, nails, sharp sheet metal, unstable facades and hidden holes. Route choice and footwear can prevent an otherwise minor evacuation from becoming a wound-care problem.

## Dress for the route
Use sturdy closed footwear, long trousers and gloves appropriate to the debris task. Eye protection is useful where dust or fragments are being moved. Do not use thin sandals for building re-entry or debris routes simply because the weather is hot.

## Look upward as well as downward
Damaged signs, glazing, balconies, tiles and masonry can fall after the main event. Avoid walking directly beside visibly damaged facades and keep distance from leaning walls or partially detached material. Aftershocks/wind can release items that initially stayed in place.

## Mark hazards for the next person
If a route contains an open hole, sharp protrusion or unstable area, mark/record it without creating another trip hazard. Share the alternate route in the household handover. Keep children and animals out of cleanup zones.

## Wound prevention is easier than wound treatment
Do not reach blindly into debris piles. Use tools rather than hands where possible and never lift heavy unstable material alone. Any puncture/cut from contaminated debris should be cleaned and assessed according to wound/tetanus guidance rather than ignored because evacuation is busy.
