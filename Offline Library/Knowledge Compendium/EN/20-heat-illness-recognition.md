# Heat illness recognition and operational escalation

## Heat exhaustion and heat stroke are not the same problem
Heavy sweating, weakness, dizziness, nausea, cramps, headache, and cool or clammy skin can occur with heat exhaustion. Heat stroke is more dangerous and may involve altered mental status, collapse, seizures, or other signs that the body is failing to control temperature. When a person becomes confused or unresponsive in the heat, treat the situation as a medical emergency rather than waiting to see whether rest alone helps.

## Move away from heat and begin cooling
Stop exertion, move to shade or an air-conditioned/cooler area, loosen unnecessary clothing, and use active cooling appropriate to your training and available resources. For suspected heat stroke, activate emergency medical services immediately and cool the person while help is coming. Do not delay the emergency call while searching the house for a thermometer.

## Hydration is not a universal treatment for every sick person
A conscious person with heat exhaustion may be able to take fluids according to first-aid guidance, but someone who is confused, vomiting repeatedly, having a seizure, or not fully awake should not be forced to drink. Medical conditions and medications can also change fluid requirements. The household water planner is therefore for logistics, not for prescribing treatment volumes.

## Prevention requires work design
Move heavy tasks out of the hottest part of the day where possible, increase rest, use shade, allow acclimatisation, and pair people so deterioration is noticed. Check older adults, children, people with chronic illness, pregnant people, and anyone without reliable cooling more frequently. A power outage during a heatwave can justify early relocation before symptoms appear.

## Record environmental and personal warning signs
The Field Log can store indoor temperature, power availability, cooling location, water consumption trend, and observed symptoms. These records help the next shift see whether conditions are worsening instead of judging each moment in isolation.
