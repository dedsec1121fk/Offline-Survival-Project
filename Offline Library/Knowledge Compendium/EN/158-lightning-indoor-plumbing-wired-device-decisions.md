# Lightning Indoors: Plumbing and Wired-Device Decisions

Being inside a substantial building is safer than remaining outdoors during lightning, but indoor behavior still matters because lightning can travel through plumbing and electrical/wired systems. CDC advises avoiding contact with water and equipment connected to electrical outlets during a thunderstorm.

## Pause water tasks
Delay showers, baths, dishwashing, and other contact with plumbing while thunder/lightning is active. This can affect hygiene schedules in a shelter, so move handwashing or meal tasks earlier/later when possible rather than crowding everyone at sinks during the storm.

## Reduce wired contact
Avoid using devices connected to wall power or wired external systems during the storm. Battery-powered equipment that is not connected to an outlet reduces that pathway. Do not stand at windows or exterior doors to watch lightning, and keep away from concrete walls/floors where metal reinforcement may conduct current.

## Re-check after the storm
A lightning event may trip protection or damage electronics without obvious exterior damage. If electrical equipment smells burned, behaves abnormally, or protective devices repeatedly trip, isolate the equipment and use qualified inspection rather than repeated resets.
