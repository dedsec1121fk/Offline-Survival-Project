# Oxygen, CPAP and other power-dependent respiratory equipment: plan continuity with the clinical team

## Treat power loss as a medical-continuity event
People using prescribed oxygen, CPAP/BiPAP or other respiratory equipment should have an outage plan from their healthcare/equipment provider. Device models, therapies and individual risks differ. Do not change flow, pressure, settings or treatment schedules to conserve electricity unless the clinical plan says to do so.

## Record equipment precisely
Keep device model, power requirements, battery type/runtime information supplied by the manufacturer, supplier contact, prescription/clinical summary and backup method approved for the person. Label proprietary adapters so they are not lost among general chargers.

## Test the approved backup
A battery that powers a phone may not support a respiratory device safely. Use only compatible equipment within manufacturer ratings. Periodically verify the backup without intentionally interrupting necessary therapy; coordinate testing with the provider when appropriate.

## Define relocation before the battery is low
Know which outage duration, battery threshold or symptom should trigger movement to a powered location or emergency evaluation. Arrange accessible transport. Waiting until the last battery percent can turn an orderly relocation into an emergency.

## Keep combustion sources away
Do not operate generators indoors or near openings, and follow oxygen-specific fire-safety instructions from the supplier/clinical team. Oxygen can increase fire risk around ignition sources. No smoking/open flame rules around prescribed oxygen must be taken seriously.

## Handover with the person
If care transfers to a shelter/caregiver, provide device settings as documented by the clinical plan, current power source, remaining approved reserve and supplier/healthcare contact. The receiving person should not have to guess how the equipment normally operates.
