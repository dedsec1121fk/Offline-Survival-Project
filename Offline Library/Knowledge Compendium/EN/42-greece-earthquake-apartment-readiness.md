# Greece: earthquake readiness for apartments and dense urban blocks

## Prevent falling-object injuries before the earthquake
Greek Civil Protection guidance emphasizes securing shelves/bookcases, water heaters and tanks, placing heavy objects low and removing heavy objects from above beds and sofas. In an apartment, also inspect tall cabinets near the exit, hanging lights, televisions, balcony items and anything that could block the door after shaking.

## Know the building without pretending to engineer it
Identify stairs, exits, utility shutoffs, fire extinguishers, assembly point and the building manager/contact. Do not make your own structural certification based on crack appearance. After a damaging earthquake, visible deformation, fallen masonry or compromised stairs require professional/authority assessment.

## The lift is not your evacuation plan
Assume electrical service may fail. Anyone who cannot use stairs independently needs a building-specific assisted-evacuation plan that does not simply say “use the elevator.” Record who can assist, where mobility equipment is kept and which safe waiting/communication procedure applies if the normal exit is blocked.

## Dense streets create exterior hazards
Immediately outside an apartment building may be a poor assembly area because façades, glass, signs, balconies and masonry can fall during aftershocks. Use the open assembly location identified by local/building planning, and keep clear of traffic routes needed by emergency vehicles.

## Coastal connection
If the apartment is in a tsunami-exposed coastal area, strong or long shaking may require a second action after immediate earthquake protection: prompt movement away from the coast according to local tsunami guidance. The household should know this before the earthquake, not discover it while waiting for an alert.
