# Post-earthquake home triage: structure, utilities, and habitability

## Use a stop-before-entry rule
After a damaging earthquake, do not let the desire to collect belongings override obvious warning signs. Partial wall separation, leaning masonry, major new cracks, displaced columns, sagging floors or roofs, fallen parapets, strong fuel odour, arcing electricity, fire, or continuing debris fall are reasons to stay out and seek competent assessment.

## Separate “usable” from “comfortable”
A home can have power and still be unsafe; it can also be uncomfortable yet structurally usable. Make a simple habitability decision using independent domains: structure, fire/fuel, electricity, water/sewage, access/egress, indoor air, weather exposure and aftershock vulnerability. A red condition in one critical domain can make the whole building unsuitable even when the other systems work.

## Water and sanitation
Broken pipes, loss of pressure and damaged sewer lines can create contamination risk. Do not assume tap water is safe after a major network disruption; follow the water utility and public-health instruction. If toilets or drains back up, stop adding wastewater to the affected system and switch to the household sanitation contingency rather than repeatedly testing it.

## Electrical and fuel systems
Use smell, sound and visible damage as warning signs, but remember that some hazards are not detectable by smell. Never search for a suspected gas leak with a flame. Do not energize wet or obviously damaged electrical equipment. Restoration decisions involving damaged building wiring, fuel systems or generators belong to qualified personnel or the responsible utility.

## Record the status room by room
Create a quick sketch or use Shelter Zoning in the Command Center. Mark rooms green only when checked, amber for restricted/uncertain areas and red for areas nobody should enter. Record photographs, time, aftershock changes and who made the decision. This prevents a later household member from assuming an unopened door leads to a safe room.
