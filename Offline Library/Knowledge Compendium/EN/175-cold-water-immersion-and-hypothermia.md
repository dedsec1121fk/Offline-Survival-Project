# Cold-Water Immersion: Escape, Rescue, and Hypothermia Boundaries

Cold water can rapidly impair breathing, hand function, swimming ability, and judgment even when the air feels mild. Floods, boating incidents, winter crossings, coastal storms, and accidental falls therefore need a different plan from ordinary cold-weather exposure.

## Prevent entry when possible
Do not walk or drive into floodwater to save distance. Wear a properly fitted personal flotation device for boating and water work. Keep throw lines/rings accessible on boats and at high-risk work areas. Clothing that is warm on land is not a substitute for flotation.

## Rescue without creating another victim
Use reach/throw/boat-assisted rescue methods when possible rather than entering hazardous water. Fast current, cold shock, submerged obstacles, and low visibility can overwhelm a strong swimmer. Activate professional rescue early and give precise location/last-seen information.

## After removal from cold water
Handle the person gently, protect them from wind and further heat loss, remove wet clothing when this can be done safely, dry/insulate, and seek medical evaluation according to symptoms and duration. Altered consciousness, abnormal breathing, severe shivering followed by reduced shivering, inability to walk normally, or collapse require urgent care.

## Do not assume apparent lifelessness means futility
Severe hypothermia complicates assessment. Follow emergency-dispatch/first-aid guidance for breathing and CPR rather than making an independent decision to stop care. Avoid rough handling and avoid direct high heat that can burn the skin or cause unsafe rewarming.

## Coastal/boat readiness
Store a dry bag with insulating layers, light, whistle, communication device, and location information. Practice how to call 112 and report coordinates from the phone/navigation system before an incident.
