# Community mutual aid: intake, distribution and safeguarding

Neighbour-to-neighbour help can fill urgent gaps before normal services recover, but an unstructured donation pile can create congestion, unfair access, spoiled food and privacy problems. A small distribution point should operate with clear boundaries and a written handover.

## Define what the point does

Choose a narrow mission such as sealed water distribution, charging queue, information board or collection of requested supplies. Do not present an informal household station as an official shelter, clinic or government service. Display opening times, stock limits and who is responsible for decisions.

## Receive supplies deliberately

Record item, quantity, donor/source when relevant, expiry or condition, storage requirement and acceptance decision. Reject leaking, opened, temperature-abused, recalled or otherwise unsafe food/medical items. Separate unknown goods until someone competent can assess them. Do not collect prescription medicines for casual redistribution.

## Distribute with dignity

Use a simple queue or token system that allows priority accommodations for people who cannot stand, caregivers, disability needs or urgent water/medical-support requirements. Do not require unnecessary identity details. Keep children and vulnerable adults out of isolated collection areas and maintain a visible, well-lit working space where possible.

## Protect information

Publish quantities and operating status, not recipients’ private stories. A public needs board can say “two infant-formula requests pending” without naming a family. Report safeguarding, violence or exploitation concerns through appropriate local channels rather than trying to investigate them alone.

## Close cleanly

At the end of a shift count remaining stock, isolate waste, record unresolved requests and hand over keys, cash or equipment with two-person verification when practical.

**Source anchors:** WHO community-engagement/risk-communication principles, local civil-protection/municipal arrangements and applicable food/medical regulations.
