# Hand hygiene and food-zone control during disruption

## Build a flow, not just a cleaning rule
During an outage or evacuation, the same small table may be used for food, medical items, charging, and dirty equipment. That is a cross-contamination problem. Designate a clean food surface and keep raw foods, waste, pet bowls, used first-aid material, and toilet supplies away from it. If space is extremely limited, separate uses by time and disinfect the surface between them rather than allowing simultaneous tasks.

## Handwashing points need four elements
A practical handwashing station needs safe water, soap, a controlled way for water to flow over the hands, and a way to keep wastewater from pooling in the clean zone. A shared basin of standing water can transfer contamination between users. Place the station where people naturally pass after the toilet and before food preparation, not in a distant location that will be skipped when everyone is tired.

## Sanitizer has limits
Alcohol-based sanitizer is useful in many situations when soap and water are not immediately available, but it is not universal. Public-health guidance still prioritises washing when hands are visibly dirty, after certain contamination events, and before tasks where residue matters. Keep sanitizer as a complement to a functioning handwashing plan, not as the only hygiene resource.

## Protect high-risk foods and people
Infant feeding equipment, allergy-safe preparation, medically necessary diets, and food for immunocompromised people deserve a clearly protected preparation area. Use separate utensils or carefully cleaned equipment when cross-contact could cause harm. If refrigeration or safe water is uncertain, simplify meals rather than increasing the number of preparation steps.

## Audit the flow once per shift
Watch one actual meal-preparation cycle. Where do hands touch before the clean surface? Where are dirty utensils placed? Does the water container tap touch cups? Does someone handle rubbish and then food? A short observation can reveal more than a long written rule and can be added to the field log as a corrective action.
