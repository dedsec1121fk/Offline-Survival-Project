# Earthquake: immediate protection and the first aftershock cycle

## During shaking
The objective during strong shaking is to reduce injury from falling, flying and collapsing objects. Indoors, use the established drop/cover/hold-on approach where appropriate: get low, protect head and neck, and take cover under sturdy furniture if it is immediately available. Do not run for stairs or an exterior exit while objects are falling. Stay away from windows, tall unsecured furniture and hanging objects. If already outside, move away from buildings, walls, glass, poles and overhead hazards into an open area when possible.

## When shaking stops
Expect aftershocks. Put on footwear before moving through broken glass and debris. Check people for injuries, then look and listen for immediate hazards such as fire, damaged wiring, leaking fuel, unstable walls, falling façade material and blocked exits. Use lifts/elevators only if authorities and building procedures specifically permit them; earthquake damage can interrupt power or compromise equipment.

## Utility decisions must be evidence-based
Do not switch utilities off automatically unless there is a reason or official instruction. If you smell gas or suspect a leak, avoid flames and electrical switching and leave the hazard area according to local utility/emergency guidance. If electrical wiring is visibly damaged, isolate it only if you can do so safely and know the correct disconnect. Photograph or record what was shut down and why so the next person does not restore it blindly.

## Aftershock posture
Identify a safer waiting area away from damaged façades, chimneys, glass and unsecured heavy items. Keep shoes, light, keys, phone, radio and essential medicines with the household. Recheck rooms after significant aftershocks because damage can progress. A building that was usable after the first shock may not remain so.

## Accountability and messages
Send one concise message to the designated contact rather than placing many nonessential calls. Record who is present, injured, missing or away from home. Follow official emergency information for evacuation, tsunami risk in coastal areas, road closures and shelter locations.
