# Drought and Long-Duration Water Management

Drought is a slow emergency. The main risk is not a single empty bottle; it is allowing household demand, storage loss, contamination, heat exposure, hygiene, animals, and uncertain resupply to drift without measurement. A good offline plan uses a water budget and changes behavior before reserves become critical.

## Separate drinking water from total water demand
Record potable water separately from water intended for cleaning, toilet flushing, plants, animals, or other non-drinking uses. Do not downgrade questionable water into “probably drinkable” merely because the potable reserve is falling. Protect verified drinking water from cross-contamination with clearly labeled containers and dedicated handling equipment.

## Measure consumption instead of guessing
Track beginning stock, additions, daily use, leaks/spills and ending stock. Calculate days of coverage using the current use rate, then compare it with the expected time to reliable resupply. Heat, illness, pregnancy, breastfeeding, physical work and some medical needs can raise demand; rationing must not create dehydration or prevent essential hygiene.

## Reduce avoidable losses early
Repair leaks, stop unnecessary washing with potable water, use basin methods where appropriate, group cleaning tasks, and capture safe non-potable water for suitable uses when local guidance permits. Keep lids closed, dispense through clean taps or utensils, and avoid dipping dirty cups/hands into storage.

## Build trigger points
Define a reserve threshold that forces a change: resupply trip, relocation, stricter non-potable allocation, or request for community assistance. Do not wait until the last container. The trigger should consider transport reliability and whether alternative sources require treatment, testing, fuel, filters, or electricity.

## Protect sanitation
Extreme conservation can create a second health problem if handwashing, food hygiene or toilet management collapses. Prioritize critical hand hygiene and safe food preparation even when recreational cleaning is reduced.
