# Wet Electrical Appliances: Do Not “Dry and Try”

An appliance that was submerged, splashed by floodwater, or exposed to water inside its electrical components can remain a shock or fire hazard after the outside dries. The correct recovery workflow is isolation, documentation, qualified inspection, and replacement when required—not repeated plug-in tests.

## Isolate before handling electrical recovery
If circuits or equipment are in or near water, CDC advises shutting off power only if the switch can be reached without entering water; otherwise use a qualified electrician. Do not stand in water while switching power or operating electrical tools. Mark flooded circuits and appliances so another household member does not reconnect them during cleanup.

## Separate salvage decisions from convenience
A refrigerator, washer, power tool, extension lead, charger, inverter, or medical device may contain water or contamination where it cannot be seen. Do not use warmth, sun, or a few dry days as evidence that internal insulation and safety components are sound. For safety-critical or high-power equipment, qualified inspection/manufacturer guidance should determine whether the item can return to service.

## Preserve evidence before disposal
Photograph the appliance, model/serial number, water level or damage, and power status before disposal if insurance or assistance may apply. Record whether it was energized during the flood and any burning smell, tripped protection, visible corrosion, or damaged cord. This history also helps an electrician prioritize the inspection.
