# Damaged Solar PV After Fire, Flood, Hail, or Storm

Solar panels can continue to produce electrical energy when light is present even if utility power is off. Storm, hail, fire, or flood damage can also leave broken modules, wet connectors, damaged wiring, or loose rooftop components. A household should not assume that “the grid is down” makes the solar system electrically dead.

## Isolate people from the damaged array
Keep occupants away from broken modules, exposed wiring, flooded equipment, and fallen rooftop components. Do not touch, hose, move, or dismantle damaged photovoltaic hardware. If the system has an approved emergency shutdown/disconnect procedure, follow only the documented procedure that the installer/manufacturer has taught the user to operate safely.

## Inspection comes before re-energization
U.S. Department of Energy resilience guidance notes the need for electrical inspection after visible severe-weather damage and highlights that submerged PV components can require replacement. Record which components were exposed to fire, impact, hail, or water and leave testing/repair to qualified solar/electrical personnel.

## Tell responders the system exists
Mark the location of panels, inverter, battery storage, disconnects, and meter on the household utility map. During fire or structural response, tell responders about PV and battery systems; they may need to manage hazards differently from a conventional de-energized building.
