# Accessible Stairwell Evacuation and Buddy Accountability

A “buddy” plan only works if the helpers, route, communication method, equipment, and fallback are defined before smoke, darkness, crowding, or an outage. It should add redundancy without making one neighbor solely responsible for another person’s life.

## Pair the person with a small team
Identify a primary helper and at least one backup. Share only the information each helper needs: how to communicate, which mobility aid travels, whether a service animal is involved, where the go-kit is, and what the official building plan says about stairwells or protected waiting areas. If a helper is absent, the plan should still work through the backup/building system.

## Keep the stair route usable
Do not store kits or mobility devices in a way that blocks the escape path. Plan for darkness, hearing loss, low vision, respiratory limits, and handrail use. If a person cannot safely use stairs, do not improvise a carry; notify building management/emergency services and follow the building’s evacuation-assistance procedure.

## Accountability continues outside
The helper role is not complete at the lobby. Move to the agreed meeting point, report missing people without re-entering, and preserve medical/communication support while waiting for responders. Record drill problems so the next exercise changes the plan instead of repeating the same failure.
