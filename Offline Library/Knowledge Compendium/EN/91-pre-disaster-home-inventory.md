# Pre-disaster home inventory: document what exists before damage makes reconstruction difficult

## Inventory the things that would be hard to remember
Start with rooms and major categories: appliances, electronics, tools, furniture, bicycles, important collections and higher-value household equipment. You do not need to catalog every spoon. The purpose is to give future-you enough evidence to reconstruct significant losses and identify serial/model information.

## Photograph from wide to close
Take an overview of each room, then important items and model/serial labels where relevant. Open cupboards or storage only if doing so does not expose sensitive information unnecessarily. A short walkthrough video can capture context, but still photos make indexing easier.

## Attach purchase evidence selectively
Receipts, invoices, warranty emails and manuals can support an inventory, but copying every payment record creates a large privacy burden. Link only useful evidence and keep financial account data minimized. Store a protected offline copy separate from the home if your threat model allows it.

## Include emergency infrastructure
Document smoke/CO alarms, extinguishers, water storage, solar/power equipment, mobility/medical-support equipment and major tools. These are not only claim items; the inventory helps maintenance and replacement planning.

## Review after material changes
Update after renovation, major purchase, disposal or move. A date-stamped inventory older than the property itself may mislead. Delete sold/discarded items so the file remains credible.

## Keep the inventory private
A detailed list of valuables can help a thief as much as an insurer. Do not publish the full document or store it unprotected in a public cloud link. Share only the relevant subset during a claim.
