# Roadside Breakdown: Visibility, Traffic, and Safe Waiting Zone

A breakdown on a high-speed road can be more immediately dangerous than the mechanical fault. Protect people from traffic first; repairs come second.

## Move out of the traffic stream if possible
If the vehicle can safely reach a shoulder, turnout or other protected area, do so. Do not stop around a blind curve, crest or narrow lane when a safer location is reachable. Use hazard signaling according to local road rules.

## Decide where occupants wait
The safest place varies by road geometry, barriers, weather and traffic. Avoid standing between vehicles, beside the traffic-facing side, or in a location where an errant vehicle would hit the group. Follow police/road-operator guidance. Children and animals should remain controlled and away from the carriageway.

## Do not attempt unsafe roadside repair
Changing a tire or inspecting under a vehicle inches from fast traffic may be unacceptable even if technically simple. Call road assistance/emergency services when the work zone cannot be made safe. Never crawl beneath a vehicle supported only by a jack.

## Make the handoff precise
Report road number, travel direction, nearest marker/exit, vehicle description, exact hazard (lane blocked, smoke, injury, fuel leak) and number of people. Preserve phone battery after contact.
