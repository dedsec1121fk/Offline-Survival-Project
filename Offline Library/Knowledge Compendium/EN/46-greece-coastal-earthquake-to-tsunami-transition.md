# Greece: from coastal earthquake to tsunami decision

## Link the hazards in one plan
A coastal earthquake plan that ends when shaking stops is incomplete. If the household is in a low-lying coastal area, the same earthquake may create tsunami risk. Before any event, identify whether frequent locations fall inside locally recognized coastal hazard or evacuation zones and where safer higher or inland ground lies.

## Immediate sequence
First protect against the earthquake. When shaking stops, if there was strong or long shaking or another natural tsunami warning and local guidance calls for evacuation, move promptly away from the shoreline. Do not go to the beach, harbour or seafront road to inspect water movement. Bring only what is already staged and necessary for life safety.

## Islands and constrained roads
On an island, moving inland may mean gaining elevation rather than travelling a long horizontal distance. Identify foot routes that do not rely on one coastal road or vehicle. A narrow road can gridlock quickly; follow local evacuation signage and Civil Protection instructions rather than improvising through unstable slopes or damaged streets.

## After reaching safer ground
Account for the group, provide first aid, send a concise status message and remain out of the hazard zone. Multiple waves can arrive and dangerous currents can persist. Do not use the first calm period as permission to return. Monitor official information through 112, local authorities and other available channels.

## Night and tourist-season complications
Keep footwear, light and basic identification near the bed in coastal accommodation. Visitors should learn the address and nearest safer route rather than assuming a host or hotel will always be able to guide them. During crowded summer conditions, walking routes and meeting points may matter more than vehicle capacity.
