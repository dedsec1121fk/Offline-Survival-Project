# Dry Staple Storage: Moisture, Pests, and Rotation

Rice, pasta, oats, flour, legumes and similar staples can provide a large fraction of outage calories, but only if they remain dry, pest-free and actually usable with the water/fuel you have. A pantry full of food that requires long cooking can become a fuel problem.

## Protect from moisture first
Store dry foods in clean, food-grade, tightly closing containers appropriate to the product and planned storage period. Keep containers off damp floors and away from leaking walls. Condensation can develop when cold containers are repeatedly moved into warm humid air; inspect rather than assuming a sealed-looking bin is dry inside.

## Detect pests early
Use a first-in/first-out rotation and inspect packaging for holes, webbing, larvae, insects, rodent gnawing or droppings. Isolate a suspect package from the main pantry before opening it. Rodent contamination is a sanitation issue, not just lost calories.

## Plan cooking energy and water
Group staples by cooking burden. Quick-cooking oats, couscous or precooked/dehydrated foods can reduce fuel demand. Dried beans may be calorie-efficient to store but require water and significant cooking unless soaked/pressure-cooked appropriately. Keep some ready-to-eat food for periods when cooking is unsafe or impossible.

## Make the inventory operational
Record package count/weight, normal serving use, opening date and minimum reserve. Rotate what the household actually eats. Do not build the emergency menu around unfamiliar foods that someone cannot tolerate, prepare, chew or digest.
