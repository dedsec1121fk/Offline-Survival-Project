# Emergency water containers: cleaning, labeling, rotation and contamination control

## The container is part of water safety
A treated water source can become unsafe if it is placed in a dirty container or handled with contaminated hands. Use food-grade containers intended for water or beverages, with closures that protect the contents. Containers that previously held toxic chemicals should never be repurposed for drinking water.

## Clean before filling
Follow authoritative emergency-water guidance and the container manufacturer’s cleaning instructions. Wash and sanitize suitable containers before storing water, then allow them to be handled without recontaminating the interior, lid or dispensing surface. Avoid touching the inside of a cap or neck with unwashed hands.

## Label what the container actually contains
Mark drinking water distinctly from nonpotable water. Include fill/treatment date, source and, during an incident, a batch identifier. If a container is refilled from a different source, update the label rather than relying on its old identity. Keep chemically suspect or utility water physically separate from drinking supplies.

## Dispense without dipping
A tap or controlled pour is preferable to repeatedly inserting cups, hands or utensils. If the mouth or spout contacts an unclean surface, mark the container for review. Do not top up an old partial container with new treated water and thereby hide the older batch history.

## Storage location matters
Keep water protected from direct sun, excessive heat, flooding, fuel, pesticides and other chemicals. Heavy containers also create lifting and floor-load problems; use sizes household members can handle safely and distribute them sensibly rather than making one inaccessible reservoir.

## Rotation is inspection
When rotating stored water, inspect for damaged caps, leaks, discoloration, unexpected odor, degraded plastic or contamination around the spout. Rotation is also the time to clean the storage area and confirm that the household has an opener/pump compatible with the containers. Follow current local/public-health guidance for storage duration rather than assuming one universal calendar interval.
