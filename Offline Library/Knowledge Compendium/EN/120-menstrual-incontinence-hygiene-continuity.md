# Menstrual and incontinence hygiene continuity: plan dignity, water and waste together

## Count consumption realistically
Emergency kits often list generic hygiene products but do not match the person’s normal product type, size, frequency or skin needs. Keep an individualized reserve and note where it is stored. Include disposal bags, clean underwear, hand hygiene, skin-protection items already used by the person, and any accessible changing equipment.

## Protect privacy and clean surfaces
Choose a changing location with a closable door or visual barrier, a clean surface and nearby handwashing. Shared shelters or crowded homes need a method to signal occupancy without forcing someone to explain a private medical or hygiene need. Keep clean supplies above floor level and away from toilet splash or flood contamination.

## Water loss changes the workflow
If normal bathing or laundry is unavailable, prioritize handwashing and safe cleaning of the body according to health guidance. Reusable products require a reliable cleaning/drying process; do not adopt them during an outage merely because disposable stock is low if safe washing cannot be maintained. Conversely, disposable products create a larger waste burden that must be contained.

## Waste must be closed and separated
Use secure bags or containers and follow local waste rules. Do not flush products that the sanitation system is not designed to accept, especially during a sewer disruption. Store waste where children and animals cannot access it and where leakage cannot contaminate clean supplies.

## Watch for continuity problems
Skin breakdown, pain, bleeding outside the person’s normal pattern, urinary symptoms, dehydration or inability to keep the area clean can require medical advice. The project should support logistics and records, not diagnose or replace clinical care.

## Include this in evacuation planning
A go-bag should carry enough preferred supplies for the travel/arrival period, not the entire home stock. Put additional reserve in a labeled module that can be loaded quickly. For a dependent person, document who carries supplies and who is the backup caregiver.
