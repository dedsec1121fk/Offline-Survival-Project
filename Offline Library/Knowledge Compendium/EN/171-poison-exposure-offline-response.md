# Poison Exposure: Preserve the Label, Stop Exposure, and Get Expert Guidance

During an outage or cleanup, poison exposures may involve cleaners, fuels, pesticides, batteries, medications, carbon monoxide, solvents, plants, or contaminated containers. The wrong household “antidote” can add a second injury, so the offline plan focuses on immediate decontamination and expert contact.

## First priority depends on the exposure route
If dangerous fumes are involved, move to fresh air without entering an unsafe atmosphere. For skin exposure, remove contaminated clothing and rinse with plentiful water when appropriate. For eye exposure, begin immediate irrigation. For a swallowed substance, do not induce vomiting or give a homemade neutralizer unless a poison professional/product-specific instruction explicitly tells you to do so.

## Preserve information
Keep the container, label, product name, concentration if shown, approximate amount involved, route of exposure, time, and the person’s age/weight if known. Photograph the label if the container cannot travel safely. Do not bring a leaking/contaminated container into a vehicle or shelter just to show it to clinicians.

## Call for expert help
If the person collapses, has a seizure, has serious breathing difficulty, or cannot be awakened, activate emergency services immediately. Otherwise contact the appropriate local poison-information/emergency medical service as soon as possible. Poison Control guidance emphasizes getting specific expert advice rather than guessing because treatment varies greatly by substance and route.

## Offline preparation
Write your country/region poison-information contact on the emergency contact card before an incident. This project should not hard-code a foreign poison number as if it applies everywhere. Keep chemical safety data/labels offline for products stored in bulk at home, workshop, farm, or boat.
