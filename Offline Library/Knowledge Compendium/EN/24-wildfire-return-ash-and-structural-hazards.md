# Returning after wildfire: ash, utilities, and hidden structural hazards

## Permission to return is only the first gate
Do not re-enter an evacuated area until authorities permit it. Permission means the area is open enough for return; it does not certify an individual building, tree, retaining wall, propane system, well, electrical installation or slope as safe. Approach the property as a new hazard scene.

## Conduct an exterior stop
Before entering, look for downed lines, leaning poles, damaged trees, unstable masonry, displaced tanks, smouldering material, damaged decks and signs that a slope has changed. After wildfire, later rain can also create debris-flow risk on burned slopes. If the building has obvious structural movement, fire damage, strong fuel odour or active utility damage, remain outside and obtain professional or emergency guidance.

## Ash is not ordinary household dust
Avoid stirring dry ash. Children and animals should not play in ash or cleanup debris. Follow local public-health instructions for protective clothing and respiratory protection, and use cleanup methods that reduce airborne dust. Do not use a household vacuum that simply redistributes fine particles unless it is specifically suitable for the material and local guidance supports that method.

## Water, food, and utilities need separate acceptance
A visually intact refrigerator, water tank or private well may still have been affected by heat, smoke, prolonged outage, contaminated runoff or damaged plumbing. Treat each system independently. Follow water-utility or well-testing instructions; discard food according to outage, heat, smoke and contamination guidance rather than smell-testing; and have damaged electrical or fuel systems assessed before normal use.

## Document before discarding
Photograph affected rooms, equipment, serial numbers, structural damage and debris before cleanup when it is safe. Keep a short chronology of evacuation, return authorization, utility status and major expenses. This can support insurance, public relief and later repair decisions without requiring you to preserve dangerous items.
