# Radiation emergency: shelter, contamination, food, water, and movement boundaries

## Radiation emergencies can require sheltering instead of immediate evacuation
A radiological release, nuclear power-plant accident, dirty-bomb event, or nuclear detonation is not managed like an ordinary chemical odor or house fire. CDC and Ready.gov guidance emphasizes getting inside a substantial building, moving toward the basement or central interior area away from exterior walls/roof, staying inside, and staying tuned for official instructions. Do not self-evacuate through a contaminated outdoor area merely because leaving feels more active.

## Reduce removable contamination without creating a new exposure
If a person was outside when material was released, follow current official decontamination instructions. In general, removing the outer layer of clothing and carefully washing exposed skin can reduce external contamination. Keep contaminated items separated from clean living space. Do not use harsh scrubbing that damages skin, and do not delay urgent medical care for serious injuries.

## Food and water decisions differ from microbial contamination
CDC radiation guidance says sealed food/drink containers and food that was already protected indoors are generally preferred, while authorities assess public water supplies. A crucial boundary: boiling water can kill many microorganisms but does not remove radioactive material. Do not apply the ordinary boil-water chapter to a radiological advisory unless authorities specifically instruct it.

## Medication usually remains important
CDC guidance notes that medicines kept indoors should generally continue to be taken; externally contaminated containers can be wiped according to instructions. Do not stop essential medicine merely because a radiation event occurred unless a clinician or authority directs otherwise. Likewise, do not take potassium iodide or any other protective medicine unless public-health authorities instruct the relevant population to do so.

## Ventilation decisions depend on official instructions and building safety
Close doors/windows and reduce outside-air intake if authorities direct sheltering. However, do not create dangerous overheating, carbon monoxide, fire, or medical-equipment failure by following a generic ventilation rule blindly. If the building becomes unsafe for another reason, follow emergency officials’ relocation directions.

## Evacuation comes when authorities identify route and timing
Emergency officials may later direct evacuation, screening, or movement to a shelter. Bring essential medicines, identification, important papers, necessary accessibility equipment, and required pet supplies. Use the route officials identify; an apparently shorter route may pass through a contaminated area.
