# Medication Reconciliation After Evacuation or Displacement

During an evacuation, medicines may be split between bags, replaced by a pharmacy, supplied by a clinic, or temporarily unavailable. The greatest documentation risk is ending up with multiple partially correct lists. Medication reconciliation means creating one current list that identifies what the person is actually taking and which uncertainties still need a pharmacist or clinician.

## Build the list from evidence
Use prescription containers, pharmacy records, discharge paperwork, device apps, clinician lists, and the person/caregiver’s knowledge. For each item record the medicine name, strength/form as printed, normal schedule from the existing prescription, purpose if already known, prescriber/pharmacy, remaining supply, and any unresolved discrepancy. Do not infer a dose from pill color or from another family member’s prescription.

## Mark changes instead of overwriting history
If a clinic changes a medicine during displacement, record the date, who directed the change, and whether the old supply is discontinued, held, or still needed. Physically separate medicines that should no longer be used to reduce accidental duplication. Photograph labels when appropriate, but do not rely on a single phone as the only copy.

## Escalate conflicting information
Two containers with different strengths, missing labels, duplicate brands/generics, or an unfamiliar substitute should trigger pharmacy/clinical confirmation. The Command Center can track the discrepancy but must not choose between competing prescriptions. This is especially important when more than one facility has provided emergency refills.

## Verification anchor
- Current pharmacy record, prescription label, discharge instructions, and treating clinician/pharmacist are the controlling sources.
