# Building Collapse: Actions for a Trapped or Partially Trapped Person

A collapsed structure is an unstable system, not just a pile of material. Secondary collapse, dust, fire, gas, electricity, sharp debris and confined spaces can turn an attempted rescue into additional casualties. Untrained household action should focus on signaling, air, immediate life threats that can be reached safely, and professional rescue access.

## If you are trapped
Stay as calm and still as conditions allow. Protect your mouth/nose from dust with available fabric without making breathing harder. Avoid unnecessary movement that raises dust or shifts debris. Use a whistle, repeated tapping pattern, flashlight, or phone message to signal location. Shouting continuously consumes air and energy and may be less effective than periodic signals.

## If you can communicate with a trapped person
Tell them help is being sought and ask about breathing, bleeding, pain, entrapment points, smoke/fire, water, and whether the void is changing. Record the last confirmed contact and exact location markers. Do not promise a rescue time you cannot know.

## Do not perform uncontrolled debris removal
Removing one beam, slab, wall section, or compressed object can transfer load or cause crushing injury to worsen. Do not enter a visibly unstable void, crawl beneath unsupported concrete, or use a vehicle/jack/rope system without competent structural rescue knowledge. Mark the location and preserve access for trained teams.

## Safe immediate help
From a stable edge, you may be able to pass a light, whistle, clean air opening, or bleeding-control material if that does not disturb debris. Shut off nearby utilities only from a known safe control point. Keep ignition sources away from possible gas leaks.

## Information for responders
Provide number of missing people, last-known locations, building layout, hazards, utility state, sounds/signals, visible voids, and any changes since the collapse. Keep bystanders away from the collapse perimeter so rescue routes remain clear.
