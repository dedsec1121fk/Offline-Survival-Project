# Portable generator safety: carbon monoxide, placement, and electrical boundaries

## The generator belongs outside
Portable fuel-burning generators create carbon monoxide, an invisible poisonous gas. CDC guidance recommends operating a portable generator outside, more than 20 feet (about 6 metres) from windows, doors and vents, with exhaust directed away from the building. A garage, basement, enclosed balcony, shed attached to living space, corridor or “open window” arrangement is not a substitute for true outdoor placement.

## Detect the hazard
Use working battery-powered or battery-backup carbon-monoxide alarms according to their manufacturer instructions. Headache, dizziness, weakness, nausea, confusion or unusual sleepiness in multiple people during generator or combustion use should be treated as a possible carbon-monoxide emergency: get to fresh air and seek emergency help rather than trying to troubleshoot indoors.

## Electrical isolation
Do not connect a generator to household wiring through an improvised “backfeed” cable. A proper building connection requires appropriate transfer/isolation equipment installed according to electrical code and qualified guidance; otherwise the generator can energize utility lines and endanger workers or damage equipment. For direct loads, use suitable outdoor-rated cords, protect connections from water and stay within the generator and cord ratings.

## Fuel and hot equipment
Refuel only after following the manufacturer’s shutdown/cooling instructions. Store fuel in approved containers away from living areas, ignition sources and the running generator. Keep the exhaust area clear of dry vegetation and combustible material. Never move a hot unit into an enclosed space just to protect it from weather.

## Load discipline
Start with critical loads and calculate continuous demand, startup surge and fuel endurance. Refrigeration, medical equipment and communications may deserve priority over comfort loads. Log run periods and fuel remaining; a generator with no fuel reserve or no safe placement is not a dependable resilience resource.
