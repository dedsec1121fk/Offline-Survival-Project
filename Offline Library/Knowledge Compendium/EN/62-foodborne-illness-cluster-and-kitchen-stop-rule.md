# Foodborne-illness cluster and kitchen stop rule

When several people become ill after sharing food or water, the priority is not to prove the exact organism at home. The useful household action is to stop exposing more people, preserve factual information and obtain medical/public-health advice when indicated.

## Recognize a possible common exposure

Record who is ill, symptoms, when symptoms began and what foods/water were shared in the preceding period. Do not assume the last meal is automatically responsible. Keep the record factual; avoid labelling a person or cook as the cause without evidence.

## Stop the suspected pathway

Set aside the suspect food or water so nobody else consumes it. Do not “test” questionable food by tasting. Clean hands and separate food-preparation surfaces according to safe-food guidance. If a refrigerator outage, flood, sewage event or water advisory occurred, include that timeline because it changes the investigation.

## Medical boundaries

Severe dehydration, blood in stool or vomit, persistent high fever, neurological symptoms, severe pain, breathing difficulty, signs of shock, illness in an infant or other vulnerable person, or rapidly worsening condition require prompt professional assessment. Medication decisions belong with appropriate clinicians/pharmacists.

## Preserve useful evidence

Keep packaging, product name, lot/date information, receipts and photos when safe. If authorities or a clinician advise retaining a food sample, follow their instructions; otherwise do not keep hazardous food where it can be eaten accidentally.

## Reopen the kitchen deliberately

Resume normal preparation only after the contamination pathway is understood and the affected surfaces, water source and cold chain are safe for use.

**Source anchors:** WHO food-safety principles, CDC food-safety/disaster guidance and local public-health reporting requirements.
