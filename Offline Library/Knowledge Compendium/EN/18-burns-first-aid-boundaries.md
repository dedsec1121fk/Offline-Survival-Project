# Burns after fire, scald, electricity, or chemicals

## Stop the source without creating a second injury
Before treating a burn, make sure the person is no longer exposed to flame, hot liquid, live electricity, steam, or chemical splash. Do not touch a person who is still in contact with an energised electrical source. For chemical exposure, avoid contaminating yourself and follow the product or emergency-service decontamination instructions.

## Cool thermal burns with safe running water when appropriate
Modern first-aid guidance favours cooling a thermal burn with clean, cool running water rather than ice. Ice and extremely cold applications can injure tissue further. Remove jewellery or tight items early if they are not stuck, because swelling can occur. Do not pull clothing or material that is adherent to the burn.

## Avoid household remedies
Butter, toothpaste, oil, powders, creams not specifically recommended, and other household substances can trap heat, contaminate the injury, or make later medical assessment harder. After appropriate cooling, protect the area with a clean non-stick covering according to first-aid guidance. Large, deep, electrical, chemical, airway, facial, hand, genital, major-joint, or circumferential burns need a lower threshold for professional assessment.

## Electrical burns may hide internal injury
A small skin mark does not show the full effect of electrical current. Make the power source safe before approach and obtain medical evaluation when significant electrical exposure is suspected. Lightning injury is also a medical emergency and can affect heart rhythm, breathing, the nervous system, and hearing even when skin damage appears limited.

## Watch the whole person
A burn incident may also involve smoke inhalation, trauma, hypothermia after cooling, or shock. Difficulty breathing, facial burns with soot, altered responsiveness, or severe pain are reasons to escalate urgently. The purpose of the offline chapter is to help the household avoid harmful improvisation, not replace a first-aid course.
