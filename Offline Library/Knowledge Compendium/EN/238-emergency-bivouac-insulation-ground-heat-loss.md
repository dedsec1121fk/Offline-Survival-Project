# Emergency Bivouac: Insulation, Wind Control, and Ground Heat Loss

An emergency bivouac is a controlled pause that keeps a delay from becoming a cold injury. It is not a construction contest. The priorities are getting out of wind and precipitation, insulating the body from the ground, protecting dry clothing, and keeping enough ventilation to avoid moisture and combustion hazards.

## Stop before exhaustion removes options
If darkness, injury, weather or navigation uncertainty makes continued travel unsafe, choose a protected site while there is still enough energy and light to organize it. Pushing until everyone is exhausted can leave the group unable to make even a simple windbreak or dry sleeping area.

## Build from the ground upward
Conduction to cold ground can drain heat rapidly. Use a sleeping pad, pack, spare clothing, dry natural material where lawful, cardboard, foam, or other dry insulating layers. Avoid crushing all insulation flat. Keep waterproof barriers from trapping wet clothing directly against the body for long periods.

## Reduce wind without sealing the shelter
A small windbreak or sheltered side of terrain can reduce convective heat loss dramatically. Do not completely seal a tiny shelter around breathing occupants; moisture accumulates and dampens insulation. If a stove or flame is used, move cooking to a safe ventilated location and follow device instructions. Never improvise an enclosed combustion heater.

## Protect the dry core
Designate one set of the driest available layers for rest. Isolate wet socks/gloves from sleeping insulation. Cover head and hands, loosen constricting footwear if safe, and eat/drink according to available safe supplies and the person's medical needs. Avoid alcohol as a warming strategy.

## Reassess before moving again
At first light or when weather changes, check injuries, foot condition, hydration, route confidence, remaining daylight and rescue/communication status before breaking the shelter. If staying put remains safer, improve signaling and conserve energy.
