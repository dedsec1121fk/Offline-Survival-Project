# Emergency documents: create usable copies without creating a privacy disaster

## Decide what must survive
Identification details, insurance contacts, property/vehicle information, medication list, essential prescriptions, emergency contacts and critical account/service references may be difficult to reconstruct during displacement. Store only what your household genuinely needs; an emergency archive should not become a copy of every confidential document you own.

## Use two forms with different risks
A physical packet works without electricity but can be lost or stolen. An encrypted digital copy can hold more detail but depends on a device, credentials and power. Use both where appropriate and understand the protection of each. The project’s normal local exports are plaintext unless the device/filesystem protects them, so do not assume the ZIP or JSON is encrypted.

## Waterproof the physical minimum
Use a water-resistant inner pouch and keep it accessible for evacuation but not visibly exposed in the home or vehicle. Consider copies rather than originals when accepted for the purpose; originals that are required should have a designated owner during evacuation.

## Make digital files readable offline
Do not rely on cloud-only links. Export essential documents in common formats and test opening them while the phone is in airplane mode. Record passwords/recovery methods in a secure way appropriate to your threat model; never put the password in the same unprotected file as the encrypted archive.

## Date and review
A five-year-old insurance number or discontinued medication is worse than no quick-reference card because it appears authoritative. Put a review date on the packet and update after a move, policy change, new prescription or vehicle change.

## Redact what you share
For shelter registration, mutual aid or a repair contractor, provide only the necessary page/data. The redacted schema template should contain no personal operational data; use that kind of blank structure when demonstrating the project publicly.
