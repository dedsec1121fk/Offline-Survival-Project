# Small-Boat Capsize: Stay With the Vessel and Count People

After a capsize, cold shock, waves and panic can turn a short swim into a life-threatening effort. The exact response depends on fire, surf, current and vessel type, but accountability and flotation come first.

## Count people immediately
Establish who is in the water and who is missing. Put on or keep life jackets secured. If the capsized vessel is floating and not presenting an immediate hazard, it can be easier for rescuers to see than individual swimmers and may provide flotation/support. Do not leave it for a distant shore simply because land looks close.

## Reduce energy loss
Limit unnecessary swimming, keep airway clear and use the flotation/device techniques taught for your equipment. In cold water, protect heat and get out of the water onto a stable craft/platform when safely possible. Avoid climbing actions that repeatedly roll the craft onto other people.

## Signal and communicate
Use marine radio, emergency beacon, phone or visual signals appropriate to the voyage. Give position, number of people, vessel description and immediate hazards. Keep the group together when conditions allow so nobody disappears unnoticed.
