# Wilderness Cooking Fire: Site Control and Extinguishment

In a survival situation, a fire that escapes control can be more dangerous than the cold or meal it was meant to solve. Fire use must follow local restrictions, weather, fuel conditions and site rules. When wildfire danger is high or bans are in force, use a safer permitted alternative rather than assuming emergency need overrides restrictions.

## Choose the smallest safe method
Prefer a stable approved stove when permitted and conditions allow. If an open fire is legal and necessary, use an existing fire ring or a site cleared from vegetation, roots, overhanging branches and wind-driven fuels. Keep water or another appropriate extinguishing resource immediately available before ignition.

## Control sparks and fuel
Use only manageable fuel. Do not burn plastics, batteries, aerosol cans, treated wood or unknown materials. Keep fuel storage, tents, dry grass and clothing outside the spark/heat zone. Never leave flame or hot coals unattended, including during a short water run.

## Wind changes the plan
Strong or shifting wind can carry embers beyond the visible site. If gusts increase or vegetation begins receiving sparks, extinguish early rather than trying to “watch it closely.” Do not build a fire under a canopy or against a rock/tree where heat can damage roots or vegetation.

## Extinguish completely
Drown with water where permitted, stir, expose hidden hot material, add more water and repeat until the remains are cool to the touch. Do not bury hot coals; buried material can retain heat and spread through roots or duff. If water is too scarce to extinguish reliably, that scarcity is a reason not to start the fire.

## Before leaving
Check outside the fire ring for escaped embers and wind-blown material. Record any restrictions or incident in the field log. If a fire escapes control, prioritize evacuation and emergency reporting rather than personal suppression beyond your capability.
