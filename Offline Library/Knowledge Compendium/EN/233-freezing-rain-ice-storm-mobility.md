# Freezing Rain and Ice Storms: Mobility, Falling Ice, and Power Loss

Freezing rain can make roads, stairs, branches, roofs and power lines dangerous at the same time. The hazard is not only slippery ground: ice loading can bring down limbs and utilities, while prolonged outages add heating and carbon-monoxide risks.

## Decide whether travel is necessary
When official warnings describe dangerous icing, postponing travel is often safer than relying on vehicle capability. Four-wheel drive does not improve braking on ice. If movement is essential, reduce speed greatly, increase distance, avoid abrupt steering/braking and choose routes with fewer steep grades, bridges and exposed sections.

## On foot, treat every shiny surface as suspect
Use footwear with good traction, keep hands free for balance and take short deliberate steps. Avoid walking beneath ice-loaded branches, damaged utility lines, roof edges or structures shedding ice. Do not touch or approach a downed line; wet icy ground can carry electrical hazards.

## Prepare for power loss before it happens
Charge essential devices, verify safe heating options, protect water pipes where relevant, and make sure smoke/carbon-monoxide alarms have working backup power. Never use a gas oven, charcoal grill, camping stove, or generator indoors as a heating substitute. Generators must remain outdoors and appropriately separated from openings according to manufacturer and fire-safety guidance.

## Vehicle stranding
If travel becomes impossible, move fully out of traffic when feasible, communicate location, conserve fuel/battery and use visible signaling. If sheltering in a vehicle with the engine running intermittently, keep the exhaust path clear of snow/ice and remain alert to carbon-monoxide risk. Follow current local emergency advice.

## After the ice starts melting
Falling branches/ice and damaged lines can remain hazardous. Do not assume restoration means every private circuit or tree is safe.
