# Long-Outage Cleaning and Disinfection Priorities

During a prolonged outage, cleaning must protect health without consuming all water, mixing dangerous chemicals, or spreading contamination from toilets, raw food, floodwater or illness into sleeping and food-preparation areas. Prioritize by transmission risk rather than by appearance.

## Establish clean and dirty zones
Keep toilet/waste handling, contaminated laundry, raw-food work and flood-damaged material away from drinking-water storage, medicine handling, clean utensils and sleeping equipment. Use separate cloths/tools where practical. Wash hands at the transition between dirty and clean tasks.

## Clean before disinfecting when required
Visible soil can reduce the effectiveness of some disinfectants. Remove debris and wash surfaces with appropriate detergent/clean water before applying a disinfectant according to its label and the target hazard. More concentrated is not automatically better; incorrect concentration can damage surfaces or injure people.

## Never create a chemical gas hazard
Do not mix bleach with ammonia, acids, vinegar, toilet cleaners or other chemicals. Ventilate according to product instructions and use gloves/eye protection where required. Keep chemicals in original labeled containers and away from children, food and drinking-water containers.

## Spend limited water on high-value hygiene
Prioritize hands after toilet/waste handling and before food/medicine tasks, food-contact surfaces, shared toilet touch points when illness is present, and cleanup of bodily fluids. Cosmetic floor or exterior cleaning can wait when the potable-water reserve is low.

## Contaminated porous items need a separate decision
Flood-soaked insulation, mattresses, some upholstered items and materials contaminated with sewage may not be safely restored by surface wiping. Follow local public-health/remediation guidance and isolate questionable material from living areas.

## Keep a cleaning log in shared shelters
Record area, contamination type, product/dilution as directed by label, time, person and whether the area is released for use. This prevents duplicate chemical application and missed high-risk surfaces.
