# Winter-Stranded Vehicle: Shelter, Signaling, and Carbon Monoxide

A stranded vehicle can provide wind protection and visibility to rescuers, but snow can also block the exhaust and create carbon-monoxide danger if the engine is run. Decide whether remaining with the vehicle is safer than walking based on official guidance, location, weather and known nearby shelter—not panic.

## Make the vehicle visible
Use hazard/marker methods that do not drain the battery unnecessarily and that local authorities recognize. Clear snow around identifying surfaces when safe. Communicate coordinates, road name/direction, vehicle description, occupants and medical needs before battery/network deteriorates.

## Conserve heat
Add dry layers, hats, gloves and insulation beneath/around occupants. Keep wet clothing out of the main insulation. Share blankets without restricting circulation. Small movements can help circulation, but avoid sweating.

## Engine/CO boundary
If guidance supports periodic engine use for heat, the exhaust outlet must remain completely clear of snow and the vehicle must not be in an enclosed/partially enclosed location. Cracked windows are not a substitute for a clear exhaust and safe location. If anyone develops headache, dizziness, nausea or confusion, stop the suspected exposure and move to fresh air safely while seeking help.
