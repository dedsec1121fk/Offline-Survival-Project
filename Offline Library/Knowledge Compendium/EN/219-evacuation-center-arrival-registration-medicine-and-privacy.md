# Evacuation Center Arrival: Registration, Medicine, and Privacy

Arriving at an evacuation center is a transition, not the end of the plan. Crowding, noise, unfamiliar routines and limited power/storage can create new problems for medicines, accessibility, children, animals and privacy.

## Register and preserve accountability
Follow the center's registration/check-in process and tell household contacts where you are when safe. Keep the group meeting rule active: children and dependent adults should know which staff point or household member to approach if separated.

## Declare critical needs early
Tell staff promptly about mobility/communication accommodations, powered medical equipment, refrigeration-dependent medication, serious food allergy, service animals and urgent prescription gaps. Do not wait until a battery or dose is almost exhausted. Keep medication in original labeled packaging where possible and maintain your own medication list.

## Protect identity and essential documents
Carry the minimum documents needed for services in a controlled pouch. Do not publicly display full IDs, medical details or contact lists more than necessary. Shared charging stations and public Wi-Fi should not be treated as trusted places for sensitive accounts; prioritize charging and use local/offline copies when possible.

## Reassess daily
Shelter conditions, road access and household needs change. Review the next 24 hours: medicines, charging, food/allergies, bathing/toilet access, sleep, animals, communication and expected relocation. Update the handover brief rather than relying on yesterday's assumptions.
