# Payment outage and cash continuity: preserve purchasing ability without concentrating loss

## A working shop may have a failed payment channel
Power, telecom or banking disruptions can leave shelves stocked but cards, ATMs or mobile payments unavailable. Keep a modest emergency payment reserve appropriate to your household and local context. Avoid storing excessive cash in one obvious location.

## Use small practical denominations
During a communications outage a seller may be unable to provide change or verify large notes. A mix of ordinary denominations can be more useful than one large note. Keep coins/notes dry and separate from documents that reveal all account details.

## Record emergency purchases
For fuel, lodging, repairs and essential supplies, keep receipts or photograph them when safe. If no formal receipt is available, write date, seller, item, amount and payment method. This supports household budgeting and may help later insurance/recovery documentation; it does not guarantee reimbursement.

## Separate currencies in the ledger
Do not add euros, dollars and other currencies into one meaningless total. Keep each currency separate unless you deliberately record a conversion rate/date. The Command Center’s expense ledger should preserve the original amount and currency.

## Plan for account access without exposing credentials
Store bank/insurer contact information, not full credentials in an unprotected emergency note. Know how to freeze a card or contact the provider if a wallet is lost during evacuation. Device authentication and account recovery should not depend on a single phone that may be damaged.

## Reconcile when systems return
Compare emergency notes with bank records, identify duplicate charges and attach evidence to recovery cases. Refill the cash reserve only after normal access is stable and review whether the reserve amount matched actual needs.
