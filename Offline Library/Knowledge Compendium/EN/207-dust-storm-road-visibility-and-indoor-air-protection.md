# Dust Storm: Road Visibility and Indoor Air Protection

Dense dust can reduce road visibility to almost nothing and can worsen respiratory symptoms. The safest travel decision may be not entering the dust area at all.

## If driving
Follow local weather/transport instructions. If dense blowing dust makes safe travel impossible, leave the traffic lane as far as practical onto a safe area, stop, set the parking brake and follow the local road-safety guidance for vehicle lighting so following drivers do not mistake your stopped vehicle for moving traffic. Do not stop in a travel lane.

## If indoors
Close openings that admit dust when doing so does not create another ventilation hazard. Use appropriate filtration if available and protect people with asthma/respiratory disease according to their medical plan. Avoid adding indoor particles through smoking, candles or unnecessary combustion.

## After the event
Dust can hide road debris and reduce traction. Clean respiratory/eye protection and filters according to their instructions rather than beating contaminated dust into living areas. People with persistent breathing difficulty or eye injury need medical evaluation.
