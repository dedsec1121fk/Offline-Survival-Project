# Landslide, rockfall and slope-failure decisions

Slope failures can begin with obvious movement or with small changes that are easy to dismiss: new cracks, tilting fences, doors that suddenly bind, fresh seepage, falling stones, rumbling, or a stream that changes level or becomes muddy. Treat clusters of new signs as a reason to increase distance and seek official information rather than as an invitation to inspect the slope closely.

## Before movement starts

Map steep cuts, retaining walls, ravines, road embankments and places where wildfire has removed vegetation. Keep sleeping and parking positions away from known fall lines when practical. For a mountain or island route, identify a turnaround point before entering a section with cliffs above and no safe shoulder. Heavy rain after wildfire, earthquakes and construction can change a slope that was previously stable.

## When warning signs appear

Do not stand below a cracked wall, cliff, boulder field or saturated bank to photograph it. Move away from the expected path of debris and from channels that can carry mud and rocks farther than the initial slide. If driving, slow early and turn around before entering moving water, fresh debris, collapsed pavement or a road whose outside edge has dropped. Night and heavy rain make visual judgement much less reliable.

## Household decision record

Write the exact location, time, rainfall or earthquake context, visible changes, road condition and who was informed. Mark the route as unavailable until a competent authority or owner clears it. A second route should not reuse the same valley, bridge or cut slope if one event could block both.

## Stop conditions

Never enter an active debris path, unstable structure or closed road for retrieval. After a slide, expect secondary movement, damaged utilities and undermined ground. Keep people and vehicles away from the toe and edge of the failure until the area is assessed.

**Source anchors:** Ready.gov landslide/debris-flow guidance; local civil-protection and road-authority instructions. Follow current local closures and evacuation orders.
