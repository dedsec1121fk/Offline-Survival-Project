# Insect Stings and Tick Bites: Monitoring and Escalation

Most stings and tick bites are manageable, but the important offline skill is recognizing the minority that require rapid escalation. Separate three questions: is there an immediate allergic emergency, is a stinger or tick still attached, and does the person develop a later illness pattern?

## Watch breathing and circulation first
After a sting, look for trouble breathing, swelling of the lips/tongue/throat, widespread hives with systemic symptoms, faintness, confusion, or signs of shock. A person with prescribed epinephrine should follow their emergency plan and use the prescribed device as directed. Activate emergency medical help for a severe allergic reaction; do not delay because the skin reaction looks modest.

## Local sting care
Move away from the insect source. If a honeybee stinger is visible, remove it promptly without wasting time debating a perfect technique. Wash the area and use a wrapped cold pack for discomfort and swelling. Remove rings or tight items early if a hand or limb is swelling. Multiple stings, stings inside the mouth, or symptoms away from the sting site deserve professional assessment.

## Tick removal
Use fine-tipped tweezers to grasp the tick as close to the skin as practical and pull upward with steady pressure. Avoid crushing the body, twisting aggressively, burning the tick, or coating it in household chemicals. Clean the bite area and hands afterward. Record the date, likely location of exposure, and—if useful and safe—a clear photograph of the tick.

## Monitor the days that follow
A later expanding rash, fever, severe headache, unusual fatigue, muscle/joint pain, neurologic symptoms, or worsening local infection should prompt medical advice, especially after known tick exposure. Different tick-borne diseases occur in different regions, so local public-health guidance matters more than a generic identification chart stored years ago.

## What the offline record should contain
Keep allergy history, prescribed rescue medication, expiry dates, known previous severe reactions, tick-bite date/location, symptoms, and any medical advice received. This turns a vague memory into a useful handoff if internet and normal records are unavailable.
