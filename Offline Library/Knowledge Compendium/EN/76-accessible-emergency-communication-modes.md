# Accessible emergency communication: deliver the same action through more than one channel

## A message is not delivered until it can be understood
An audible announcement may fail for someone who is deaf or hard of hearing; small text may fail for low vision; complex language may fail under cognitive stress; a touch-screen may be inaccessible to someone with motor limitations. Build redundant formats rather than one “standard” message.

## Pair sound with text/visual cues
Important household alerts should have a visual or written version where practical. Use large high-contrast text and short action phrases. For someone who uses sign language or an interpreter, store contact pathways and a written fallback. Do not assume lip-reading is reliable in darkness, smoke, masks or noisy rooms.

## Keep communication aids charged and identifiable
Hearing-device batteries, charging cables, speech devices, magnifiers, glasses and communication boards belong in the same continuity inventory as phones. Mark proprietary chargers and keep backups where possible. A generic USB cable does not solve every assistive-device power requirement.

## Simplify without removing autonomy
Use one instruction at a time when a person benefits from plain-language communication, but speak to the person directly rather than only to a caregiver. Confirm understanding through teach-back or a familiar response method. Do not equate a communication disability with inability to make decisions.

## Include emergency services information
Record how the person communicates location, medical/accessibility needs and preferred method. In Greece, 112 is a core emergency pathway and mass-alert system; follow current 112 capabilities/instructions for accessible contact methods rather than relying on an old screenshot.

## Test under realistic conditions
Run a drill with lights off, hearing devices charging, background noise or gloves that reduce dexterity—without intentionally disabling essential equipment. The purpose is to find which message format survives the conditions.
