# Food exposed to floodwater or stormwater

## Floodwater changes the discard threshold
Floodwater can contain sewage, chemicals, fuel, soil, and debris. Food that has directly contacted floodwater should be treated much more conservatively than food affected only by a power outage. Porous packaging such as cardboard, paper, cloth, twist caps, snap lids, screw tops, and many home containers cannot be reliably disinfected once contaminated water reaches them. Follow current public-health guidance rather than trying to “save” food by washing the outside.

## Separate the kitchen into three decisions
Create three groups: clearly protected food, food requiring official container-specific evaluation, and food to discard. Do not let leaking or wet packages remain beside accepted food. If a refrigerator or pantry was flooded, photograph the condition before cleanup if it is safe to do so, then remove damaged material without spreading contaminated water through the clean kitchen.

## Canned goods need container-specific guidance
Commercially sealed metal cans that are undamaged may sometimes be recoverable through a specific cleaning and sanitizing process described by public-health authorities. Bulging, leaking, badly dented, rusted, or damaged cans are different. Home-canned jars and containers with non-waterproof closures generally require more conservative decisions. Do not invent a cleanup process when the official guidance distinguishes packaging types.

## Food-contact surfaces matter too
Floodwater on a worktop, utensil drawer, cutting board, or appliance can contaminate later food even after the original groceries are removed. Clean and disinfect hard food-contact surfaces according to public-health instructions and discard porous items that cannot be effectively cleaned where guidance requires it. Keep children and pets out of the cleanup zone.

## Record what was lost
A simple list of discarded food, photos, and purchase evidence can help with insurance, assistance, or household resupply planning. The Recovery Cost ledger can store the financial impact without keeping contaminated items as “proof.”
