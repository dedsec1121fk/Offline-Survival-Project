# Vehicle Idling Near Shelters: Carbon Monoxide and Air-Intake Boundaries

Vehicles are often used during outages for charging, heating/cooling, radio monitoring, or temporary refuge. That makes exhaust management part of shelter safety. An engine running near a garage, tent, doorway, open window, or air intake can introduce carbon monoxide into occupied space.

## Keep combustion outside the shelter envelope
Never idle a vehicle in an attached garage or enclosed/partly enclosed structure, even with a door open. Position running vehicles so exhaust cannot enter occupied buildings, tents, or temporary shelters through windows, vents, or doors. Wind direction can change, so “the exhaust is blowing away” is not a permanent control.

## Separate charging trips from living space
If a vehicle is used to recharge phones or batteries, assign a specific outdoor location and an operator who remains alert to fuel, exhaust, traffic, and theft hazards. Do not create a sleeping arrangement around a running vehicle. If the car is used as a temporary climate refuge, follow manufacturer and public-safety guidance and reassess whenever snow, debris, floodwater, or other obstruction could affect exhaust flow.

## Log fuel and engine use
Track start/stop times, fuel reserve, charging purpose, and which devices were charged. This supports both resource planning and detection of excessive engine use that may create unnecessary exhaust exposure.
