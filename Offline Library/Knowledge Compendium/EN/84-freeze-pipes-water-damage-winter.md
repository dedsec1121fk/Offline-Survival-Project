# Freeze, pipes and winter water damage: prevent secondary flooding during cold outages

## Loss of heat can become a plumbing incident
Prolonged freezing conditions may affect exposed plumbing, especially in unheated or poorly insulated areas. Know which parts of the property are vulnerable and who is qualified to manage them. Follow local building/utility guidance for winterization rather than improvising with open flames or unsafe heaters.

## Watch for reduced flow and visible frost carefully
A change in water flow can indicate a developing problem, but do not heat a pipe with a torch or other open flame. Keep combustible heating devices away from building materials. If a pipe location is hidden, inaccessible or near electrical hazards, use a professional.

## A frozen pipe can leak when it thaws
The absence of water on the floor while everything is frozen does not prove the pipe is intact. Know the safe water shutoff location and inspect during thaw/restoration. Be ready to isolate the water if a leak appears, provided access is safe and the household knows the correct control.

## Protect drinking-water continuity separately
A plumbing failure may cut normal water even when the utility is functioning. Maintain stored potable water and do not drink water contaminated by contact with building materials, floodwater or chemicals. If utility pressure is lost or an advisory is issued, follow the official water guidance.

## Dry building materials promptly after a leak
Once safe, remove standing water and begin drying according to cleanup guidance to reduce mold/moisture damage. Electrical systems exposed to water require qualified assessment. Photograph damage before major removal when safe.

## Learn from the cold spots
After recovery, record the section that froze, outdoor/indoor conditions and which protective measures failed. Improve insulation, heating continuity or shutoff/winterization planning during normal weather rather than waiting for the next freeze.
