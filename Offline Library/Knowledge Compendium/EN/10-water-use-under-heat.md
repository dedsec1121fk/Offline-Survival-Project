# Water planning during extreme heat and physical work

## Heat changes both demand and risk
A household water plan based on mild indoor conditions can fail quickly during a heatwave, evacuation on foot, cleanup work, or a power outage without cooling. CDC emergency-water guidance explicitly notes that hot climates and some health conditions can require more than the basic reserve. Increase the protected supply before high-temperature periods instead of waiting until people are already dehydrated and transport is difficult.

## Separate hydration planning from rationing pressure
Do not create a rigid “everyone gets the same bottle” rule. Age, pregnancy, illness, medications, acclimatisation, work intensity, and heat exposure can change needs. Use the project to track stock and consumption while leaving individual medical requirements to qualified advice. If the available reserve cannot support safe conditions, reducing heat exposure, stopping heavy work, moving to a cooling location, or evacuating can be more important than stretching the water mathematically.

## Work-rest-water coordination
Schedule physically demanding tasks for cooler periods where possible. Put drinking water at the work area rather than forcing repeated long trips, and assign a second person to notice confusion, weakness, unusual behaviour, or loss of coordination. Heat illness can impair judgement before the person recognises their own decline. The operational plan should therefore connect weather/temperature, task duration, check-ins, and remaining water.

## Protect the reserve from contamination
In hot conditions people drink more frequently, which increases handling. Use personal cups or bottles, avoid mouth contact with the main dispensing tap, and keep refill points shaded and clean. Do not leave emergency drinking water in a vehicle where extreme heat can damage containers or make access impossible during evacuation.

## Escalation
Confusion, collapse, loss of consciousness, seizures, or suspected heat stroke are medical emergencies. Move away from heat and activate emergency medical help; do not let an inventory calculation delay care. For Greece and the EU, 112 provides emergency access, while local heat instructions from civil-protection and health authorities take precedence over generic planning guidance.
