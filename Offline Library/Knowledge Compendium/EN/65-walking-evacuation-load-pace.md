# Walking evacuation: match load, pace and route to the slowest critical need

## Walking is a system, not a fitness test
A route that looks short on a map can become demanding with heat, smoke, rain, stairs, children, luggage or mobility limitations. Estimate distance and elevation, then test representative sections before an emergency. Do not base the plan on the fastest adult’s pace.

## Reduce load before increasing effort
Carry water, essential medicines, identification, communication, weather protection and only the equipment needed for the destination/route. Remove duplicate comfort items before overloading a child, older adult or caregiver. If a mobility aid is required, ensure the route surface and width actually permit it.

## Set checkpoints
Use recognizable locations where the group stops, accounts for people, checks feet, drinks, reviews official information and decides whether the next leg remains safe. A checkpoint is also a reunification point if the group becomes separated.

## Time the route against hazard movement
Wildfire, flood, extreme heat or severe weather may close the walking route faster than the household can travel. Leave according to official warnings and pre-agreed triggers, not after the group is fully packed. If evacuation on foot is the backup, it must be activated before vehicle routes fail completely.

## Protect communications energy
One device should remain in reserve rather than every phone running navigation continuously. Use offline route notes, street names, landmarks and coordinates. Send concise status updates at planned points instead of maintaining a continuous call.

## Stop when walking becomes unsafe
Severe heat/cold stress, worsening smoke, rising water, lightning exposure, injury or loss of route integrity require a new decision. Continuing because “we already started” is not a safety strategy. Move to the nearest safer option and seek official assistance when necessary.
