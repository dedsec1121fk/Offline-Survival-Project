# Neighborhood Welfare Checks: Safety, Consent, and Privacy

After a long outage, heat event, storm, earthquake, or evacuation warning, a structured welfare check can find problems before they become emergencies. It can also expose volunteers to unsafe buildings, aggressive animals, conflict, medical information, or personal details that should not be spread. The system needs boundaries as well as goodwill.

## Define who is actually being checked
Use an agreed list of households or people who opted into mutual aid where possible. Prioritize known vulnerabilities such as power-dependent medical equipment, mobility limits, people living alone, communication barriers, or lack of transport. Do not publicly label people by diagnosis or disability.

## Use pairs and a check-in window
Avoid sending one volunteer alone into uncertain areas. Record departure, intended route, expected return and a contact method. Welfare checkers should not enter visibly unsafe structures, cross official exclusion lines, handle weapons, or force entry unless acting within lawful emergency authority and appropriate training.

## Ask practical questions
Can everyone be accounted for? Is there safe drinking water, essential medication, tolerable temperature, working mobility equipment, a safe toilet method, and a way to receive official warnings? Does the household want assistance or merely want its status logged? Observe without turning the visit into an interrogation.

## Escalate rather than improvise
Chest pain, severe breathing difficulty, altered consciousness, uncontrolled bleeding, serious heat illness, suspected carbon monoxide exposure, fire/gas/electrical hazards, or inability to safely remain in the building should trigger emergency or professional help. Volunteers should not invent treatment plans or repair dangerous utilities.

## Minimize the record
Store only what the coordination team needs: time, location, status category, requested assistance, responsible follow-up and next check. Keep sensitive medical or identity details off public lists. Destroy temporary paper notes when their purpose is complete if local policy permits.
