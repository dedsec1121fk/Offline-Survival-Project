# Candles and Open-Flame Lighting During Blackouts

A candle solves darkness by introducing an ignition source. During outages people are more likely to fall asleep in unusual rooms, move bedding, stage bags by exits, use oxygen, charge batteries, or cook nearby—exactly the conditions that make unattended flame more dangerous.

## Prefer electric light
Use LED flashlights, headlamps, lanterns, and low-power area lights as the primary outage lighting. Store them where they can be reached in the dark and keep spare batteries/charging plans distributed so one failed device does not force a switch to open flame.

## If a candle is used despite safer alternatives
Place it on a stable, noncombustible surface well away from curtains, bedding, paper, decorations, fuel, and the edge of furniture. Keep it out of reach of children and pets. Never carry a lit candle while searching cluttered rooms, and never leave it burning while people sleep or leave the room.

## Oxygen and fuel change the rule
Do not use candles/open flame near medical oxygen or in areas where fuel/LPG/solvent vapors may be present. A suspected gas leak makes ignition sources unacceptable. Do not use a candle to inspect a fuel tank, battery compartment, gas appliance, or damaged utility space.

## Build a blackout-light map
Assign a light to each sleeping zone, toilet route, exit, medical equipment area, and utility shutoff location. Photoluminescent or reflective markers can make stairs/doors easier to find without continuous power. Keep one headlamp with the first-aid/medical kit so both hands remain free.
