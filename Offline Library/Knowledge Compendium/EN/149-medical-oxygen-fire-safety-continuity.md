# Medical Oxygen: Fire-Safe Continuity During Disruption

Medical oxygen supports care but changes the fire environment because oxygen-enriched materials ignite more easily and burn more intensely. During an outage, the temptation to add candles, portable heaters, cooking flames, generators, or improvised charging near the patient can increase the danger.

## Build an oxygen-safe zone
Keep cylinders and oxygen equipment away from open flames, smoking, heat sources, and activities that can create sparks. U.S. Fire Administration guidance specifically warns against flames near medical oxygen. Maintain the route to exits and do not surround the equipment with bags, bedding, paper supplies, or fuel containers during evacuation staging.

## Continuity is more than cylinder count
Record supplier, device type, flow/settings only as already prescribed, cylinder/concentrator inventory, power requirement, backup duration, transport method, and the next resupply contact. Do not change prescribed oxygen settings to “make it last longer.” If a concentrator requires electricity, connect its power plan to the relocation trigger for power-dependent devices.

## Treat fire or heat exposure as an emergency
Do not use cylinders or regulators that have been damaged, involved in fire, contaminated, or exposed in a way the supplier considers unsafe. If a fire starts, evacuation takes priority; household members should not remain inside to retrieve oxygen equipment. Tell responders that medical oxygen is present and where it is normally stored.
