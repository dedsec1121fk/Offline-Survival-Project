# Downed Power Lines and Electrically Energized Water

Storm, flood, fire, and earthquake damage can place electrical conductors where people do not expect them. A line lying on the ground, a tree, fence, roof, or vehicle may still be energized. Floodwater can also become electrically hazardous through damaged systems. The safe household action is distance and reporting, not testing whether a line is live.

## Treat the area as a no-entry zone
Keep people and animals away from downed wires, damaged poles, transformers, service drops, and anything they are touching. Do not move a line with wood, rope, tools, or a vehicle. Do not enter water near damaged electrical infrastructure. Ready.gov advises avoiding downed utility lines and reporting them immediately; CDC likewise warns against entering water to access electrical controls.

## Route evacuation around the hazard
If the normal exit crosses a damaged line or energized water, use the alternate route and tell arriving responders exactly where the hazard is. Keep children and bystanders from approaching for photographs or cleanup. If a wire contacts a vehicle, follow emergency-service/utility instructions; leaving the vehicle without a specific life-threatening reason can introduce a dangerous path to ground.

## Re-entry requires utility/electrical clearance
A line being reattached does not automatically mean the household electrical system is safe. Fire, water, or impact can damage the service entrance, panel, wiring, grounding, and connected appliances. Record what was damaged and do not re-energize questionable circuits until qualified inspection.
