# Extreme-Heat Stranded Vehicle: Shade, Water, and Rescue

A disabled vehicle in extreme heat can become dangerously hot, especially for children, older adults, animals and people with chronic illness. Do not treat the vehicle cabin as a safe waiting room just because it blocks the sun.

## Get help early
Share exact location, road direction, vehicle description and number/condition of occupants. If the vehicle is in a dangerous traffic position, prioritize traffic safety while waiting for professional help. Do not send a vulnerable person walking along an exposed road toward an uncertain destination.

## Reduce heat load
Use safe shade outside the vehicle when traffic/environment make it appropriate, or improve shading/airflow without entering hazardous terrain. Keep direct sun off people and minimize exertion. Use available safe drinking water according to individual medical needs and watch for worsening heat illness.

## Never leave a person or animal in a closed hot vehicle
Cabin temperature can rise rapidly. If the air conditioning stops, reassess immediately rather than relying on “just a few minutes.” Follow local emergency guidance when a child/person/animal is trapped.

## Walking is a deliberate decision
Distance signs are not proof that the route has shade, water or an open facility. Walking in extreme heat increases water demand and can turn a vehicle problem into a medical emergency. Move only when the route and destination are known and safer than remaining.
