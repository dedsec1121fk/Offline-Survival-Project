# Cold exposure: hypothermia and frostbite boundaries

## Treat cold as a system problem
Dangerous cold exposure is not limited to snow. Wind, wet clothing, exhaustion, inadequate food, immobility, an unheated home, or a person who cannot communicate discomfort can create risk well above freezing. A household plan should therefore track room temperature, dry clothing, bedding, safe heat sources, calories, medications, mobility needs, and who is checking vulnerable people. Do not wait for a dramatic temperature reading before acting on a person who is becoming confused, unusually sleepy, clumsy, or unable to care for themselves.

## Suspected hypothermia
Move the person away from continued cold and wet exposure when it is safe to do so. Replace wet layers with dry insulation and protect them from wind and cold surfaces. Handle a severely cold person gently and prioritize the trunk rather than aggressively heating hands and feet. A person with altered mental status, severe shivering that stops, marked weakness, slowed responsiveness, or other concerning symptoms needs urgent professional assessment. Do not use alcohol as a warming strategy and do not assume that a person who says they are “fine” is reliable if their thinking is impaired.

## Frostbite concerns
Frostbite can affect exposed or poorly perfused tissue, especially fingers, toes, ears, nose and cheeks. Protect suspected frozen tissue from further freezing and from rubbing, pressure or direct high heat. Rewarming tissue that may freeze again can worsen injury, so remote situations require a conservative evacuation decision rather than improvised treatment. Remove constricting jewelry when swelling is possible and seek medical care for suspected deep injury, blistering, loss of sensation, or abnormal skin colour that does not resolve.

## Indoor cold continuity
During a long outage, consolidate people into the safest room rather than trying to heat an entire building. Use layers, dry bedding and room zoning; keep combustion appliances outside their intended use. Never turn a grill, generator or other outdoor fuel-burning equipment into an indoor heater. Carbon-monoxide risk can rise precisely when households are cold and improvising.

## Decision record
Record when symptoms were first noticed, environmental conditions, wet exposure, warming actions, medications or conditions that may affect temperature regulation, and whether symptoms improve or worsen. This helps handover to responders and prevents the next shift from restarting the assessment.
