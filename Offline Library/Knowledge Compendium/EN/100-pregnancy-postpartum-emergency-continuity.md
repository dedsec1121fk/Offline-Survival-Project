# Pregnancy and postpartum emergency continuity: keep care, transport and infant needs connected

## Pregnancy changes evacuation and care logistics
Prenatal appointments, prescribed medicines, mobility, hydration, temperature exposure and transport needs may change across pregnancy. Keep the maternity/clinical contact, expected care location, due-date information and any clinician-defined warning signs with the medical continuity card.

## Plan more than one route to care
Know the usual facility and an alternative if the normal route or hospital is unavailable. For island/rural settings, transport disruption may justify earlier contact with the care team. Emergency services should be used for urgent symptoms according to clinical guidance; the project does not determine obstetric triage.

## Protect medication and records
Carry the current medicine list, allergies, relevant pregnancy/postpartum information and identification required by the healthcare system. Do not change prescribed medicines because of heat, evacuation or supply problems without professional advice.

## Postpartum plans include the baby and the recovering parent
Safe water, hygiene, infant feeding, temperature control, sleep and follow-up care all become linked. If breastfeeding/expressing milk, use current CDC/health guidance during power and water outages. If formula feeding, emergency-safe preparation depends on safe water and clean feeding equipment; ready-to-feed options may simplify some emergency situations when appropriate and available.

## Protect privacy and physical support
Shared sheltering should provide access to a private feeding/changing area when possible, sanitation and a safe place to rest. Identify a second caregiver who can carry supplies or assist if the recovering parent cannot manage the normal load.

## Escalate concerns promptly
The person should know the warning signs and contact pathway given by their maternity/medical team for pregnancy and postpartum complications. Severe or concerning symptoms require professional care, not troubleshooting by a preparedness manual.
