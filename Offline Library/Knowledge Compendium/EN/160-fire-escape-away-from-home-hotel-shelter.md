# Fire Escape When Away From Home: Hotel, Shelter, or Temporary Accommodation

Temporary accommodation creates an unfamiliar escape problem. People may not know which corridor leads outside, whether a second staircase exists, where alarms are, or how many doors separate the room from the exit. This uncertainty is greatest at night, when smoke or power loss can remove visual landmarks.

## Orient before sleeping
On arrival, locate the two nearest usable exits where available, count doors or major landmarks between the sleeping area and the exit, identify alarm points, and check that mobility devices can pass the route. U.S. Fire Administration guidance for hotels recommends identifying the two closest exits and counting doors so the route can be followed when visibility is poor.

## Keep the exit kit small
Shoes, identification, essential medicine, mobility/communication aids, and a light should be reachable without unpacking the whole bag. Do not obstruct doors or corridors with luggage. Agree on an outside meeting point so household members do not re-enter looking for someone who already escaped by another route.

## Follow building instructions during an alarm
Use stairs rather than elevators unless responders/building emergency procedures specifically direct otherwise. If smoke or fire blocks the planned route, use the alternate route rather than forcing through. Report missing or mobility-limited occupants to responders; do not send an unprotected family member back inside.
