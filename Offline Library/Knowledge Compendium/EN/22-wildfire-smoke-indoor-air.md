# Wildfire smoke: creating a cleaner indoor air zone

## Separate flame threat from smoke threat
A wildfire can be far enough away that immediate flame evacuation is not required while smoke still makes outdoor air hazardous. Treat these as different decisions. Follow evacuation instructions first; if authorities do not require departure, manage indoor exposure deliberately rather than repeatedly opening the home to “check the air.”

## Build one cleaner-air room
Choose a room that can be closed, has enough space for household members, and does not contain obvious combustion sources. Close windows and doors when outdoor smoke is worse. If the building has HVAC that can safely recirculate indoor air, use the recirculation setting and the best compatible filter recommended for the system. A correctly sized portable air cleaner can add protection when available. Avoid creating indoor particles by smoking, burning candles or incense, frying foods heavily, vacuuming without suitable filtration, or running fuel-burning devices.

## People who need earlier action
Smoke can affect anyone, but households should identify people with asthma, chronic lung or heart disease, pregnancy, children, older adults and others whose clinician has given an action plan. Keep prescribed medicines and written action instructions immediately accessible. New or worsening breathing difficulty, chest pain, severe symptoms or symptoms that do not improve are reasons to seek medical help rather than simply remaining in the cleaner-air room.

## Leaving the clean zone
If evacuation or essential travel is necessary, minimize time in smoke and follow current public-health guidance on appropriate respiratory protection. A loose cloth or ordinary dust mask should not be treated as equivalent to a properly fitting particulate respirator. Do not let a respirator delay evacuation from fire, heat or structural danger.

## Power failure complication
An outage may disable HVAC and air cleaners. The plan should include a low-power fan/cleaner strategy only if it can be operated safely, a charged backup power source, and an alternative cleaner location such as a community facility if local authorities identify one. Never bring a generator indoors to keep an air cleaner running.
