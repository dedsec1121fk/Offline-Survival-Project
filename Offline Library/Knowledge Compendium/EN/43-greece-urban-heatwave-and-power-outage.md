# Greece: urban heatwave with power interruption

## Heat and outage amplify each other
A hot apartment may become unsafe faster when air conditioning, fans, lifts, water pumps or refrigeration fail. Greek Civil Protection advises staying in cool/shaded places and avoiding heavy exertion during heatwaves. The household plan should therefore identify a cooler fallback location before the outage, not after indoor temperature has already climbed.

## Monitor people, not only temperature
Older adults, infants, pregnant people, those with chronic disease and people taking certain medicines may need closer monitoring. Use direct check-ins and look for changes in alertness, coordination, ability to drink, urination and usual behaviour. Heat stroke is a medical emergency; severe confusion, loss of consciousness or other alarming symptoms require immediate emergency response.

## Apartment tactics
Reduce solar gain using shutters/shades where appropriate, move activity to the coolest safe rooms, reduce heat-producing appliances and schedule necessary tasks for cooler periods. Ventilation decisions should reflect actual outdoor temperature, smoke and air-quality conditions; opening everything at midday can add heat, while wildfire smoke may make outdoor air undesirable even at night.

## Water and lifts
Keep drinking water accessible on each occupied level if building pumps are unreliable. A person who depends on a lift should have an alternate plan before the power goes out. If evacuation to a cooling location is needed, leave while transport and mobility remain manageable rather than waiting for severe symptoms.

## Medicines and food
Some medicines and medical supplies have storage-temperature requirements; follow pharmacy/manufacturer/clinician instructions if refrigeration or air conditioning is lost. Apply the separate food-outage rules to the refrigerator/freezer rather than repeatedly opening doors.
