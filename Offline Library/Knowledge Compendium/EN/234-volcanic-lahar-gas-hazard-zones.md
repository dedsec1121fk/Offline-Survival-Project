# Volcanic Hazards: Lahars, Gas, Ash, and Exclusion Zones

Volcano emergencies are multi-hazard events. Ash, fast volcanic flows, lahars, toxic gases, earthquakes, fires and disrupted roads can affect different areas at different times. A single rule such as “move uphill” is not enough; use official hazard zones and event-specific instructions.

## Know the local hazard map before unrest
If living or travelling near an active or potentially active volcano, save the official hazard map offline. Mark valleys/drainages that can channel lahars, official evacuation routes, high-ground alternatives, and locations outside mapped exclusion zones. A lahar can travel far downstream from the volcano, including during or after heavy rain.

## Lahars: leave channels and low ground
If authorities warn of a lahar or you observe a rapidly rising muddy flow/roaring in a volcanic drainage, move promptly to high ground away from the channel rather than trying to outrun the flow along a valley road. Bridges can become choke points or fail.

## Volcanic gases are not solved by a dust mask
Respirators used for ash particles do not make an unknown toxic-gas atmosphere safe. Heed exclusion zones and gas warnings, especially in depressions where some gases can accumulate. Do not enter closed valleys, craters or restricted zones to collect photographs or belongings.

## Ash management
During heavy ash fall, reduce driving, protect eyes/airways according to official guidance, close openings where practical, protect sensitive electronics and water containers, and avoid overloading roofs during substantial accumulation. Cleanup should minimize re-suspending fine dust.

## Re-entry is hazard-specific
Road reopening does not automatically mean water supplies, roofs, electrical systems or valleys are safe. Follow geological observatory and civil-protection instructions as conditions evolve.
