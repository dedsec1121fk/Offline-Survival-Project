# Resource movement log

## Objective
Inventory answers “what do we have?” A movement log answers “what changed?” Keeping those questions separate makes it easier to see whether a plan is consuming supplies faster than expected or whether losses and transfers are being forgotten.

## What to record
Create an entry when a critical resource is used, restocked, lost or discarded, or transferred to another location or group. Record the measured amount and unit you actually observed. Add enough context to explain the movement later, such as “radio charging,” “moved to vehicle kit,” or “container damaged.”

Do not turn this log into an invented rationing standard. Water, food, medicine, fuel, battery capacity, and hygiene needs vary with people, conditions, equipment, and official guidance. The useful value is the record of what really moved.

## Reading the log
Look for repeated use entries, unplanned losses, resources that move between locations often, and items that are restocked only after reaching zero. Compare the pattern with your inventory and continuity plans. A discrepancy is a prompt to recount or correct the record, not proof that material is missing.

## Handover use
During a shift or household handover, summarize the largest changes since the previous check and identify any resource whose location is now uncertain. This prevents the next person from planning with yesterday’s quantities.
