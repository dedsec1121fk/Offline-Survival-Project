# Service restoration priorities

## Purpose
After the immediate danger has passed, many tasks compete for attention. A restoration order prevents convenience work from displacing actions that protect people, preserve essential capability, or stop further loss.

## Build the order before the incident
List the services your household or small group depends on: safe shelter functions, communication, essential care routines, water access, food protection, sanitation, transport, document access, and other locally relevant capabilities. For each, note what happens if restoration is delayed and what must be verified before use resumes.

## Triage after disruption
First identify conditions that require official or professional clearance. Then separate tasks into: essential now, stabilizing next, useful later, and cosmetic. A service that appears restored may still be unsafe or unreliable; “available” and “safe to use” are not the same status.

## Coordination
Assign one owner to confirm each restored capability and record the evidence used: official notice, successful safe check, verified replacement, or qualified assessment. Keep unavailable systems isolated when required and prevent multiple people from independently changing the same setup.

## End state
Recovery is not complete when power, water, connectivity, or access simply returns. Update inventories, close temporary workarounds, recharge or replace used backups, capture damage and cost records, and revise the dependency map while the failure is still fresh.
