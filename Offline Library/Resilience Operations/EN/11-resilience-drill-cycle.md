# Resilience drill cycle

## Drill one assumption at a time
A useful resilience drill does not need theatrical disaster simulation. Choose one assumption, temporarily pretend it is unavailable, and observe how the plan behaves. Examples include a normal meeting point being inaccessible, the primary charging method being unavailable, or one usual communicator being absent.

## Prepare
Define the start condition, safe boundaries, expected duration, and stop conditions. Tell participants what is real and what is simulated. Never disable a real safety system, obstruct an emergency exit, withhold needed medication, create hazardous electrical conditions, or interfere with official services for the sake of a drill.

## Observe
Watch for delays, hidden dependencies, ambiguous responsibilities, inaccessible instructions, duplicated work, and communication gaps. Record facts rather than grading people. A drill is testing the system first.

## Debrief quickly
Within the same day if possible, ask what was confusing, what backup actually worked, what information was missing, and what action created unnecessary effort. Convert only the strongest findings into changes.

## Repeat with variation
After fixing a weakness, retest it under a slightly different but still safe constraint. Over time, rotate through communication, access, transport, resource location, handover, and recovery assumptions so preparedness becomes demonstrated capability rather than a static checklist.
