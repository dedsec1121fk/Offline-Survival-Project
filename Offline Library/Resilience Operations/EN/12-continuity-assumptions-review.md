# Continuity assumptions review

## Find the sentences hiding inside the plan
Every continuity plan contains assumptions even when they are not written down: “we will all be home,” “the car will be available,” “the phone list will be reachable,” “this person knows the procedure,” or “the alternate route will still be open.” Writing those assumptions down makes them testable.

## Classify assumptions
Mark each assumption as verified, plausible but unverified, changing, or fragile. Add the evidence and the date it was last checked. An assumption about a seasonal road, a person’s availability, a subscription, stored credentials, or a device battery can expire even when the document containing it looks current.

## Convert fragile assumptions
For a fragile assumption, choose one response: verify it, create an independent fallback, remove the dependency, or explicitly accept the limitation. Avoid adding multiple layers of planning around an assumption nobody has checked.

## Use event-driven reviews
Do not wait only for an annual review. Revisit assumptions after moving home, changing a vehicle, changing a caregiver, replacing communications equipment, adding a pet or dependent, changing work schedules, or discovering a failure during a drill.

## Keep the plan readable
Archive obsolete assumptions rather than letting them compete with current information. The aim is not to prove the plan can handle every possible event; it is to know which parts of the plan depend on facts that may no longer be true.
