# Action trigger matrix

## Why triggers matter
Plans often fail at the transition between “watching” and “acting.” A trigger matrix converts vague phrases such as “if conditions get worse” into an observable signal, a condition, a pre-agreed action, an owner, and a review time.

## Design a trigger
Choose a signal that can be checked without entering danger. Prefer official warnings, loss of a required service, a confirmed route closure, a missed accountability check, a measured resource state, or another clear observation. Describe the threshold in ordinary language so another person can apply it without guessing.

Write the action before the threshold is met. The action can be as simple as switching to a backup communication method, moving a kit near an exit, contacting a designated person, or following an official evacuation instruction. Assign one owner and a review time when the condition is not yet met.

## During an incident
Keep four states: watching, met, action taken, and closed. When a trigger is met, record what was observed and whether the planned response is still safe and lawful. If official guidance conflicts with a pre-written household trigger, official emergency instructions take priority.

## Debrief
Afterward, check whether the signal was observable early enough, whether the wording caused hesitation, and whether the action relied on an unavailable dependency. Rewrite only the parts that failed in practice.
