# Resource substitution map

## Goal
A substitute map records what can replace a resource without assuming that every alternative is equivalent. It is especially useful when the primary item is unavailable but the underlying need still exists.

## Start from the function
Write the function first, not the product name. “Provide light for a short indoor task,” “carry essential documents,” “communicate a check-in,” and “keep a device powered” are easier to substitute than a specific brand or object.

For each function, list possible alternatives already available to you. Then record the constraint that makes an alternative unsuitable: incompatible connector, different fuel, limited duration, inaccessible storage location, requires another person, cannot be used safely indoors, or depends on a network that may be down.

## Avoid false equivalence
Do not substitute medicine, protective equipment, food for a medically restricted person, water treatment, electrical components, fuel systems, or other safety-sensitive items based only on superficial similarity. Where compatibility or professional guidance matters, mark the entry “verification required” rather than guessing.

## Use in planning
Link viable substitutes to the dependency register and to the location where they are stored. During drills, test only safe operational aspects such as whether people can find the item, understand the instructions, and reach it when the normal storage area is unavailable.
