# Shared equipment control

## Problem
Shared equipment tends to become “someone else’s responsibility.” During disruption, that can mean a radio with no charged battery, a tool moved without notice, a first-aid kit missing an item, or a vehicle key that nobody can locate.

## Custody record
For equipment that matters to multiple people, record its normal location, current custodian, readiness state, and any accessory required for use. When it moves, log the destination and expected return rather than relying on memory.

## Handover
A handover should answer four questions: where is it, is it ready, what changed, and what does the next user need to know? If equipment is damaged or uncertain, mark it unavailable instead of leaving it in the ready pool.

## Access
Keep access arrangements proportionate to the item. Emergency gear should not be locked behind a process that becomes impossible during an outage, while hazardous, regulated, or privacy-sensitive equipment may require controlled access. Do not store secret codes or sensitive credentials in a general shared board.

## Maintenance link
Connect equipment that needs periodic checks to the maintenance schedule. A successful maintenance entry should identify the item and date, but it should not erase its movement history. Separate “where it is” from “when it was serviced.”
