# Staged recovery checkpoints

## Why stages help
Returning to normal too quickly can hide unfinished safety checks, depleted backups, missing records, or temporary workarounds that quietly become permanent. Recovery checkpoints make the transition deliberate.

## Checkpoint 1 — immediate stability
Confirm that people are accounted for, urgent hazards have been escalated, essential care is continuing, and temporary shelter or communication arrangements are understood. Capture major changes while they are still easy to remember.

## Checkpoint 2 — service confidence
Before abandoning backups, verify that restored services are stable enough for your use and that any required official or professional clearance has been received. Keep a fallback available during the observation period when practical.

## Checkpoint 3 — resource reset
Recount critical supplies, recharge power banks, replace consumed kit items, return shared equipment, update medication or document continuity records as appropriate, and close stale mutual-aid requests.

## Checkpoint 4 — learning
Compare what happened with the dependency map, triggers, routes, communication windows, and handover notes. Pick a small number of corrections with owners and dates. Preserve useful logs, then remove personal data that no longer has an operational reason to remain.
