# Water source, treatment and container-traceability manual

## Never let “water” be one undifferentiated stock
A household may simultaneously have sealed commercial water, normal tap water stored before an incident, water collected after a boil notice, treated emergency water, non-potable cleaning water and a questionable container. Give each batch a source, date, container and intended use.

## Protect the decision trail
If water is boiled or otherwise treated according to current official instructions, record the method and time. Do not copy a chemical dose from memory into a generic field; product concentration and official advice can change. EPA emergency-disinfection guidance explains boiling and chemical disinfection methods and should be consulted when that method is actually needed.

## Container discipline
A clean treatment process can be undone by a dirty container, contaminated scoop or hand. Keep drinking-water containers closed and distinguish them physically from greywater or cleaning containers. Label containers so another person does not have to infer which one is safe.

## Volume accounting
Track volume accepted for drinking/cooking separately from water reserved for hygiene or other uses. Ready.gov recommends at least one gallon, roughly four litres, per person per day for several days for drinking and sanitation as a preparedness baseline, while individual needs vary with heat, illness, work, pregnancy, children, animals and other factors.

## Source changes
Flooding, pipe breaks, unusual odor/color, official advisories or a flooded well can change the status of a source that was safe yesterday. Do not mix new questionable water into a trusted stored batch. Isolate it until the correct official instruction is known.

## Handover
At shift change, report accepted volume, uncertain volume, containers awaiting treatment or cleaning, the next replenishment source and any official water notice. This makes water status a controlled inventory rather than a guess.
