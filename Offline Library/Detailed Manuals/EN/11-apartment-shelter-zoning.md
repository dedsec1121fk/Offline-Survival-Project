# Apartment shelter-zoning manual

## Think in zones, not rooms
A room can be usable for one purpose and unsuitable for another. Map sleeping, clean food/water, charging, cooking, waste, medical/accessibility support, animal area, smoke isolation and damaged/avoid zones separately. The Shelter board records operational use; it is not a structural inspection.

## Interior migration
During heat, smoke, broken windows or localized damage, the best operating area may move. Record the current primary sleeping area and a fallback. Keep evacuation paths free even if furniture is moved to create a cleaner or cooler zone. Do not create a barricade that prevents rapid exit.

## Vertical-building dependencies
Apartment residents depend on common stairs, lifts, pumps, entrance doors, electronic locks, garage gates and shared utilities. A private apartment can be intact while the building’s access or water system fails. Add common-area observations to the recovery board and avoid assuming another resident has reported them.

## Charging and fire load
Concentrating all batteries and chargers in one corner simplifies monitoring but can also concentrate heat and fire risk. Use intact equipment, keep charging away from escape paths and flammable clutter, and stop using damaged or swollen batteries. Do not place improvised wiring across a doorway.

## Quiet and accessibility space
Crowding increases conflict, fatigue and sensory overload. If the household includes a person who needs a low-stimulation area, mobility clearance or medical equipment, protect that function early rather than trying to create it after every other room is full.

## Re-check cycle
After an aftershock, water leak, smoke incursion or utility change, re-check zone status. “Usable yesterday” is not permanent. Time-stamped observations are more useful than a single label because they show when the condition was last actually seen.
