# Portable Generator Placement and Operating Boundary

## Treat carbon monoxide as invisible
A portable generator is a combustion engine, not an indoor appliance. Operate it outdoors and well away from occupied spaces, doors, windows and vents, following the manufacturer's instructions and current official guidance. Never move it into a garage, balcony enclosure, shed attached to living space or other partly enclosed area simply to protect it from weather.

## Plan the site before the outage
Choose a candidate outdoor location while you still have time to examine exhaust direction, cable routing, rain protection that does not enclose the unit, fuel storage and access for refueling. The route from the generator to the load should not create trip hazards, crushed cables or improvised connections through wet areas.

## Keep electrical limits explicit
Record rated continuous output, startup limitations and which loads are allowed. Do not treat the sum of appliance labels as proof that an installation is safe. Building connection, transfer equipment, grounding and protection can be location-specific electrical matters that require qualified guidance.

## Fuel and hot-surface discipline
Shut down and allow equipment to cool according to its instructions before refueling. Keep fuel containers appropriate for the fuel, closed, labeled and away from ignition sources and occupied rooms. Record fuel use in the power plan so a generator is not run merely because fuel happens to be available.

## Detection and symptoms
Maintain working carbon-monoxide alarms where recommended for the building. If anyone develops symptoms that could indicate exposure, move to fresh air and seek emergency guidance rather than trying to diagnose the problem from the dashboard.

## Handover items
A generator shift handover should state runtime, estimated fuel remaining, next check, active loads, any unusual noise or fault, and who is responsible. Stop using damaged or suspect equipment until it has been assessed appropriately.
