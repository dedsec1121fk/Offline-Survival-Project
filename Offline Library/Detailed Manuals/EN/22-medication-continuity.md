# Medication and Medical-Equipment Continuity

## Build an identification layer
Keep an accurate medication and medical-equipment list that a second responsible person can understand. Record the medicine or device name, prescribed purpose if useful, normal schedule as written by the clinician, prescriber/pharmacy contact, refill date and any storage instruction printed on the label. The project should not invent doses or alter treatment.

## Separate supply from treatment decisions
The continuity register answers operational questions: what is available, where it is, when it may run out, and who can help replace it. Questions about changing dose, substituting a medicine, using a damaged product or handling a temperature excursion belong to a pharmacist, clinician or official emergency-health service.

## Plan powered equipment
For medical equipment that needs electricity, document rated power, battery type, expected runtime from the manufacturer's information, charging method and a backup location or service if one exists. Test the backup under controlled conditions. Do not assume the same inverter or power bank that charges a phone can safely support medical equipment.

## Protect privacy while preserving access
The full medical card can be sensitive. Keep the minimum necessary emergency information accessible to the people who may need it, and protect detailed diagnoses or identifiers from casual access. A printed emergency summary may be more useful than a locked digital file if the primary phone fails.

## Refill before the threshold
Use a review date that gives time for normal refill processes rather than waiting until the last dose or last consumable. During a widespread incident, contact the usual pharmacy or healthcare provider early because transport and supply delays may affect replacement.

## After any interruption
Record what happened, the actual time without normal support, any professional instruction received and what supply was used. That history helps the care team assess continuity without relying on memory after a stressful event.
