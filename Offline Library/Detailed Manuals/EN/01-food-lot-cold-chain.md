# Food lot rotation and cold-chain decision manual

## Purpose
A food store becomes operationally useful only when individual lots can be identified. Record the product, container condition, storage position, opening date, review date, and whether the lot remained inside its intended temperature range. Keep uncertain food physically separate from food already accepted for use so one questionable container does not contaminate the decision about the rest of the pantry.

## Before an outage
Map the refrigerator, freezer, dry pantry, vehicle food box, evacuation food and any medically important nutrition separately. Put a thermometer in cold-storage equipment if available. Photograph or write down freezer contents before a high-risk weather period, because a closed freezer is easier to manage when nobody keeps opening it to inspect individual packages. Store a manual can opener with shelf-stable food rather than in a distant kitchen drawer.

## During loss of refrigeration
Treat door opening as a resource decision. Retrieve several planned items at once, then close the door. Record when power was lost and when reliable cooling returned. CDC and Ready.gov advise discarding perishable food that has not been kept safely cold; appearance and smell cannot prove that food is safe. Floodwater exposure is a separate reason to discard affected food and containers as directed by public-health guidance.

## Rotation logic
Use intact shelf-stable items with the nearest practical review date first, unless a diet, allergy or medical requirement changes priority. Mark opened packages and record what can realistically be consumed before quality declines. Do not mix an isolated lot back into normal stock just because another person thinks it “looks fine.” The decision should have a reason that the next shift can understand.

## Energy accounting
Calories are useful for endurance planning but do not describe nutritional adequacy. Track total energy only as one operational dimension. Also keep notes about infant food, allergy-safe food, texture requirements, pet food, hydration needs and any medically directed diet. The Food Operations module deliberately asks you to choose your own kcal assumption instead of prescribing one.

## Sources to verify when conditions change
Ready.gov food guidance; CDC natural-disaster food safety; local public-health and civil-protection instructions.
