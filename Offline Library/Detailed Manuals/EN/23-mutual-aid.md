# Neighbor Mutual-Aid Boundaries

## Define the relationship before the crisis
Mutual aid works best when expectations are explicit. Discuss what households are willing to share, what remains private, how contact will occur, and which situations justify checking on one another. Do not promise medical, rescue or technical capabilities that nobody actually has.

## Exchange capabilities, not sensitive inventories
It can be useful to know that one household has a battery radio, another has language skills, and another can help move a person with limited mobility. It is usually unnecessary to disclose exact cash, medicine, fuel or valuable-equipment quantities. Share enough information to coordinate without creating a new security or privacy problem.

## Create a simple check protocol
Agree on a time window, primary communication method, fallback and what “no response” means. A missed check should trigger a proportionate follow-up rather than immediate assumptions. During an official evacuation, nobody should remain in danger solely to complete the neighborhood protocol.

## Keep loans traceable
For reusable equipment, note who took it, when, its condition and whether an accessory or charger went with it. For consumables, decide whether the item is a gift or expected replacement. Clear records prevent disputes when everyone is tired and recovering.

## Respect household constraints
Allergies, disability needs, pets, cultural requirements, safeguarding responsibilities and privacy can make a seemingly simple shared solution unsuitable. Ask before moving supplies or people into another household's space.

## End the temporary arrangement cleanly
When normal services return, reconcile borrowed items, remove obsolete contact information, review what worked, and keep only the agreements that households still want. Emergency cooperation should not become an indefinite obligation by accident.
