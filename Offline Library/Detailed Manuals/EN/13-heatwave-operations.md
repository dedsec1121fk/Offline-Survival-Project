# Household heatwave operations manual

## Heat is an endurance problem
A heatwave becomes more dangerous when high temperature combines with poor sleep, medication effects, chronic disease, physical work, power loss, isolation or inability to reach a cooler place. Treat cooling, hydration access, communication and workload as coordinated resources rather than separate tips.

## Map the coolest usable zone
Measure or compare rooms at several times of day. Sun exposure shifts. A room that is cooler in the morning may become the hottest late in the afternoon. Close or shade sun-exposed openings when appropriate, ventilate when outdoor conditions are actually better, and avoid creating an unsafe security or smoke exposure problem while chasing airflow.

## Protect vulnerable people
Older adults, infants and young children, pregnant people, people with chronic disease or disability, and those taking certain medicines may require additional planning. Follow individualized medical advice. A buddy check should confirm actual condition and access to cooling, not merely that a text message was sent.

## Shift physical work
Move non-urgent heavy work away from the hottest periods when possible. Record who is doing outdoor or cleanup work and when they are expected back. Heat illness can impair judgment, so a person may underestimate their own deterioration.

## Power prioritization
If cooling depends on limited battery or generator energy, put fans or other cooling equipment in the Power Operations ledger and compare them with medical, refrigeration and communication loads. The correct tradeoff depends on conditions and people; the ledger exposes the tradeoff instead of making it invisible.

## Escalation
Know the signs that require urgent medical action and use official heat alerts. If the home cannot remain safe, identify accessible cooler locations before the most dangerous period. Do not wait for a vulnerable person to become severely ill before deciding where they could go.
