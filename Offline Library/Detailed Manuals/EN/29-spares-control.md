# Tools, Spares and Repair-Control Ledger

## Know what can actually be repaired
A drawer of miscellaneous parts is not a repair capability. Identify the specific household systems you are prepared to maintain safely—such as lighting, device charging, bicycle punctures or manufacturer-approved filter replacement—and keep the correct tools, parts and instructions together.

## Track compatibility
Record model, connector, size or other compatibility detail for critical spares. Visually similar fuses, chargers, filters, batteries or seals may have important differences. Do not substitute a part in electrical, fuel, pressure, medical or life-safety equipment unless the substitution is approved for that use.

## Preserve complete kits
When a tool is borrowed, note accessories that travel with it. A pump without its adapter, a driver without the correct bit or a radio without its charging lead can turn an apparently complete inventory into a failed repair at the moment it is needed.

## Set a stop rule
Write down work the household will not attempt. Structural, mains-electrical, gas, complex vehicle, hazardous-material and medical-equipment repairs can create greater risk than the original failure. Isolation and professional assistance are valid operational decisions.

## Record repair history
For any consequential repair, capture date, symptom, action, part used and whether the system passed a functional check. If the same failure repeats, treat that as evidence of an unresolved root cause rather than continuing to consume spares.

## Replenish after practice
Training consumes tape, fasteners, patches, batteries and other materials just like a real incident. Refill immediately after drills and update quantities so the readiness picture does not assume the practice kit is still complete.
