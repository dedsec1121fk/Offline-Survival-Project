# Low-Power Communications Discipline

## Spend battery on information, not habit
During a long outage, communication energy should be budgeted like water or fuel. Continuous screen use, repeated network searching and duplicated status calls can consume the same batteries needed later for an evacuation instruction or medical contact.

## Use scheduled windows
Agree on specific local-time windows for routine check-ins. Outside those windows, leave devices in an appropriate low-power state unless monitoring an official alert channel is necessary. A schedule reduces the temptation for every person to keep checking whether service has returned.

## Standardize the message
A useful status message can contain sender, location, condition, immediate need, intended next action and next contact time. Keep private details out of untrusted channels. If the message is relayed by another person, preserving the original time and sender prevents a forwarded update from appearing current when it is old.

## Choose the cheapest successful channel
When networks are congested, a short text may succeed where a voice or video call fails and may use less energy. If radio is part of the plan, follow applicable rules and licensing and practice concise call procedures beforehand. The project does not assign frequencies or authorize radio operation.

## Maintain device rotation
If several devices or power banks exist, rotate them deliberately rather than running all to the same low state. Label which battery is active, reserve or depleted. Keep one communication path in reserve for a change in conditions.

## Log missed windows
A missed contact is an operational event, not proof of an emergency. Record it, try the agreed fallback and apply the household's escalation rule. If immediate danger is known, use emergency services rather than waiting for the next scheduled window.
