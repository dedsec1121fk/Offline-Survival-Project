# Supply Receiving and Intake Control

## Create one receiving point
When friends, relief services or household members bring supplies from different places, uncontrolled unpacking can mix damaged, expired or uncertain items into trusted stock. Choose one receiving surface or area where new material is checked before it enters normal storage.

## Identify the item and condition
Record what arrived, quantity, date, source if relevant, packaging condition and any expiry or review date. For food or medicines, do not assume an item is safe because it was donated or commercially packaged; inspect for damage and follow official or manufacturer guidance for products exposed to heat, floodwater or other hazards.

## Route by status
Move acceptable items into their normal location, mark use-first stock clearly, and isolate anything uncertain until a competent decision can be made. Discarded items should leave the active inventory so summaries do not count unusable material as resilience.

## Avoid accidental duplication
Check the inventory before opening new multipacks or consuming newly arrived stock. Keep older suitable stock in the use-first path where appropriate. This reduces waste and preserves sealed reserve material for later stages of the incident.

## Handle shared supplies transparently
If a delivery belongs to several households, record allocation before the boxes are split. Keep quantities factual rather than relying on memory. A clear distribution note prevents both accidental double claims and the perception that supplies disappeared.

## Close the intake event
After sorting, update totals, remove packaging that creates unnecessary waste, clean the receiving area as appropriate and record any item that changes an operational plan such as new battery capacity, replacement medicine supply or additional potable water.
