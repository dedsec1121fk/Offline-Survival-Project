# Critical Dependency Mapping

## Start with outcomes, not objects
Instead of making another long gear list, identify outcomes the household must preserve: drinkable water, essential communication, temperature control, necessary mobility, medication continuity, lighting, sanitation and safe food handling. Then trace what each outcome depends on.

## Build dependency chains
A refrigerator may depend on grid power, an inverter, a battery bank, cables and ventilation. A communication plan may depend on a charged phone, a working network, remembered credentials and a contact who still has service. Draw these chains explicitly because failure often occurs at a small hidden link rather than the expensive main device.

## Mark single points of failure
Highlight any outcome that has only one method, one person, one connector, one fuel type or one location. Decide whether the best treatment is a spare, a substitute method, a printed instruction, a second trained person or acceptance that the capability will be lost after a defined threshold.

## Track consumables separately
Batteries, filters, fuel, hygiene materials, pet supplies, prescription continuity and printer paper decline with use or time. Connect each consumable to a burn-rate or review date instead of merely marking it “owned.” A dependency map becomes useful when it can answer how long a capability remains available.

## Test substitutions
Do not assume two items are interchangeable because they perform similar functions. Check connectors, voltage, fuel, capacity, physical fit, safe operating environment and user skill before listing an alternative. Run a low-risk practice setup before the emergency whenever possible.

## Review after change
Any new device, household member, medical need, vehicle, move or seasonal hazard can alter the map. Revisit the dependency chain after those changes and after every drill that exposes a hidden requirement.
