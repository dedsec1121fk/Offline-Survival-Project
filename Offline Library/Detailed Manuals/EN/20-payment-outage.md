# Payment and Cash Continuity During Service Disruption

## Plan for partial failure
A payment outage may affect card terminals, mobile data, bank applications or one provider while other services remain available. Avoid building the plan around a single assumption such as “cash always works” or “cards always work.” Maintain several lawful payment options that fit your circumstances.

## Set a household limit
Choose in advance how much emergency spending authority each responsible adult has without further consultation. Separate routine replacement purchases from unusually expensive commitments. This reduces duplicate buying and rushed decisions when communications inside the household are poor.

## Record evidence as you spend
For each recovery expense, note date, supplier, purpose, amount, currency, payment method and whether a receipt or other evidence exists. Photograph or scan receipts when practical, but retain originals when a claim, warranty or public-assistance process may require them. Do not place banking credentials or card security codes in the recovery ledger.

## Keep currencies separate
If travel or evacuation creates spending in more than one currency, total each currency independently. Do not create a misleading combined total unless you deliberately apply a documented exchange rate for a specific accounting purpose.

## Protect physical funds
Store emergency cash discreetly, split across sensible locations and keep only an amount appropriate to your risk and legal context. During evacuation, treat it like other sensitive documents: accessible to the responsible adult but not visible in an unattended bag or vehicle.

## Reconcile after connectivity returns
Compare the local ledger with bank transactions, receipts and reimbursements. Mark duplicated authorizations, pending transactions and refunded items without deleting the original record. A complete timeline is more useful for recovery than a perfectly neat but incomplete final total.
