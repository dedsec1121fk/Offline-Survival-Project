# Accessibility, caregiving and dependency-continuity manual

## Map functions, not labels
Emergency planning is stronger when it records what a person needs to function: mobility assistance, transfer technique, power for equipment, refrigeration, medication continuity, communication format, sensory accommodation, feeding support, supervision, service-animal needs or a predictable low-stimulation space. A diagnosis alone often does not tell a helper what to do.

## Primary and backup support
For each critical task, name the usual person and a backup who has actually practised the task where appropriate. “Someone will help” is not redundancy. If safe transfer requires specific equipment or technique, the backup must know that; improvising a lift during an evacuation can injure both people.

## Equipment dependencies
List chargers, proprietary cables, batteries, filters, consumables, adapters, spare parts and transport cases. Mark what must stay dry, cool or continuously powered. If a device has a manufacturer emergency procedure, store it offline with the equipment rather than relying on a website.

## Communication access
Write how the person best receives urgent information: spoken language, large print, hearing device, sign language, pictograms, simple one-step instructions, text, or a trusted supporter. During high stress, the method that works on an ordinary day may be less effective, so prepare a second method.

## Evacuation reality
Check stairs, lift dependency, doorway width, vehicle transfer, pet/service-animal transport and the destination itself. An evacuation destination is not usable if the bathroom, charging, refrigeration, quiet space or mobility access needed for continuity is absent. Ready.gov specifically advises people with disabilities and caregivers to plan around individual assistance and equipment needs.

## Sensitive information
Store only what an emergency helper needs. Avoid passwords, bank credentials or unrelated private history. A printed or exported continuity card should be protected but also reachable; perfect confidentiality is not useful if nobody can access the information in the emergency it was meant for.
