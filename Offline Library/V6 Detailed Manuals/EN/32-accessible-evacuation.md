# Accessible Evacuation Staging

## Describe the task, not a label
Accessibility planning should record the practical support required: transferring, stairs, walking distance, communication format, sensory environment, battery-powered equipment, medication access or assistance with animals. A diagnostic label alone rarely tells the team what must happen during evacuation.

## Test every transition
Walk through movement from the sleeping area to the exit, from the exit to transport, from transport to the destination and then to toilets, charging and sleeping space at the destination. A route can be technically accessible at one point and unusable at the next because of a step, narrow doorway, dead battery or missing helper.

## Preserve autonomy
Ask the person how they want assistance provided and which equipment must stay with them. Do not separate a mobility aid, communication device, medication, service animal or essential support item merely to make loading easier unless an immediate life-safety situation requires it.

## Create backup support
Name a primary helper and at least one realistic backup. The backup needs enough practice to use straps, chargers, communication methods or other equipment safely; a name on a list is not a trained capability.

## Destination questions
Before choosing a shelter or alternate home, verify the features that actually matter to the individual: physical access, power, refrigeration if medically required, quiet space, bathroom access, transport links, animal policy and ability to communicate with staff.

## Keep the plan current
Changes in mobility, device configuration, medication, caregiver availability or transport can invalidate an old plan. Re-run the staging exercise after those changes instead of assuming the previous route remains suitable.
