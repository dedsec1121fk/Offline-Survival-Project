# Sanitation flow and contamination-boundary manual

## Design the flow before the crisis
Sanitation fails when clean and dirty movement share the same space without rules. Mark drinking-water storage, food preparation, hand washing, toilet use, waste holding, laundry, bathing, chemical storage and any flood-contaminated area as separate functions. A small apartment may use the same room for several functions at different times, but the sequence and cleaning boundary should be explicit.

## Toilet continuity
If sewer service is suspected to be damaged or officially restricted, do not assume flushing is harmless. Record whether each toilet is normal, limited, out of service or awaiting confirmation. Keep the reason visible. An improvised waste method must follow local public-health instructions; the project does not invent universal disposal depths, chemical recipes or treatment doses because these depend on infrastructure, groundwater, local law and the incident.

## Hand hygiene point
A useful hand-washing station needs a clean-water source, a way to dispense water without contaminating the source, soap, drainage or collection for wastewater, and a method to keep the clean and dirty sides distinct. Position it where people naturally pass before food handling and after toilet or waste tasks. Refill responsibility should have an owner and a service time.

## Solid waste
Separate ordinary household waste from sharp objects, chemical containers, contaminated cleanup material and anything authorities classify differently. Heavy bags that tear during transport create a second exposure problem. Record where waste is staged and how long it has accumulated, especially during hot weather.

## Laundry and bathing
Prioritize clothing, bedding or equipment that directly affects hygiene, skin integrity, infant care or contamination control. Do not spend scarce drinking water automatically on low-priority washing. Conversely, postponing every wash can create health and morale problems. Make the tradeoff visible in the sanitation log instead of leaving it to memory.

## Escalation signs
Escalate the plan when toilets back up, wastewater appears where it should not, hand-washing water is unavailable, waste cannot be contained, pests increase, floodwater enters clean areas, or multiple people develop gastrointestinal illness. These are reasons to seek current public-health instructions rather than reasons to improvise more aggressively.
