# Waste Segregation and Temporary Storage

## Decide categories before containers fill
During service disruption, household waste can include ordinary dry refuse, food waste, contaminated cleanup material, broken glass or sharps, pet waste and other locally regulated materials. Keep categories simple enough that everyone can follow them, but separate anything that presents a distinct injury, contamination or legal handling requirement.

## Select a controlled location
Temporary storage should be away from food preparation, drinking-water storage, children, animals and occupied sleeping areas as practical. Containers need to remain stable, closable when appropriate, and protected from spreading material through wind, water or pests.

## Never improvise with dangerous mixtures
Do not combine unknown chemicals, fuel-contaminated material or reactive cleaners merely to save space. Preserve labels when available and follow local disposal instructions. If a container is leaking or producing fumes, isolate people from the area and seek appropriate emergency or hazardous-material guidance.

## Track capacity
A waste point is an operational resource with a finite capacity. Record current state, owner and the next action before it is full. If normal collection is interrupted, reduce unnecessary packaging entering the living area and plan a controlled overflow location rather than creating scattered bags.

## Protect handlers
Use the protective equipment appropriate to the material, avoid compressing bags by hand when contents are uncertain, and maintain hand-cleaning capability near the workflow without contaminating clean-water storage.

## Re-entry and cleanup records
After flooding, fire or structural damage, photograph major waste and damaged property before disposal when it is safe and when evidence may be needed for insurance or assistance. Do not keep hazardous debris solely for documentation; safety and official disposal instructions take priority.
