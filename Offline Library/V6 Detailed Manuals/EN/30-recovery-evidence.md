# Recovery Photo and Evidence Workflow

## Capture context before detail
When it is safe to document damage, start with wide views that establish the room, vehicle or property area before taking close photographs. This creates a sequence that another person can understand later and reduces the chance that a detailed image is impossible to locate in context.

## Preserve time and description
Use the incident log to connect each group of photographs with date, approximate time, location and a short factual description. Avoid editing originals in a way that removes metadata or changes the apparent condition. If annotations are helpful, make a separate copy.

## Link evidence to costs
Give repairs, temporary accommodation, transport and replacement purchases a simple claim or case reference when one exists. Use the same reference in the expense ledger and document register. This makes it possible to find the receipt, photograph and decision that relate to one recovery item without exposing payment credentials.

## Prioritize safety over documentation
Do not enter a damaged, flooded, smoke-affected or officially restricted area to obtain better evidence. Follow re-entry instructions and professional assessment requirements. If an item must be discarded promptly for safety, document it only if that can be done without delaying necessary removal.

## Keep originals redundant
Maintain at least one separate local copy of important photographs and scanned documents when storage permits. A backup should not depend on the same phone that may be lost or damaged during cleanup. Encrypt sensitive storage using the device or medium's supported security where appropriate.

## Close each recovery item
Mark whether the evidence was submitted, accepted, needs follow-up or is no longer required. Keep the final decision and reimbursement outcome instead of deleting rejected or superseded material, because the history may explain later totals.
