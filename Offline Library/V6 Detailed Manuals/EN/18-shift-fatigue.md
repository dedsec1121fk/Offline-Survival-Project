# Shift Planning and Fatigue Control

## Why shifts matter
A household can have enough supplies and still make poor decisions because the same exhausted person is handling radio checks, cooking, charging, children, sanitation and route updates. During a long incident, deliberately distribute attention. A simple shift roster is a reliability tool, not bureaucracy.

## Define roles by function
List functions that truly require continuity: communications, safety walk-through, water and food control, dependent care, power checks and record keeping. Assign a primary and backup for each function. Avoid assigning one person every critical role merely because they have the most technical knowledge; instead pair them with someone who can take over using written instructions.

## Protect sleep and handovers
Set quiet periods and protect uninterrupted rest wherever the situation permits. Before a person goes off duty, require a short handover containing only changes, unresolved hazards, next scheduled checks and items that can wait. A long narrative encourages omissions because the next person cannot identify what is actionable.

## Recognize degraded performance
Treat repeated arithmetic mistakes, forgotten tasks, irritability, fixation on one problem and difficulty following a checklist as signals to reduce workload and introduce a second checker. For navigation, electrical work, medication continuity or consequential decisions, use a deliberate read-back or two-person verification when practical.

## Build a resilient roster
Plan for one person becoming unavailable. Put key instructions where another adult can find them without unlocking someone else's phone. Rotate mentally demanding duties with simpler physical or observational tasks, and keep food, hydration and temperature control part of the shift plan rather than optional personal chores.

## Debrief the schedule
After each operational period note which shift was overloaded, which tasks were unnecessary, and which knowledge was concentrated in one person. Update the skills matrix and written procedures so the next incident requires less improvisation.
