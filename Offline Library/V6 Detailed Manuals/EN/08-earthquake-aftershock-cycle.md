# Earthquake aftershock operational-cycle manual

## First cycle: protect, account, isolate
After shaking stops, expect aftershocks. Check people before property. Put on sturdy footwear if debris is present. Identify fire, gas odor, damaged wiring, falling material, broken glass and obvious structural damage from a safe position. Greek Civil Protection advises leaving by stairs rather than lifts after an earthquake and avoiding re-entry when there are visible hazards such as damage, broken cables or gas leakage.

## Accountability
Use a short name/status/location check instead of assuming people are together. If someone is missing, record the last confirmed location and what is known; do not send untrained people into an unstable structure. Keep access routes clear for responders.

## Utility decisions
Know the normal shutoff locations before the incident. After an earthquake, isolate a utility only when the situation and official/property guidance call for it and you can reach the control safely. Do not create a second hazard by manipulating damaged electrical or gas equipment.

## Aftershock posture
Place essential shoes, light, phone/radio and grab items where they remain reachable even if the next shock happens at night. Move sleeping or waiting positions away from broken glass and visibly unstable objects. Recheck shelter zones after a significant aftershock because a previously usable area may change.

## Communications
Greek guidance warns against unnecessary telephone use after earthquakes because networks can overload. Use concise status messages and predetermined check-ins. Follow official information and ignore rumors, especially claims that predict an exact aftershock.

## Recovery handover
Each operational cycle should end with a short handover: people accounted for, unsafe areas, utilities isolated or uncertain, medical needs, water status, communications status, decisions awaiting review and the next planned check. This keeps the response from resetting to zero whenever responsibility changes.
