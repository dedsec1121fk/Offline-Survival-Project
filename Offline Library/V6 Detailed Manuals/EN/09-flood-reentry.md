# Flood re-entry and contamination-boundary manual

## Re-entry is a new hazard phase
Water going down does not mean the building is safe. Before entry, consider structural damage, unstable ground, electrical systems exposed to water, gas leaks, contaminated floodwater, mold, displaced animals and hidden sharp objects. CDC guidance advises caution with damaged electrical and gas systems and unsafe food or water after flooding.

## Observe before entering
From outside, record foundation movement, wall displacement, roof damage, leaning trees or poles, downed lines, standing water and unusual odors. If authorities have restricted access, do not enter. A photograph from a safe boundary can be more valuable than a close-up obtained by crossing an unknown electrical or structural hazard.

## Electrical boundary
Do not step into standing water to reach a breaker or appliance. Water-damaged electrical equipment may remain hazardous even when power appears off. Qualified inspection may be necessary before re-energizing a damaged system.

## Clean and dirty zones
Choose a boundary where contaminated footwear, gloves, tools and debris remain on one side. Keep drinking water, food, medication, child-care items and clean clothing on the other. If the boundary cannot be maintained, the shelter plan may need to move elsewhere during cleanup.

## Food and medicine
Discard food exposed to floodwater according to public-health guidance. Refrigerated food may also be unsafe because of the preceding outage. Medicines or medical supplies exposed to contaminated water require professional or manufacturer guidance; do not assume drying restores safety.

## Moisture record
Record what got wet, when water receded, what materials were removed and where persistent dampness remains. Mold can become a major recovery problem. Protect respiratory health during cleanup and seek appropriate guidance for large contamination areas or vulnerable occupants.
