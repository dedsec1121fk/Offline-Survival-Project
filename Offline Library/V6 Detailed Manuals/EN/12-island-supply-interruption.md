# Island and ferry-supply interruption manual

## Understand the transport dependency
On an island, weather or port disruption can affect food, medicines, fuel, mail, repair parts and movement at the same time. Record which household supplies depend on the next ferry or flight and which have local substitutes. A seven-day medicine stock and a seven-day snack stock do not represent the same continuity risk.

## Trigger inventory earlier
Do not wait until shelves are empty. When forecasts or official notices indicate likely transport disruption, check essential prescriptions, medical-device consumables, infant supplies, animal feed, drinking water, cooking method, battery capacity and vehicle fuel. Avoid panic buying that removes basic supplies from other residents; the goal is to expose genuine single points of failure.

## Medical continuity
Know the local health facility, pharmacy arrangements, emergency contact route and what normally requires transfer to a larger island or mainland. Do not alter prescribed medication schedules to “make them last” without professional advice. Store the medication list and prescriber/label instructions offline.

## Power and water coupling
Some island water systems depend on pumping or desalination, which themselves depend on electricity and fuel. A power problem can therefore become a water problem. Track household water separately from general utility status, and watch official local instructions.

## Communications
Port rumors spread quickly during disruption. Distinguish an official cancellation or civil-protection alert from social-media speculation. Keep one person responsible for checking authoritative updates at defined intervals instead of every household member repeatedly refreshing devices.

## Recovery from delay
When transport resumes, replenish the dependencies that created the greatest risk first, not necessarily the items that ran lowest. Update the continuity plan with the actual delay duration and consumption rate so the next planning horizon is based on evidence.
