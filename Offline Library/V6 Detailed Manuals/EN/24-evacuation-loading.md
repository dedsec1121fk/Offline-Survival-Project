# Evacuation Loading Sequence

## Design for departure, not storage
An evacuation load is successful when the right people and essential items can leave safely within the available time. A beautifully organized stockpile that requires forty minutes to move into a vehicle is not an evacuation system. Separate stay-at-home reserves from the smaller set intended to travel.

## Establish loading priority
People and required accessibility equipment come first. Next are medicines and essential medical devices, identity/contact documents, communication equipment, water and practical food, weather protection and necessary pet/service-animal supplies. Lower-priority comfort or replaceable items should not block seats, exits, visibility or safe load limits.

## Pre-assign physical locations
Decide which bag goes near which door, which person carries documents, where mobility equipment fits and what must remain reachable during the journey. Heavy objects need secure placement so sudden braking does not turn them into hazards. Keep the driver area clear of loose equipment.

## Use a departure trigger
Write down who can authorize departure and which official order, hazard threshold or household condition ends further packing. This prevents a common failure mode in which people continue searching for optional belongings after the decision to evacuate should already have been executed.

## Account before movement
Use a short count: people, pets/service animals, essential medicines, communication device, primary documents and vehicle keys. Confirm the planned destination and alternate route. Do not delay an urgent evacuation to achieve a perfect inventory.

## At arrival
Record the new location and status through the communication plan, keep keys and critical documents controlled, and inspect the load for damaged containers or temperature-sensitive items. Update the return or onward-movement plan using new official information.
