# Sanitation Consumables Forecasting

## Count functions, not just packages
Toilet operation, hand washing, waste containment, surface cleaning, laundry and personal hygiene consume different materials. Inventorying “cleaning supplies” as one category hides which sanitation function will fail first. Track each critical consumable against the point where it is used.

## Establish an observed burn rate
During a drill or normal week, measure how quickly soap, bags, wipes, toilet material, gloves or other household-specific items are actually used. Use that observed rate as a planning input and add a reserve appropriate to your disruption horizon. Do not invent chemical dilution or disinfection ratios; follow product labels and public-health guidance.

## Separate clean and dirty flow
Store clean consumables where they cannot be contaminated by waste handling. Define where used material goes before a bin or temporary toilet fills. If flood or sewer conditions change, reassess the entire flow rather than continuing the old routine automatically.

## Trigger resupply early
Set a reorder or conservation threshold above zero. Once the threshold is crossed, reduce optional consumption, identify an appropriate substitute if one has already been evaluated, and seek resupply while transport options still exist.

## Protect the hand-washing capability
A household may have water yet lose effective hand hygiene because soap, a dispensing container or a safe wastewater route is missing. Treat the station as a complete system and record its status in Sanitation Operations.

## Record abnormal use
A sudden increase in bags, gloves or cleaning material can indicate leakage, illness, flooding or another problem. Note the reason rather than silently adjusting the forecast; the change may require a different response, not simply more supplies.
