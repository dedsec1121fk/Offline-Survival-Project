# Failure Mode 17: Landslide threat on a route

## What has changed
A road or path near unstable ground now shows movement, falling material or an official closure that invalidates the familiar route.

## Evidence to confirm
- Check slope crack and falling rock against the last known normal condition; write the time of the change.
- Look for a mismatch involving mud flow, debris warning or closed road before assuming the original plan still works.
- Confirm whether alternate corridor and rainfall provide an independent fallback rather than another dependency on the same failure.

## Decisions for the next safe step
1. Mark the failed assumption explicitly and move the immediate task away from reliance on slope crack or mud flow.
2. Use alternate corridor as the first alternate only after checking that drainage channel does not change its safety or availability.
3. Record the decision with leaning object and set a new review point around reopening notice so the temporary workaround is not forgotten.

## Safety boundary
For landslide threat on a route, preserving the old plan is never more important than avoiding a second hazard. Treat problems involving slope crack, debris warning or drainage channel as reasons to stop, use an established safe alternative, and follow relevant official or professional instructions.

## Recovery checkpoint
Close this failure mode only after slope crack and mud flow are usable again, the workaround involving alternate corridor has been reviewed, and the record for leaning object reflects what actually happened.

## Field cues
