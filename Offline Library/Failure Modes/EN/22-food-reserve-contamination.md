# Failure Mode 22: Food reserve contamination

## What has changed
Food counted as emergency reserve has been exposed to water, pests, chemicals or damaged packaging and some stock may no longer be safe.

## Evidence to confirm
- Check damaged package and flood exposure against the last known normal condition; write the time of the change.
- Look for a mismatch involving pest mark, chemical spill or isolation bin before assuming the original plan still works.
- Confirm whether discard record and usable stock provide an independent fallback rather than another dependency on the same failure.

## Decisions for the next safe step
1. Mark the failed assumption explicitly and move the immediate task away from reliance on damaged package or pest mark.
2. Use discard record as the first alternate only after checking that storage inspection does not change its safety or availability.
3. Record the decision with swollen container and set a new review point around cross-contamination so the temporary workaround is not forgotten.

## Safety boundary
For food reserve contamination, preserving the old plan is never more important than avoiding a second hazard. Treat problems involving damaged package, chemical spill or storage inspection as reasons to stop, use an established safe alternative, and follow relevant official or professional instructions.

## Recovery checkpoint
Close this failure mode only after damaged package and pest mark are usable again, the workaround involving discard record has been reviewed, and the record for swollen container reflects what actually happened.

## Field cues
Use these concrete cues when searching notes, supplies, or related records: damaged package, flood exposure, pest mark, chemical spill, isolation bin, discard record, usable stock, storage inspection, swollen container, cross-contamination.
