# Failure Mode 21: Handwashing-water shortage

## What has changed
The water reserved for hand hygiene is being depleted or has become unusable while high-contact tasks continue.

## Evidence to confirm
- Check hygiene container and soap against the last known normal condition; write the time of the change.
- Look for a mismatch involving hand sanitizer, dirty task or food area before assuming the original plan still works.
- Confirm whether caregiving and clean-water reserve provide an independent fallback rather than another dependency on the same failure.

## Decisions for the next safe step
1. Mark the failed assumption explicitly and move the immediate task away from reliance on hygiene container or hand sanitizer.
2. Use caregiving as the first alternate only after checking that usage log does not change its safety or availability.
3. Record the decision with hand station and set a new review point around contamination break so the temporary workaround is not forgotten.

## Safety boundary
For handwashing-water shortage, preserving the old plan is never more important than avoiding a second hazard. Treat problems involving hygiene container, dirty task or usage log as reasons to stop, use an established safe alternative, and follow relevant official or professional instructions.

## Recovery checkpoint
Close this failure mode only after hygiene container and hand sanitizer are usable again, the workaround involving caregiving has been reviewed, and the record for hand station reflects what actually happened.

## Field cues
Use these concrete cues when searching notes, supplies, or related records: hygiene container, soap, hand sanitizer, dirty task, food area, caregiving, clean-water reserve, usage log, hand station, contamination break.
