# Failure Mode 18: Wildfire forces a route shift

## What has changed
Fire, smoke, wind or a revised evacuation instruction makes the original departure route inappropriate even if it still appears open.

## Evidence to confirm
- Check evacuation zone and fire front against the last known normal condition; write the time of the change.
- Look for a mismatch involving smoke visibility, wind change or traffic closure before assuming the original plan still works.
- Confirm whether alternate destination and fuel level provide an independent fallback rather than another dependency on the same failure.

## Decisions for the next safe step
1. Mark the failed assumption explicitly and move the immediate task away from reliance on evacuation zone or smoke visibility.
2. Use alternate destination as the first alternate only after checking that official alert does not change its safety or availability.
3. Record the decision with departure time and set a new review point around responder lane so the temporary workaround is not forgotten.

## Safety boundary
For wildfire forces a route shift, preserving the old plan is never more important than avoiding a second hazard. Treat problems involving evacuation zone, wind change or official alert as reasons to stop, use an established safe alternative, and follow relevant official or professional instructions.

## Recovery checkpoint
Close this failure mode only after evacuation zone and smoke visibility are usable again, the workaround involving alternate destination has been reviewed, and the record for departure time reflects what actually happened.

## Field cues
Use these concrete cues when searching notes, supplies, or related records: evacuation zone, fire front, smoke visibility, wind change, traffic closure, alternate destination, fuel level, official alert, departure time, responder lane.
