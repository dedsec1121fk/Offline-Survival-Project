# Failure Mode 08: Fuel-supply loss

## What has changed
Fuel assumed for transport or approved backup equipment is unavailable or below the quantity used by the continuity plan.

## Evidence to confirm
- Check station outage and approved container against the last known normal condition; write the time of the change.
- Look for a mismatch involving fuel type, route distance or reserve level before assuming the original plan still works.
- Confirm whether ventilation and ignition source provide an independent fallback rather than another dependency on the same failure.

## Decisions for the next safe step
1. Mark the failed assumption explicitly and move the immediate task away from reliance on station outage or fuel type.
2. Use ventilation as the first alternate only after checking that resupply point does not change its safety or availability.
3. Record the decision with vehicle range and set a new review point around storage check so the temporary workaround is not forgotten.

## Safety boundary
For fuel-supply loss, preserving the old plan is never more important than avoiding a second hazard. Treat problems involving station outage, route distance or resupply point as reasons to stop, use an established safe alternative, and follow relevant official or professional instructions.

## Recovery checkpoint
Close this failure mode only after station outage and fuel type are usable again, the workaround involving ventilation has been reviewed, and the record for vehicle range reflects what actually happened.

## Field cues
Use these concrete cues when searching notes, supplies, or related records: station outage, approved container, fuel type, route distance, reserve level, ventilation, ignition source, resupply point, vehicle range, storage check.
