# Failure Mode 37: Single-person knowledge loss

## What has changed
A critical procedure exists only in the memory of one person who is now absent, injured, unreachable or overloaded with other duties.

## Evidence to confirm
- Check manual release and medicine routine against the last known normal condition; write the time of the change.
- Look for a mismatch involving radio setup, utility shutoff or password location before assuming the original plan still works.
- Confirm whether pet care and route knowledge provide an independent fallback rather than another dependency on the same failure.

## Decisions for the next safe step
1. Mark the failed assumption explicitly and move the immediate task away from reliance on manual release or radio setup.
2. Use pet care as the first alternate only after checking that supplier contact does not change its safety or availability.
3. Record the decision with paper procedure and set a new review point around backup owner so the temporary workaround is not forgotten.

## Safety boundary
For single-person knowledge loss, preserving the old plan is never more important than avoiding a second hazard. Treat problems involving manual release, utility shutoff or supplier contact as reasons to stop, use an established safe alternative, and follow relevant official or professional instructions.

## Recovery checkpoint
Close this failure mode only after manual release and radio setup are usable again, the workaround involving pet care has been reviewed, and the record for paper procedure reflects what actually happened.

## Field cues
Use these concrete cues when searching notes, supplies, or related records: manual release, medicine routine, radio setup, utility shutoff, password location, pet care, route knowledge, supplier contact, paper procedure, backup owner.
