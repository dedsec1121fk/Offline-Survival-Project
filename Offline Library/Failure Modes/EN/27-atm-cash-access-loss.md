# Failure Mode 27: ATM and cash-access loss

## What has changed
Cash cannot be withdrawn where expected and nearby banking access is also disrupted, reducing flexibility for essential local purchases.

## Evidence to confirm
- Check atm outage and branch closure against the last known normal condition; write the time of the change.
- Look for a mismatch involving cash reserve, small denomination or travel cost before assuming the original plan still works.
- Confirm whether fuel purchase and receipt log provide an independent fallback rather than another dependency on the same failure.

## Decisions for the next safe step
1. Mark the failed assumption explicitly and move the immediate task away from reliance on atm outage or cash reserve.
2. Use fuel purchase as the first alternate only after checking that bank notice does not change its safety or availability.
3. Record the decision with secure storage and set a new review point around shared expense so the temporary workaround is not forgotten.

## Safety boundary
For atm and cash-access loss, preserving the old plan is never more important than avoiding a second hazard. Treat problems involving atm outage, small denomination or bank notice as reasons to stop, use an established safe alternative, and follow relevant official or professional instructions.

## Recovery checkpoint
Close this failure mode only after atm outage and cash reserve are usable again, the workaround involving fuel purchase has been reviewed, and the record for secure storage reflects what actually happened.

## Field cues
Use these concrete cues when searching notes, supplies, or related records: atm outage, branch closure, cash reserve, small denomination, travel cost, fuel purchase, receipt log, bank notice, secure storage, shared expense.
