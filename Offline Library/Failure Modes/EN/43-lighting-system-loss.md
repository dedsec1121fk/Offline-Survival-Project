# Failure Mode 43: Primary emergency lighting failure

## What has changed
The main emergency lights are depleted, damaged or unavailable, increasing fall and navigation risk after dark or inside unlit spaces.

## Evidence to confirm
- Check flashlight and headlamp against the last known normal condition; write the time of the change.
- Look for a mismatch involving spare cell, stair light or exit path before assuming the original plan still works.
- Confirm whether lantern and reflective marker provide an independent fallback rather than another dependency on the same failure.

## Decisions for the next safe step
1. Mark the failed assumption explicitly and move the immediate task away from reliance on flashlight or spare cell.
2. Use lantern as the first alternate only after checking that night route does not change its safety or availability.
3. Record the decision with battery contact and set a new review point around daylight task so the temporary workaround is not forgotten.

## Safety boundary
For primary emergency lighting failure, preserving the old plan is never more important than avoiding a second hazard. Treat problems involving flashlight, stair light or night route as reasons to stop, use an established safe alternative, and follow relevant official or professional instructions.

## Recovery checkpoint
Close this failure mode only after flashlight and spare cell are usable again, the workaround involving lantern has been reviewed, and the record for battery contact reflects what actually happened.

## Field cues
Use these concrete cues when searching notes, supplies, or related records: flashlight, headlamp, spare cell, stair light, exit path, lantern, reflective marker, night route, battery contact, daylight task.
