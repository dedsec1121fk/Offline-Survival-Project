# Failure Mode 28: Radio equipment failure

## What has changed
A radio expected to provide backup communication cannot reliably receive, transmit or remain powered under the current setup.

## Evidence to confirm
- Check radio handset and antenna against the last known normal condition; write the time of the change.
- Look for a mismatch involving battery contact, frequency card or spare cell before assuming the original plan still works.
- Confirm whether receive test and transmit check provide an independent fallback rather than another dependency on the same failure.

## Decisions for the next safe step
1. Mark the failed assumption explicitly and move the immediate task away from reliance on radio handset or battery contact.
2. Use receive test as the first alternate only after checking that headset does not change its safety or availability.
3. Record the decision with cable connector and set a new review point around alternate channel so the temporary workaround is not forgotten.

## Safety boundary
For radio equipment failure, preserving the old plan is never more important than avoiding a second hazard. Treat problems involving radio handset, frequency card or headset as reasons to stop, use an established safe alternative, and follow relevant official or professional instructions.

## Recovery checkpoint
Close this failure mode only after radio handset and battery contact are usable again, the workaround involving receive test has been reviewed, and the record for cable connector reflects what actually happened.

## Field cues
Use these concrete cues when searching notes, supplies, or related records: radio handset, antenna, battery contact, frequency card, spare cell, receive test, transmit check, headset, cable connector, alternate channel.
