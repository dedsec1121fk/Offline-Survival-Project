# Failure Mode 19: Sewage backup

## What has changed
Wastewater is backing up through a drain or fixture and ordinary sanitation routines can no longer continue in the affected area.

## Evidence to confirm
- Check floor drain and toilet backup against the last known normal condition; write the time of the change.
- Look for a mismatch involving wastewater, clean-water separation or protective glove before assuming the original plan still works.
- Confirm whether sewer notice and contaminated surface provide an independent fallback rather than another dependency on the same failure.

## Decisions for the next safe step
1. Mark the failed assumption explicitly and move the immediate task away from reliance on floor drain or wastewater.
2. Use sewer notice as the first alternate only after checking that electrical exposure does not change its safety or availability.
3. Record the decision with cleanup zone and set a new review point around service repair so the temporary workaround is not forgotten.

## Safety boundary
For sewage backup, preserving the old plan is never more important than avoiding a second hazard. Treat problems involving floor drain, clean-water separation or electrical exposure as reasons to stop, use an established safe alternative, and follow relevant official or professional instructions.

## Recovery checkpoint
Close this failure mode only after floor drain and wastewater are usable again, the workaround involving sewer notice has been reviewed, and the record for cleanup zone reflects what actually happened.

## Field cues
Use these concrete cues when searching notes, supplies, or related records: floor drain, toilet backup, wastewater, clean-water separation, protective glove, sewer notice, contaminated surface, electrical exposure, cleanup zone, service repair.
