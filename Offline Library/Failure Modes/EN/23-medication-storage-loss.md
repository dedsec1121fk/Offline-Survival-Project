# Failure Mode 23: Medication storage conditions lost

## What has changed
A medicine or medical supply can no longer be kept under the storage conditions specified for it, creating uncertainty about continued use.

## Evidence to confirm
- Check medicine label and storage temperature against the last known normal condition; write the time of the change.
- Look for a mismatch involving pharmacist contact, cool pack or prescription list before assuming the original plan still works.
- Confirm whether dose record and expiry date provide an independent fallback rather than another dependency on the same failure.

## Decisions for the next safe step
1. Mark the failed assumption explicitly and move the immediate task away from reliance on medicine label or pharmacist contact.
2. Use dose record as the first alternate only after checking that backup pharmacy does not change its safety or availability.
3. Record the decision with device battery and set a new review point around manufacturer guidance so the temporary workaround is not forgotten.

## Safety boundary
For medication storage conditions lost, preserving the old plan is never more important than avoiding a second hazard. Treat problems involving medicine label, cool pack or backup pharmacy as reasons to stop, use an established safe alternative, and follow relevant official or professional instructions.

## Recovery checkpoint
Close this failure mode only after medicine label and pharmacist contact are usable again, the workaround involving dose record has been reviewed, and the record for device battery reflects what actually happened.

## Field cues
Use these concrete cues when searching notes, supplies, or related records: medicine label, storage temperature, pharmacist contact, cool pack, prescription list, dose record, expiry date, backup pharmacy, device battery, manufacturer guidance.
