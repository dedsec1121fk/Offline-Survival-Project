# Failure Mode 11: Shelter overcapacity

## What has changed
The planned shelter or host site cannot safely accommodate the people, dependents, accessibility needs or supplies now arriving.

## Evidence to confirm
- Check capacity notice and secondary shelter against the last known normal condition; write the time of the change.
- Look for a mismatch involving accessible space, sanitation load or service animal before assuming the original plan still works.
- Confirm whether accountability list and host contact provide an independent fallback rather than another dependency on the same failure.

## Decisions for the next safe step
1. Mark the failed assumption explicitly and move the immediate task away from reliance on capacity notice or accessible space.
2. Use accountability list as the first alternate only after checking that destination update does not change its safety or availability.
3. Record the decision with sleeping area and set a new review point around intake desk so the temporary workaround is not forgotten.

## Safety boundary
For shelter overcapacity, preserving the old plan is never more important than avoiding a second hazard. Treat problems involving capacity notice, sanitation load or destination update as reasons to stop, use an established safe alternative, and follow relevant official or professional instructions.

## Recovery checkpoint
Close this failure mode only after capacity notice and accessible space are usable again, the workaround involving accountability list has been reviewed, and the record for sleeping area reflects what actually happened.

## Field cues
Use these concrete cues when searching notes, supplies, or related records: capacity notice, secondary shelter, accessible space, sanitation load, service animal, accountability list, host contact, destination update, sleeping area, intake desk.
