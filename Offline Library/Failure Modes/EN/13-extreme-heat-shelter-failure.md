# Failure Mode 13: Shelter overheating during extreme heat

## What has changed
The indoor refuge is accumulating heat and no longer provides dependable relief for the people who must remain there.

## Evidence to confirm
- Check indoor thermometer and shade room against the last known normal condition; write the time of the change.
- Look for a mismatch involving cooling centre, fan outage or transport plan before assuming the original plan still works.
- Confirm whether heat symptom and medication need provide an independent fallback rather than another dependency on the same failure.

## Decisions for the next safe step
1. Mark the failed assumption explicitly and move the immediate task away from reliance on indoor thermometer or cooling centre.
2. Use heat symptom as the first alternate only after checking that night temperature does not change its safety or availability.
3. Record the decision with water access and set a new review point around vulnerable person so the temporary workaround is not forgotten.

## Safety boundary
For shelter overheating during extreme heat, preserving the old plan is never more important than avoiding a second hazard. Treat problems involving indoor thermometer, fan outage or night temperature as reasons to stop, use an established safe alternative, and follow relevant official or professional instructions.

## Recovery checkpoint
Close this failure mode only after indoor thermometer and cooling centre are usable again, the workaround involving heat symptom has been reviewed, and the record for water access reflects what actually happened.

## Field cues
Use these concrete cues when searching notes, supplies, or related records: indoor thermometer, shade room, cooling centre, fan outage, transport plan, heat symptom, medication need, night temperature, water access, vulnerable person.
