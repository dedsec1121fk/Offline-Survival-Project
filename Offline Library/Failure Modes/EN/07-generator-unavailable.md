# Failure Mode 07: Generator unavailable or unusable

## What has changed
The generator expected in the backup plan cannot be started, reached or operated safely under current conditions.

## Evidence to confirm
- Check generator fault and outdoor position against the last known normal condition; write the time of the change.
- Look for a mismatch involving carbon monoxide, fuel reserve or extension rating before assuming the original plan still works.
- Confirm whether transfer equipment and maintenance log provide an independent fallback rather than another dependency on the same failure.

## Decisions for the next safe step
1. Mark the failed assumption explicitly and move the immediate task away from reliance on generator fault or carbon monoxide.
2. Use transfer equipment as the first alternate only after checking that start procedure does not change its safety or availability.
3. Record the decision with weather exposure and set a new review point around backup load so the temporary workaround is not forgotten.

## Safety boundary
For generator unavailable or unusable, preserving the old plan is never more important than avoiding a second hazard. Treat problems involving generator fault, fuel reserve or start procedure as reasons to stop, use an established safe alternative, and follow relevant official or professional instructions.

## Recovery checkpoint
Close this failure mode only after generator fault and carbon monoxide are usable again, the workaround involving transfer equipment has been reviewed, and the record for weather exposure reflects what actually happened.

## Field cues
Use these concrete cues when searching notes, supplies, or related records: generator fault, outdoor position, carbon monoxide, fuel reserve, extension rating, transfer equipment, maintenance log, start procedure, weather exposure, backup load.
