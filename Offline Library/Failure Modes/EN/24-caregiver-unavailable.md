# Failure Mode 24: Primary caregiver unavailable

## What has changed
The person who normally performs essential care is absent, unreachable or unable to continue, so knowledge and responsibilities must transfer immediately.

## Evidence to confirm
- Check care plan and backup caregiver against the last known normal condition; write the time of the change.
- Look for a mismatch involving medication schedule, mobility aid or feeding routine before assuming the original plan still works.
- Confirm whether contact sheet and consent document provide an independent fallback rather than another dependency on the same failure.

## Decisions for the next safe step
1. Mark the failed assumption explicitly and move the immediate task away from reliance on care plan or medication schedule.
2. Use contact sheet as the first alternate only after checking that service animal does not change its safety or availability.
3. Record the decision with appointment note and set a new review point around handover so the temporary workaround is not forgotten.

## Safety boundary
For primary caregiver unavailable, preserving the old plan is never more important than avoiding a second hazard. Treat problems involving care plan, mobility aid or service animal as reasons to stop, use an established safe alternative, and follow relevant official or professional instructions.

## Recovery checkpoint
Close this failure mode only after care plan and medication schedule are usable again, the workaround involving contact sheet has been reviewed, and the record for appointment note reflects what actually happened.

## Field cues
Use these concrete cues when searching notes, supplies, or related records: care plan, backup caregiver, medication schedule, mobility aid, feeding routine, contact sheet, consent document, service animal, appointment note, handover.
