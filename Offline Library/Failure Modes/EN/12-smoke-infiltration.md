# Failure Mode 12: Smoke infiltration into shelter

## What has changed
Outdoor smoke is entering the occupied building and the assumed shelter-in-place room is losing separation from the hazard.

## Evidence to confirm
- Check smoke odour and window seal against the last known normal condition; write the time of the change.
- Look for a mismatch involving ventilation intake, clean room or air cleaner before assuming the original plan still works.
- Confirm whether air-quality alert and evacuation notice provide an independent fallback rather than another dependency on the same failure.

## Decisions for the next safe step
1. Mark the failed assumption explicitly and move the immediate task away from reliance on smoke odour or ventilation intake.
2. Use air-quality alert as the first alternate only after checking that door gap does not change its safety or availability.
3. Record the decision with filter status and set a new review point around breathing symptom so the temporary workaround is not forgotten.

## Safety boundary
For smoke infiltration into shelter, preserving the old plan is never more important than avoiding a second hazard. Treat problems involving smoke odour, clean room or door gap as reasons to stop, use an established safe alternative, and follow relevant official or professional instructions.

## Recovery checkpoint
Close this failure mode only after smoke odour and ventilation intake are usable again, the workaround involving air-quality alert has been reviewed, and the record for filter status reflects what actually happened.

## Field cues
Use these concrete cues when searching notes, supplies, or related records: smoke odour, window seal, ventilation intake, clean room, air cleaner, air-quality alert, evacuation notice, door gap, filter status, breathing symptom.
