# Failure Mode 15: Flood isolation

## What has changed
Persistent floodwater has cut ordinary road or foot access and the location must now be managed as an isolated site.

## Evidence to confirm
- Check flooded road and bridge closure against the last known normal condition; write the time of the change.
- Look for a mismatch involving water current, high ground or route record before assuming the original plan still works.
- Confirm whether rescue instruction and contamination provide an independent fallback rather than another dependency on the same failure.

## Decisions for the next safe step
1. Mark the failed assumption explicitly and move the immediate task away from reliance on flooded road or water current.
2. Use rescue instruction as the first alternate only after checking that access reopening does not change its safety or availability.
3. Record the decision with water depth and set a new review point around isolated site so the temporary workaround is not forgotten.

## Safety boundary
For flood isolation, preserving the old plan is never more important than avoiding a second hazard. Treat problems involving flooded road, high ground or access reopening as reasons to stop, use an established safe alternative, and follow relevant official or professional instructions.

## Recovery checkpoint
Close this failure mode only after flooded road and water current are usable again, the workaround involving rescue instruction has been reviewed, and the record for water depth reflects what actually happened.

## Field cues
Use these concrete cues when searching notes, supplies, or related records: flooded road, bridge closure, water current, high ground, route record, rescue instruction, contamination, access reopening, water depth, isolated site.
