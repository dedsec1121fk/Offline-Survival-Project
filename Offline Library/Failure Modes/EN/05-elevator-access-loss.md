# Failure Mode 05: Elevator and powered-access loss

## What has changed
A lift, powered gate or other assisted access point is unavailable and the normal route may no longer work for every household member.

## Evidence to confirm
- Check lift landing and manual gate key against the last known normal condition; write the time of the change.
- Look for a mismatch involving accessible exit, stair barrier or building manager before assuming the original plan still works.
- Confirm whether responder access and mobility device provide an independent fallback rather than another dependency on the same failure.

## Decisions for the next safe step
1. Mark the failed assumption explicitly and move the immediate task away from reliance on lift landing or accessible exit.
2. Use responder access as the first alternate only after checking that door release does not change its safety or availability.
3. Record the decision with alternate entrance and set a new review point around meeting point so the temporary workaround is not forgotten.

## Safety boundary
For elevator and powered-access loss, preserving the old plan is never more important than avoiding a second hazard. Treat problems involving lift landing, stair barrier or door release as reasons to stop, use an established safe alternative, and follow relevant official or professional instructions.

## Recovery checkpoint
Close this failure mode only after lift landing and accessible exit are usable again, the workaround involving responder access has been reviewed, and the record for alternate entrance reflects what actually happened.

## Field cues
Use these concrete cues when searching notes, supplies, or related records: lift landing, manual gate key, accessible exit, stair barrier, building manager, responder access, mobility device, door release, alternate entrance, meeting point.
