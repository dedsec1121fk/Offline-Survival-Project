# Failure Mode 01: Cascading grid-power failure

## What has changed
A short outage has become a wider unstable loss of power that affects several unrelated household systems at once.

## Evidence to confirm
- Check breaker and power bank against the last known normal condition; write the time of the change.
- Look for a mismatch involving refrigerator, lamp or charging queue before assuming the original plan still works.
- Confirm whether manual release and radio provide an independent fallback rather than another dependency on the same failure.

## Decisions for the next safe step
1. Mark the failed assumption explicitly and move the immediate task away from reliance on breaker or refrigerator.
2. Use manual release as the first alternate only after checking that utility notice does not change its safety or availability.
3. Record the decision with backup load and set a new review point around restart so the temporary workaround is not forgotten.

## Safety boundary
For cascading grid-power failure, preserving the old plan is never more important than avoiding a second hazard. Treat problems involving breaker, lamp or utility notice as reasons to stop, use an established safe alternative, and follow relevant official or professional instructions.

## Recovery checkpoint
Close this failure mode only after breaker and refrigerator are usable again, the workaround involving manual release has been reviewed, and the record for backup load reflects what actually happened.

## Field cues
Use these concrete cues when searching notes, supplies, or related records: breaker, power bank, refrigerator, lamp, charging queue, manual release, radio, utility notice, backup load, restart.
