# Failure Mode 03: Loss of water pressure

## What has changed
Normal tap flow has failed, so ordinary household water use must switch to measured stored water and verified alternatives.

## Evidence to confirm
- Check tap pressure and storage container against the last known normal condition; write the time of the change.
- Look for a mismatch involving potable label, utility advisory or boil notice before assuming the original plan still works.
- Confirm whether sanitation reserve and main break provide an independent fallback rather than another dependency on the same failure.

## Decisions for the next safe step
1. Mark the failed assumption explicitly and move the immediate task away from reliance on tap pressure or potable label.
2. Use sanitation reserve as the first alternate only after checking that discoloured water does not change its safety or availability.
3. Record the decision with flush guidance and set a new review point around alternate source so the temporary workaround is not forgotten.

## Safety boundary
For loss of water pressure, preserving the old plan is never more important than avoiding a second hazard. Treat problems involving tap pressure, utility advisory or discoloured water as reasons to stop, use an established safe alternative, and follow relevant official or professional instructions.

## Recovery checkpoint
Close this failure mode only after tap pressure and potable label are usable again, the workaround involving sanitation reserve has been reviewed, and the record for flush guidance reflects what actually happened.

## Field cues
Use these concrete cues when searching notes, supplies, or related records: tap pressure, storage container, potable label, utility advisory, boil notice, sanitation reserve, main break, discoloured water, flush guidance, alternate source.
