# Failure Mode 14: Heating loss during extreme cold

## What has changed
The primary heating system has failed while outdoor conditions continue to cool the occupied building and its utilities.

## Evidence to confirm
- Check heating fault and indoor thermometer against the last known normal condition; write the time of the change.
- Look for a mismatch involving warm room, pipe freeze or carbon monoxide alarm before assuming the original plan still works.
- Confirm whether approved heater and ventilation provide an independent fallback rather than another dependency on the same failure.

## Decisions for the next safe step
1. Mark the failed assumption explicitly and move the immediate task away from reliance on heating fault or warm room.
2. Use approved heater as the first alternate only after checking that relocation threshold does not change its safety or availability.
3. Record the decision with blanket layer and set a new review point around utility status so the temporary workaround is not forgotten.

## Safety boundary
For heating loss during extreme cold, preserving the old plan is never more important than avoiding a second hazard. Treat problems involving heating fault, pipe freeze or relocation threshold as reasons to stop, use an established safe alternative, and follow relevant official or professional instructions.

## Recovery checkpoint
Close this failure mode only after heating fault and warm room are usable again, the workaround involving approved heater has been reviewed, and the record for blanket layer reflects what actually happened.

## Field cues
Use these concrete cues when searching notes, supplies, or related records: heating fault, indoor thermometer, warm room, pipe freeze, carbon monoxide alarm, approved heater, ventilation, relocation threshold, blanket layer, utility status.
