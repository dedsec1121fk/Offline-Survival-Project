# Failure Mode 16: New damage after an aftershock

## What has changed
A later shock has changed the condition of a building or room that had been treated as usable after the first earthquake.

## Evidence to confirm
- Check fresh crack and fallen contents against the last known normal condition; write the time of the change.
- Look for a mismatch involving gas odour, blocked stair or utility shutoff before assuming the original plan still works.
- Confirm whether aftershock and structural assessment provide an independent fallback rather than another dependency on the same failure.

## Decisions for the next safe step
1. Mark the failed assumption explicitly and move the immediate task away from reliance on fresh crack or gas odour.
2. Use aftershock as the first alternate only after checking that relocation plan does not change its safety or availability.
3. Record the decision with door change and set a new review point around falling material so the temporary workaround is not forgotten.

## Safety boundary
For new damage after an aftershock, preserving the old plan is never more important than avoiding a second hazard. Treat problems involving fresh crack, blocked stair or relocation plan as reasons to stop, use an established safe alternative, and follow relevant official or professional instructions.

## Recovery checkpoint
Close this failure mode only after fresh crack and gas odour are usable again, the workaround involving aftershock has been reviewed, and the record for door change reflects what actually happened.

## Field cues
Use these concrete cues when searching notes, supplies, or related records: fresh crack, fallen contents, gas odour, blocked stair, utility shutoff, aftershock, structural assessment, relocation plan, door change, falling material.
