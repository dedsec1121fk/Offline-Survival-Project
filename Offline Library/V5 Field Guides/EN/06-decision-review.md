# Decisions need expiry points

A reasonable decision can become unsafe when the conditions that justified it change. Write the decision separately from the issue and give important decisions a review point.

A useful entry answers five questions:
1. What problem are we deciding now?
2. What did we decide to do or not do?
3. Which facts or constraints mattered most?
4. Who owns the next action?
5. What event or time forces a review?

“Stay here until 18:00 unless smoke enters the corridor or an official evacuation order arrives” is operationally clearer than “stay for now.” Mark a decision as superseded rather than silently editing its history when a later decision replaces it. That preserves why the plan changed and prevents two versions from circulating at once.
