# Shelter zoning without false certainty

A shelter board is useful only if its labels describe what you actually know. “Usable” should mean that the area is currently being used under the limits you have observed; it does not mean an engineer, fire officer or utility technician has certified it.

Use four states:
- **Unknown** when nobody has checked the area from a safe position.
- **Needs re-check** when conditions changed, information conflicts or the previous check is stale.
- **Usable** when the area is presently serving its intended role and no known exclusion applies.
- **Avoid / isolate** when a hazard, official restriction or unresolved concern means people should stay out.

For each zone, note occupants and essential services only when they help coordination. A short note such as “no lighting; dry floor; exterior door available” is more useful than a vague “okay.” Re-check after an aftershock, new smoke, rising water, loss of power, a strong odour, visible structural change or an official update.
