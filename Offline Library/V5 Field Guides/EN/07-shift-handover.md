# Shift handover when people are tired

Long disruptions create mistakes at the moment responsibility changes hands. A useful handover is short enough to read when tired but specific enough to continue the work without guessing.

Cover only what changed or still matters:
- people who need checking and the next check time;
- supplies that crossed a reserve point or changed location;
- equipment that is unavailable, isolated or running on limited power;
- active decisions and their review triggers;
- unresolved hazards and places that should not be entered;
- the next two or three actions already assigned.

The incoming person should repeat back the highest-risk item. If the handover depends on a phone, keep a paper fallback for the minimum critical information. Do not transfer passwords, private medical details or access codes unless the next person genuinely needs them and you can protect the record.
