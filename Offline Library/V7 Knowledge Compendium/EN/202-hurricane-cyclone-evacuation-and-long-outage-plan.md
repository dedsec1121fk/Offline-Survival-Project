# Hurricane/Cyclone Evacuation and Long-Outage Plan

Tropical cyclones combine several hazards: destructive wind, storm surge/coastal flooding, inland flooding, tornadoes, heat after power loss and long supply interruptions. A single “hurricane kit” is not enough; plan the evacuation and the post-storm outage separately.

## Decide evacuation triggers before landfall
Know whether the property lies in an evacuation or storm-surge zone and where official orders come from. Leave when authorities direct you; do not wait for water or wind to prove the forecast. Identify at least two routes because bridges and low roads can close early. Reserve fuel and account for animals, mobility equipment and medicines that need temperature control.

## If sheltering is officially appropriate
Move loose outdoor objects, charge communications/power storage, protect documents, fill only approved water containers, and choose an interior protected room away from windows. Never run a generator or charcoal/fuel-burning appliance inside a home, garage or other enclosed/partially enclosed shelter.

## Plan for the days after
Assume roads, refrigeration, mobile networks, pharmacies, payment terminals and fuel stations may not recover together. Use the Command Center to track water, food, power loads, medicines, fuel and verified information. Do not return to an evacuated area until authorities allow it.
