# Emergency supply intake: inspect, record and quarantine before distribution

## More supplies can create more uncertainty
Donations and emergency purchases may arrive with damaged packaging, unknown storage history, wrong specifications or no clear owner. Create a small intake point instead of placing everything directly into the household pantry or medical kit.

## Record basic provenance
For important items note date received, source, quantity, expiration/best-before where relevant and any storage requirement. For batteries, chargers, filters or spare parts record model compatibility. For food, preserve labels and allergen information.

## Quarantine questionable items
Flood-wet packages, leaking chemicals, swollen batteries, opened medicines, unlabeled food or equipment with visible damage should not be mixed with verified stock. Mark them for review/disposal according to appropriate guidance. “It was donated” does not establish safety.

## Separate controlled/special items
Prescription medicines belong to the intended patient and should be handled according to pharmacy/clinical rules; a neighborhood supply table is not a medication exchange. Allergy-safe and infant foods should remain identifiable. Fuel and hazardous chemicals stay physically outside food/water storage.

## Update the inventory at distribution
When supplies leave intake, assign them to the correct household kit, food lot, power reserve or sanitation point. This prevents double counting and helps the next shift know which resources are still unallocated.

## Learn which supply channels are reliable
After the event, note vendors, public points or community channels that delivered usable items and those that produced unsuitable stock. A good intake log improves future procurement rather than becoming paperwork for its own sake.
