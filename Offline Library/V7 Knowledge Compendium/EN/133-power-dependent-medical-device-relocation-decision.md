# Power-Dependent Medical Devices: Deciding When to Relocate

A backup battery is only one part of continuity for a power-dependent medical device. The household also needs to know the device’s actual power demand, battery age, charging time, safe environmental limits, transport requirements, and what happens if backup power cannot be restored. Ready.gov specifically recommends alternate plans for power-dependent medical devices during outages.

## Build a device-specific endurance picture
Record the manufacturer/model, power adapter rating, battery type, normal runtime, verified backup runtime, required accessories, and whether the device can safely operate from a vehicle inverter or portable battery. Do not assume the wattage printed on one adapter equals real-world endurance. Test the backup plan before an emergency under the clinician/manufacturer’s constraints.

## Define relocation triggers in advance
Relocation should not begin only when the battery reaches zero. Triggers can include declining backup reserve, inability to recharge, unsafe indoor temperature, transport service availability, official outage estimates, oxygen or consumable shortages, or loss of the caregiver needed to operate the equipment. Record a primary and alternate powered destination and confirm that the destination can support the device before travel.

## Protect medical continuity during movement
Move the device with its cables, batteries, consumables, prescription/device documentation, settings information that the care team says should travel, and a simple handover note. Avoid changing prescribed settings or substituting improvised power connections. If the device fails, alarms, overheats, gets wet, or behaves abnormally, follow its emergency instructions and seek professional support rather than repeatedly resetting it.
