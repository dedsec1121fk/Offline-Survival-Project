# LPG Cylinders and Portable Gas Cooking During Outages

Liquefied petroleum gas can keep cooking available during a power outage, but leaks, poor ventilation, damaged regulators/hoses, and improvised indoor use can create fire, explosion, or carbon-monoxide hazards. The fuel reserve is only useful if the appliance is operated exactly within its approved environment.

## Use equipment only where it is designed to operate
Follow the manufacturer’s location and ventilation instructions. Outdoor-only burners, grills, and camping appliances remain outdoor-only during a blackout. Do not move them into a kitchen, corridor, tent, car, garage, or enclosed balcony because of rain or cold. Never use a gas oven or range as a room heater.

## Treat odor/leak suspicion as a stop condition
If gas odor or a suspected leak occurs, avoid flames, smoking, and electrical switching that could create a spark. Move people away and follow supplier/fire-service instructions. Do not test a suspected leak with a flame. Damaged cylinders, valves, hoses, or regulators should be isolated from use and assessed/replaced through the appropriate supplier/professional route.

## Store cylinders deliberately
Keep cylinders upright and protected from physical damage, heat, ignition sources, and unauthorized access according to local rules and supplier instructions. Do not store a large improvised fuel cache in sleeping areas. Label which cylinder is full/in service/empty so an emergency changeover does not involve guesswork.

## Cooking-zone discipline
Keep combustibles, curtains, paper, fuel containers, and evacuation bags away from the flame. Never leave cooking unattended. Keep children/pets out of the work zone and maintain a clear exit path. A working smoke/CO alarm appropriate to the space adds warning but does not make unsafe indoor combustion acceptable.
