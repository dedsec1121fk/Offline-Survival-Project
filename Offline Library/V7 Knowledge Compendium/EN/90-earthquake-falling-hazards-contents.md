# Earthquake falling hazards and contents: reduce what can injure people before the shaking

## Nonstructural hazards cause real injuries
Shelves, televisions, mirrors, cabinets, water heaters, lighting and stored objects can fall or move during shaking even when the main structure remains standing. Walk through sleeping areas, exits and frequently occupied spaces and identify heavy items above head level or along evacuation paths.

## Secure with methods suitable for the object and building
Use appropriate anchoring/restraint products and follow manufacturer/building guidance. A weak improvised tie may create false confidence or damage the wall. Renters should follow lease/building requirements and seek approved methods rather than drilling critical assemblies blindly.

## Put heavy items low
Store dense containers, tools and large supplies on lower shelves. Use latches where suitable so cabinets do not release contents into walkways. Keep glass and heavy decoration away from beds/sofas when possible.

## Protect the post-shaking route
After an earthquake, expect broken glass and fallen objects. Keep sturdy shoes and a light near sleeping areas. Do not rush into dark corridors. Follow Greek Civil Protection guidance for aftershocks and building evacuation; do not use elevators where instructions advise stairs.

## Recheck after every meaningful shake
An anchor, shelf or cabinet may be loosened even if it did not fall. Do not stand beneath visibly displaced heavy objects to inspect them. Isolate unsafe rooms and use qualified assessment for structural concerns.

## Feed the inspection into preparedness
Photograph the normal arrangement and note corrected hazards. After an incident, record which objects moved. This is one of the easiest areas where a household can convert a real observation into a concrete prevention change.
