# Portable batteries and power banks: make stored energy auditable

## Capacity labels are planning inputs, not promises
Record each power bank or battery station by model, rated energy/capacity, output types, maximum output, charger requirement and date last tested. Real usable energy is lower than the printed cell capacity because conversion, temperature, age and device charging losses consume part of the reserve. Build margin into endurance calculations instead of planning to the final percent.

## Give each device a job
A small power bank may be assigned to one phone; a larger station may be reserved for radios, lighting or approved medical equipment. Labeling prevents a high-priority reserve from being emptied for entertainment. Where several people share identical packs, number them and rotate through them deliberately so one remains untouched as a reserve.

## Store and charge within product limits
Lithium batteries can be damaged by heat, physical impact, water, incorrect chargers or unsafe charging environments. Follow the manufacturer’s temperature and charging instructions. Do not charge a swollen, punctured, leaking or unusually hot pack. Isolate damaged units away from combustible materials and obtain appropriate disposal guidance; do not keep using them because they still produce power.

## Track energy, not just indicator bars
For longer incidents, note starting percentage, important loads supplied and ending percentage. If the device provides watt-hour data, use it. Otherwise build an empirical record from repeated normal use. A four-bar indicator is too coarse for deciding whether a communications reserve will last another night.

## Plan charging windows
Match charging to the best available source: mains when restored, vehicle when operating for legitimate transport needs, or a compatible solar/DC source. Avoid running a combustion engine solely inside or adjacent to occupied space for charging. If solar is used, protect equipment from water and trip hazards and keep the setup within manufacturer electrical limits.

## Retirement criteria matter
A pack that self-discharges rapidly, shuts down under modest load, has damaged connectors or has experienced swelling/impact belongs in review, not in the emergency reserve. Record retirement/replacement so the readiness inventory reflects equipment that can actually be trusted.
