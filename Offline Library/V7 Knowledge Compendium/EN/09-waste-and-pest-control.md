# Waste accumulation, vectors, and pest control during service interruption

## Waste becomes an environmental-health problem quickly
When municipal collection stops, household rubbish shifts from a convenience issue to a storage and pest-management problem. Food scraps, nappies, animal waste, and contaminated cleanup materials create odour, insects, rodents, leakage, and contact hazards. Separate these from clean recyclables and dry packaging so the highest-risk waste occupies the smallest controlled volume.

## Choose a temporary storage point deliberately
The temporary waste area should be away from drinking-water storage, food preparation, children’s play, sleeping spaces, and heat or flame. It should not block an exit or emergency access. Protect bags or bins from animals and rain, and avoid placing them where floodwater could carry contents into the home or drainage system.

## Reduce volume without creating new hazards
Flatten clean boxes and containers, but do not crush glass, sharp metal, batteries, aerosol cans, unknown chemical containers, or medical sharps. Keep damaged lithium batteries isolated from ordinary rubbish because they can become a fire hazard. Follow local instructions for hazardous and medical waste rather than mixing them into general household waste.

## Monitor signs of pest activity
Gnaw marks, droppings, flies, larvae, ants, or torn bags mean the storage method needs adjustment. Remove food residue from the clean living area promptly, close containers, and correct water leaks or standing water. Avoid indiscriminate pesticide use in occupied, poorly ventilated shelters, especially around children, food, or pets.

## Re-entry into normal collection
When collection resumes, do not move every bag at once if doing so blocks stairs or contaminates clean routes. Stage removal in small loads, clean the storage area afterward, and record any hazardous containers that need a separate disposal route.
