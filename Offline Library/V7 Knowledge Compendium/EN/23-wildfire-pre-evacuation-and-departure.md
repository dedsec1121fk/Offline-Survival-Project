# Wildfire pre-evacuation: leaving before the route becomes the emergency

## Define departure triggers in advance
A household under wildfire threat should not make every decision from scratch while smoke, traffic and alerts are escalating. Write down hard triggers: an official evacuation order, fire crossing a named landmark, loss of the primary road, a vulnerable person becoming symptomatic, or a vehicle/fuel problem that removes the remaining margin. A trigger is useful only if everyone knows what action it causes.

## Stage the household for movement
Keep identity documents, essential medicines, glasses, mobility equipment, chargers, water, animal carriers, keys and go-bags together. Park for a clean departure where local conditions allow, avoid blocking fire-service access, and keep enough fuel or charge to reach the planned safe area with a reserve. Assign one person to monitor official alerts and another to account for people and animals so the driver is not simultaneously doing every task.

## Route logic
Maintain at least one alternative route and identify choke points that can be closed by fire, fallen vegetation, police control or congestion. A longer open route is often better than a shorter route whose status is uncertain. Do not drive toward smoke or flames merely because the navigation application still shows the road. Official road closures and responder directions override the stored plan.

## The final ten minutes
If departure is triggered, do not turn the house into a last-minute packing exercise. Take the staged essentials, close doors and windows if time permits and authorities recommend it, move everyone into assigned vehicles, perform an accountability check, and leave. Do not re-enter for replaceable property. Share destination and route with the designated contact once safely underway.

## If evacuation is impossible
A household should know beforehand which local authority or emergency number to contact and where the most defensible available shelter is, but improvised sheltering is not a substitute for a timely evacuation order. If trapped, communicate precise location, number of people, mobility/medical constraints and visible fire/smoke conditions to emergency services.
