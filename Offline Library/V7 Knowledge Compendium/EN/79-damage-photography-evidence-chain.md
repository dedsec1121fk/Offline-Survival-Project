# Damage photography and evidence chain: document safely before cleanup changes the scene

## Safety comes before documentation
Do not enter a structurally unsafe, smoke-filled, electrically hazardous, flooded or contaminated area to obtain a better photo. Wait for clearance where required. A claim photograph is never worth exposure to an active hazard.

## Start with context, then detail
When safe, photograph the exterior/location, then the room/area, then individual damaged items. Include identifying features that show where the damage occurred. For large losses, a short narrated video can complement still images, but still photographs are easier to index and compare.

## Keep original files
Avoid editing or repeatedly re-saving the only copy. Preserve original timestamps/metadata where possible and make a backup. If you annotate a copy, keep the original separately. Record the photographer and date/time if device time may have been wrong during the outage.

## Link images to an inventory
Use a simple identifier for each damaged item or area and put the same identifier in the expense/recovery log. Add purchase information or serial number only where relevant and safe to store. This prevents a folder of hundreds of unnamed images from becoming unusable.

## Document disposal decisions
Some spoiled food, wet material or contaminated property may need to be removed quickly. Photograph it before disposal when safe and retain any official/contractor instruction that required removal. Do not keep hazardous material in the home solely because an insurer might want to see it physically.

## Protect private information
Images can expose IDs, prescriptions, addresses, security systems and people. Share only the subset required by the insurer, authority or contractor. Keep the full evidence archive protected like other sensitive emergency documents.
