# Asthma Action-Plan Continuity During Smoke, Mold, and Displacement

Disasters can combine several asthma triggers at once: smoke, dust, mold, cleaning chemicals, stress, disrupted sleep, heat, cold, and missed medication access. The safest offline plan is therefore built around the person’s existing clinician-created Asthma Action Plan rather than improvised treatment.

## Protect the written plan
Keep a paper copy and an offline digital copy of the Asthma Action Plan, medication list, inhaler/spacer instructions, prescriber/pharmacy contacts, and known triggers. CDC advises taking asthma medicines exactly as prescribed and not changing the treatment plan without a health-care provider. Mark refill/review dates early enough that an approaching storm or evacuation does not coincide with an empty controller or rescue medicine.

## Separate air-quality control from medication
During wildfire smoke, post-flood mold cleanup, dusty debris work, or chemical cleaning, reduce exposure first: move to cleaner air, keep the person away from cleanup zones, and use building/filtration controls appropriate to the hazard. Medication cannot make an unsafe exposure environment safe. If the normal safe room becomes too hot during an outage, heat risk must also be considered.

## Know the escalation point in advance
The person’s action plan should state what symptoms/measurements require rescue medicine and what indicates urgent/emergency care. Household members should know where the plan is stored and how to describe worsening breathing to emergency dispatch. If severe breathing difficulty, inability to speak normally, bluish/gray lips or altered consciousness appears, treat it as an emergency rather than waiting for another household remedy.

## Protect devices and supplies
Keep inhalers away from extreme heat, contamination, and water; follow label/manufacturer storage limits. If a nebulizer or other device needs electricity, link it to the power-dependent medical equipment plan and relocation threshold.
