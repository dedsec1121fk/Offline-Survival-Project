# Wildfire ash cleanup: wait for clearance and control dust instead of redistributing it

## Ash is not ordinary household dust
Wildfire ash can contain fine particles and residues from burned buildings, vehicles and household materials. Follow local public-health/fire guidance for the affected area. Children, pets and people with respiratory or cardiovascular vulnerability should be kept away from ash cleanup where possible.

## Do not create airborne dust
Avoid dry sweeping, leaf blowers or activities that aggressively resuspend ash. Use cleanup methods recommended by health/environment authorities for the incident and suitable protective equipment. Do not wash large amounts of ash into storm drains or water bodies unless local guidance permits it.

## Protect food and water
Discard food or water that authorities say has been contaminated and keep clean supplies covered during cleanup. Outdoor water tanks/catchments exposed to heavy ash require assessment before drinking use. Boiling water does not remove chemical contamination from burned materials.

## Inspect the building first
Fire can damage roofs, decks, trees, utility lines and hidden structural elements even when a house remains standing. Do not climb onto damaged roofs or enter unstable structures to remove ash. Treat damaged solar/electrical systems and propane/fuel equipment as specialist hazards.

## Keep dirty and clean zones separate
Remove outer cleanup clothing before entering the main living/sleep area, wash hands and control ash on footwear. Bag heavily contaminated debris according to local waste instructions. Protect HVAC equipment/filters according to manufacturer/public-health guidance.

## Document and follow official disposal rules
Photograph affected areas before cleanup when safe. Some ash/debris from burned structures may be regulated differently from green-waste ash. Use the municipality/authority’s collection and disposal instructions rather than dumping it with ordinary waste by assumption.
