# Kitchen Oil and Grease Fire: Smother, Isolate, and Escape First

Cooking fires remain a major household hazard, and outage cooking can increase risk because people use unfamiliar burners, poor lighting, extension cords, crowded counters, or alternative fuels. A pan of burning oil is especially dangerous if water is thrown onto it.

## Stay with active cooking
Keep the cooking area clear of paper, towels, curtains, plastic packaging, fuel, and evacuation bags. Turn pot handles inward without placing them over another burner. In low light, use stable electric lighting rather than carrying an open flame near hot oil.

## If a pan ignites
If it is safe and you can do so without reaching through flames, turn off the heat. Cover the pan with a suitable metal lid or baking sheet to smother the fire and leave it covered until completely cool. Do not carry the burning pan through the home and never throw water on burning cooking oil/grease; violent splattering can spread flame and cause severe burns.

## Oven or appliance fire
Keep the door closed when opening it would feed/spread fire, shut off the appliance if safe, and follow fire-service guidance. If fire escapes the container/appliance, smoke is building, you lack a safe exit behind you, or you are uncertain, evacuate and call the fire service rather than trying repeated household extinguishing attempts.

## Extinguisher limits
A suitable extinguisher can be useful only for a small fire, with a clear exit, and for a person who is trained/comfortable using it. Escape is the priority. Do not let possession of an extinguisher trap someone between the fire and the door.
