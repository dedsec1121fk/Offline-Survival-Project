# Severe wind: building envelope, falling objects, and post-storm movement

## Wind turns normal objects into hazards
Strong wind can fail roof tiles, shutters, signs, branches, scaffolding, solar panels and unsecured balcony items. Before conditions worsen, bring loose objects inside or secure them without exposing anyone to an already dangerous exterior. Close and latch openings according to the building’s design; do not improvise by standing outside to hold or repair a failing shutter.

## Choose the protected interior position
The safest location depends on the building and local hazard, but a household should identify an interior area away from windows, skylights and large unsupported glazing. Keep sturdy footwear, a light and a way to receive official alerts there. In apartments, also consider objects from balconies or façades above, not only damage to your own unit.

## Power and fire consequences
Wind damage can bring down distribution lines or create arcing and fire. Treat fallen or low wires as energized and keep people and animals away. Do not move branches touching lines. If the building loses power, activate the normal outage plan rather than immediately starting fuel-burning equipment near windows or entrances.

## After the peak
Do not rush outdoors when the wind briefly eases. Check official warnings and observe from a protected location first. When leaving, watch for hanging roof material, unstable trees, broken glass, damaged walls and overhead lines. Photograph damage only after the area is safe enough to occupy.

## Long-duration exposure
If repeated storms are expected, convert the first event into maintenance information: which window leaked, which drain overflowed, which exterior item moved, which room accumulated glass or water and which communications method worked. Repair priority should be driven by life safety and weatherproofing before cosmetic damage.
