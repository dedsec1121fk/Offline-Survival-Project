# Shared-Kitchen Food Allergy Control During Displacement

Displacement changes where food is prepared, who handles it, which utensils are shared, and whether original packaging remains available. For a person with a serious food allergy, the hazard may be cross-contact or an unknown ingredient rather than lack of calories. The continuity plan should make uncertainty visible instead of pressuring the person to “eat something.”

## Protect identifiable food
Keep allergy-safe food in a clearly marked container or bag with the person’s name and allergen information. Preserve original labels whenever possible. If food is repacked, copy the product name, ingredients/allergen statement, lot or expiry when relevant, and who repacked it. Unlabeled communal food should be treated as unknown unless the responsible kitchen can verify ingredients and handling.

## Control utensils and surfaces
Where feasible, designate cleaned utensils, cutting surfaces, storage space, and serving tools for the allergy-safe meal. A utensil that touched the allergen should not be “wiped clean” and returned to the safe station. Handwashing and cleaning should happen before safe-food preparation, not only afterward. Communicate with shelter/kitchen staff rather than creating a parallel hidden kitchen that violates site safety rules.

## Keep the emergency plan attached to the person
The allergy action plan, prescribed emergency medicine, identification, and caregiver instructions should travel together. The app does not decide when or how to administer prescription emergency medication; follow the person’s clinician-provided plan and local emergency guidance. After any suspected reaction, obtain appropriate medical help rather than relying on symptom improvement alone.

## Verification anchors
- Existing personal allergy action plan and treating clinician.
- Shelter/kitchen food-safety and allergen procedures at the operating location.
