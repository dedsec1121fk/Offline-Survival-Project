# Emergency Water Quantity, Allocation and Conservation

## Use categories, not one magic number
Water demand includes drinking, food preparation, medicines, infant care, hand hygiene, minimal personal hygiene, pets and sometimes sanitation. Climate, illness, pregnancy, workload and infrastructure change requirements. Planning figures are useful context, not a universal medical prescription.

## Protect potable water first
Separate drinking-quality water from water reserved for flushing or cleaning. Use clearly different containers and labels so the last treated water is not spent on a task that can use lower-grade water when officially suitable.

## Count occupants and days explicitly
Enter actual people, animals and planning horizon. Visitors or displaced relatives immediately change endurance. A three-day reserve for two people is not a three-day reserve for six.

## Discount inaccessible volume
Containers do not always drain fully, sediment may be discarded, and powered pumps can make tank water inaccessible during an outage. Test how stored water is actually withdrawn without electricity.

## Track batches and sources
Record collection date, source, treatment, container, quantity and status. If a source is later found contaminated, traceability helps isolate affected batches without discarding everything or unknowingly continuing use.

## Conserve high-volume uses before drinking
Delay laundry, long showers, unnecessary cleaning and nonessential flushing before reducing appropriate drinking. Conservation should remove waste, not create dehydration.

## Heat and illness change demand
High temperatures, hard work, fever, vomiting and diarrhea can increase fluid needs and medical risk. Do not use a rigid ration schedule to deny water to someone who clinically needs more.

## Match treatment to the contaminant
Boiling and chemical disinfection target many microbes but do not remove dissolved salts, fuels or many chemical contaminants. Filters have specific performance limits. Suspected chemical contamination calls for an alternative safe source and official guidance.

## Set resupply triggers
Define a minimum potable reserve and initiate resupply or relocation before it is reached. Waiting for the final bottle removes time and route options.
