# Private Wells After Flooding: Isolation, Return, and Testing

A flooded private well should not be treated as safe simply because the water becomes clear again. Floodwater can enter through the wellhead, casing, nearby soil or damaged plumbing and carry microorganisms or chemicals that are invisible.

## During and immediately after flooding
Avoid using flooded well water for drinking, cooking, brushing teeth or other ingestion-related uses until the well has been appropriately assessed and tested. Electrical well equipment can also be hazardous after immersion; do not energize damaged pumps or controls casually. Use an alternate safe water supply while the well is out of service.

## Inspect before restoration
Look for displacement, cracked casing, a damaged cap, debris, erosion around the wellhead and damage to pressure tanks/piping. A licensed well or pump professional may be needed before disinfection or sampling, especially if electrical parts or the well structure were affected.

## Testing matters
Follow your public-health/environmental authority's post-flood sampling instructions. Microbiological testing is commonly part of return-to-service; suspected fuel, pesticide, industrial or septic contamination may require additional testing. Boiling can address many microbial hazards but does not remove many chemical contaminants.

## Keep a recovery record
Document flood level, photographs, inspection, repairs, disinfection performed by qualified guidance, laboratory results, date returned to service and any follow-up sampling. This record is valuable after later illness or repeated flooding.
