# Heat Work, Acclimatization, and Work-Rest Control

Cleanup and evacuation often begin exactly when heat exposure is highest. People who are normally sedentary may suddenly carry water, move debris, climb stairs, work in protective clothing, or stand in sun for hours. Heat safety therefore needs a work plan, not only a reminder to drink water.

## Reduce the workload before adding endurance
Schedule heavy tasks for cooler periods, create shade, shorten carries, use carts/teams, and rotate workers. New or returning workers may be less acclimatized than people who have been doing the task regularly; do not use yesterday’s performance as proof that a newcomer can match the same pace.

## Use planned breaks
Breaks should happen before a person becomes confused or collapses. During high heat/humidity, increase rest frequency, move rest into shade/cooling, and reduce heavy protective clothing where the hazard permits. A buddy should watch for unusual behavior, poor coordination, headache, nausea, weakness, cramps, dizziness, or cessation of effective work.

## Heat stroke is an emergency
Altered mental status, collapse, seizure, or severe overheating suggests a life-threatening emergency. Activate emergency services and begin rapid cooling using the methods supported by current first-aid/medical guidance while waiting. Do not leave the person alone or assume that drinking water is enough.

## Hydration is not unlimited water
Provide safe drinking water regularly, but do not force extreme volumes or treat all symptoms as simple dehydration. People with fluid restrictions or significant heart/kidney disease need their clinician’s emergency plan. Long work may also require normal food/electrolyte intake; avoid concentrated homemade mixtures.

## Log exposure
For community cleanup, record heat conditions if available, work/rest periods, who has medical limitations, and any symptoms. Stop the operation if multiple people become symptomatic; the work plan itself may be unsafe.
