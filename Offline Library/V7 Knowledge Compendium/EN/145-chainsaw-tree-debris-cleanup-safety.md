# Chain Saw and Fallen-Tree Cleanup After a Disaster

Fallen trees after storms, wildfire, or earthquakes can be under compression, tension, or torsion that is not obvious from appearance. A chain saw cuts quickly but can also release a bent limb, roll a trunk, strike a hidden object, or kick back. CDC recommends trained and experienced operators for disaster tree removal and appropriate protective equipment.

## Decide whether the job is household work at all
Do not cut trees or limbs touching or near power lines. Avoid unstable trees, hanging limbs, partially uprooted trunks, fire-weakened trees, and situations where the cut can move a structure or vehicle unexpectedly. If the operator cannot explain the escape path and what the wood is likely to do when the cut closes or opens, the task belongs with a trained professional.

## Prepare the saw and the work zone
Use the manufacturer’s instructions, correct chain tension/lubrication, functioning safety features, and PPE appropriate to chain-saw work. Keep bystanders out of the cutting zone. Clear stable footing and an escape route before starting. Do not work from a ladder with a saw or make overhead cuts as a casual cleanup shortcut.

## Fatigue changes the risk
Disaster cleanup encourages long hours and rushed decisions. Stop for loss of concentration, poor footing, heat stress, worsening weather, failing light, equipment faults, or unexpected movement in the tree. A job that looked simple can change when tension is released; pause and reassess rather than “finishing the cut quickly.”
