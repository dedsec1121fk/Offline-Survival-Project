# Volcanic ash and fine-particle disruption

Volcanic ash is not soft fireplace ash. Fine abrasive particles can reduce visibility, irritate eyes and airways, contaminate exposed water and food, clog filters and damage engines or electronics. Even when lava is nowhere nearby, ashfall can disrupt transport, power, water treatment and communications.

## Reduce exposure first

Follow official evacuation or shelter instructions. During ashfall, stay indoors when authorities advise it, close unnecessary openings and reduce the movement of outdoor dust into the cleanest room. If you must go outside, use the respiratory and eye protection recommended by authorities; people with asthma, COPD or other respiratory conditions should use their medical action plan and seek professional advice when symptoms worsen.

## Protect equipment and supplies

Cover clean water containers and food preparation surfaces. Keep ash away from open electronics, fans and mechanical intakes. Do not run a vehicle simply to “test” it in heavy ash: abrasive material can enter air systems, and visibility or road traction may be poor. Keep spare filters only if they match the equipment and follow the manufacturer’s replacement instructions.

## Cleaning without creating a dust cloud

Wait for official guidance on cleanup. Avoid aggressive dry sweeping that throws fine material back into breathing zones. Separate ash-contaminated clothing and shoes from sleeping and food areas. Roof loading can become a structural issue, especially when ash becomes wet; do not climb onto a roof that may be overloaded, slippery or damaged.

## Record what changed

Log ash depth or visible accumulation at a safe observation point, water status, filter condition, breathing symptoms, road access and official notices. This makes later decisions about relocation, cleaning and equipment use more defensible.

**Source anchors:** Ready.gov volcano guidance; local civil-protection, health and aviation/road authority instructions. The manual does not predict eruptions or replace official hazard zones.
