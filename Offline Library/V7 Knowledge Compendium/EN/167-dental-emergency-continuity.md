# Dental Emergencies During Isolation: Pain, Trauma, and Infection Boundaries

Dental problems can become operationally serious during isolation because pain interferes with sleep, eating, hydration, and concentration, while an infection can progress beyond the tooth. The goal of an offline guide is to preserve the tooth/tissue where reasonable, reduce additional injury, and identify situations that require urgent professional care.

## Trauma needs gentle handling
For a broken tooth or mouth injury, rinse gently with safe water and control bleeding with clean gauze/cloth and steady pressure when appropriate. Keep fractured pieces if easily recovered. A knocked-out permanent tooth is time-sensitive dental trauma; professional dental guidance should be sought immediately. Do not scrub the root or force a tooth back if you are unsure whether it is permanent, if the person is very young, or if the situation is unsafe.

## Swelling changes the urgency
Facial/neck swelling, difficulty breathing or swallowing, spreading redness, fever with severe dental pain, confusion, or rapid deterioration are not “wait until the roads reopen” problems. Escalate to emergency care. A localized toothache without these features still needs dental review, but the transport priority is different.

## Avoid unsafe improvisation
Do not place aspirin, caustic chemicals, fuel, alcohol, or household disinfectants directly on gums or teeth. Do not attempt home extraction. Use only pain medicines the person can normally take and exactly according to the labeled/prescribed directions; this project does not calculate doses.

## Prepare for access disruption
Keep dentist details, dental insurance/record information, denture/retainer cases, cleaning supplies, and spare essentials in the continuity register. People with dentures or orthodontic appliances should have a plan for cleaning with safe water and for sharp/broken components that injure soft tissue.
