# Infant and Child Feeding During Supply Disruption

Infants and young children have less margin for unsafe water, incorrect formula preparation and prolonged food disruption. Household plans should protect the child's normal feeding method and caregiver instructions rather than inventing emergency substitutions.

## Preserve the established plan
For breastfeeding families, support continued breastfeeding where possible by protecting the caregiver's safety, hydration, privacy and access to skilled support when problems occur. For formula-fed infants, maintain the exact type normally used unless a qualified clinician/public-health authority advises a change. Do not dilute formula to make it last longer.

## Water safety is part of formula safety
Use water that public-health guidance considers safe for infant feeding and follow the product/authority's preparation instructions. Clean hands and feeding equipment matter. In an emergency with unreliable water/sanitation, ready-to-feed liquid formula can reduce preparation-water risks when it is appropriate and available, but storage and individual clinical needs still matter.

## Keep enough small equipment
Plan clean feeding cups/bottles as appropriate, cleaning supplies, backup nipples/parts, a way to measure safely and enough product to bridge a realistic disruption. Record the child's normal schedule, allergies/intolerances, clinician contact and minimum reserve.

## Watch for illness/dehydration
Vomiting, diarrhea, poor intake or fewer wet diapers can become serious more quickly in infants. Seek medical advice promptly rather than using improvised electrolyte recipes or adult medications.
