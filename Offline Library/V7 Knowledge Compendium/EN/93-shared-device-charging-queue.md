# Shared charging station: allocate limited electricity without losing critical devices

## Rank devices by function
Separate life/safety and accessibility equipment, communications, lighting, navigation and convenience electronics. The order may change during the incident. A phone at 80% may not need charging while a radio or caregiver device is nearly empty.

## Label cables and ports
Keep compatible cables with each device and mark high-power/proprietary chargers. A shared box of similar black cables can waste time and cause incorrect charging. Reject damaged cords and connectors.

## Use time windows
Create a charging schedule tied to generator, solar or mains-restoration windows. Record start/end percentage and estimated energy used where the source can provide it. This helps the Power Operations ledger predict whether the reserve is lasting.

## Protect the station physically
Keep it dry, ventilated according to equipment instructions and away from bedding, exits, children’s play and combustible clutter. Do not stack hot chargers under clothing. Power strips, adapters and extension cords must remain within their ratings.

## Reserve one communication path
Avoid charging every device to full while leaving no energy for the final night. Designate a primary emergency phone/radio and preserve a minimum reserve. If a device can be turned off completely between check-in windows, do that rather than relying on low-power mode alone.

## Close each cycle
Return devices to their owners/roles and mark failures. A charger that intermittently disconnects should leave the emergency kit even if it sometimes works. Refill the cable/spare inventory after the incident.
