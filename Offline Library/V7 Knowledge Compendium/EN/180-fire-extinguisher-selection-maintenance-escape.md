# Fire Extinguisher Readiness: Selection, Maintenance, and Escape-First Use

A fire extinguisher is a limited tool for an early, small fire. It is not a reason to remain in smoke, block an exit, or fight a growing fire. Readiness means choosing an extinguisher suitable for local hazards, mounting it where it can be reached while moving toward an exit, maintaining it, and training before the emergency.

## Match the extinguisher to the hazard
Fire classifications and extinguisher labels vary by region, but the principle is universal: the extinguishing agent must be compatible with what is burning and with energized electrical equipment where relevant. Follow local fire-service/building requirements and the extinguisher label. Kitchen oils/fats can require specific extinguishing agents; water can be dangerous on burning oil or energized electrical equipment.

## Position for escape
Do not store the only extinguisher behind the likely fire source. Place suitable units on accessible escape paths according to local code/manufacturer guidance. Everyone should know the location, but children/untrained users should not be assigned fire-fighting duties.

## Maintenance is part of the tool
Inspect the pressure/status indicator where present, pin/seal, hose/nozzle, corrosion/damage, and access. Follow manufacturer/service intervals and replacement/inspection requirements. An extinguisher that is discharged, damaged, expired/out of service, or inaccessible behind stored boxes should be treated as unavailable.

## Fight only a small fire with a safe exit
Raise the alarm and ensure others are leaving. Use an extinguisher only if the fire is small/contained, smoke conditions allow safe breathing/visibility, you know how to use the unit, and you keep an unobstructed exit behind you. If one extinguisher does not control the fire immediately, leave; do not advance deeper into the building for a second attempt.
