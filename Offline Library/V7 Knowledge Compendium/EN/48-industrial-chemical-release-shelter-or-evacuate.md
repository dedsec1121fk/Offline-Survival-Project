# Industrial or chemical release: shelter, isolate, or evacuate only on current instructions

## The substance changes the correct action
A visible plume, unusual odour, industrial alarm or official alert does not justify one universal response. Some incidents require evacuation; others require sheltering indoors and reducing outside-air entry. Follow the current instruction from Civil Protection, fire or hazardous-material authorities or the facility emergency system rather than applying wildfire or smoke rules automatically.

## Shelter-in-place setup
If instructed to shelter, move people and animals indoors, close doors and windows, stop ventilation that draws outdoor air when authorities advise it, and move to the designated interior room. Keep radio or phone alerts available. Temporary sealing materials may reduce air exchange, but do not create a dangerous unventilated environment around combustion appliances. Never run a generator, grill or engine indoors.

## Do not investigate
Avoid walking or driving toward the plume to identify the source. Do not touch deposited material, unknown liquids or damaged containers. If exposed, follow the specific decontamination and medical instruction from authorities; different chemicals require different actions, so improvised neutralization can make an injury worse.

## Evacuation route direction
If evacuation is ordered, use the route and direction given by authorities. The shortest road on a map may pass through the affected plume or responder exclusion zone. Close the building as directed, take staged essential medicines and identification, account for everyone, and leave promptly.

## Information to record
Capture the time of the alert, named substance if officially confirmed, affected zone, protective action, ventilation setting, symptoms or exposure and every subsequent instruction. If someone develops breathing difficulty, altered consciousness or severe symptoms, contact emergency medical services and state that chemical exposure is possible.

## Source anchors
- Greek Civil Protection: general and technological or industrial hazard instructions.
- Local fire or hazardous-material response authority.
- Facility-specific emergency instructions when officially issued.
