# Greece: reading 112 alerts and reporting your location accurately

## Read the whole alert
A 112 warning is not merely a siren on the phone. It can contain the hazard, affected area, protective action and direction or destination. Stop, read the full text and identify whether you are inside the named area. Do not rely on a cropped screenshot forwarded by someone else when the original alert is available on your device.

## Location first in an emergency call
When contacting 112 for immediate help, give the location early: municipality or settlement, road or landmark, building/floor if relevant, and coordinates only when you can provide them reliably. Then state what happened, how many people are affected, immediate hazards and access constraints. If moving, say that you are moving and give the direction or route.

## Coordinate discipline
A phone can display coordinates, but format errors matter. Read latitude and longitude slowly and confirm them if the operator repeats them. A misplaced pin can matter in a remote, coastal or wildfire setting. Keep a written address and recognizable local landmark even when satellite positioning is available.

## Communication barriers
If someone may have difficulty speaking, hearing or understanding under stress, prepare a short emergency card in Greek and another language if needed. Include address, number of people, functional assistance needed, essential equipment and contact. Avoid private detail that does not change emergency response.

## After the call
Keep the phone available for callback and follow dispatcher instructions. If conditions change materially—fire cuts the access road, someone becomes unconscious, or the group relocates—update emergency services rather than assuming the first report remains accurate.
