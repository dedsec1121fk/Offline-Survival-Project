# Family reunification: design meeting points that still work when phones do not

## One meeting point is not enough
Choose an immediate point just outside the home for fire/rapid evacuation, a neighborhood point if the block cannot be reached, and an out-of-area contact who can relay status. Every family member capable of doing so should know the plan without opening an app.

## Describe locations unambiguously
“Meet at the school” is weak if the campus has several gates or is itself closed. Use a specific entrance, landmark or coordinate and define an alternate nearby location. Confirm that the point is accessible to the household’s mobility needs and safe for children to wait with a responsible adult.

## Define waiting rules
People need to know how long to remain, when to move to the alternate point and what message to leave if communications briefly work. Without a rule, two groups can repeatedly move past each other while both believe they are “looking for the family.”

## Use an out-of-area relay
A person outside the affected region may be easier to reach than nearby contacts during local network congestion. Give everyone the same relay contact and keep the number on paper. The relay should record who checked in, time, location and intended next step rather than interpreting rumors.

## Include schools, work and care settings
Know the reunification/authorized pickup procedures for children, care facilities and workplaces. Do not rush into a controlled site or ask a child to leave with someone not authorized. Emergency plans should list who is allowed to collect each dependent.

## Drill the plan without creating panic
Walk to the local points, verify accessibility and have family members explain the sequence back in their own words. Update after moves, school changes or road construction. Reunification is successful when people can follow it under stress without relying on memory fragments.
