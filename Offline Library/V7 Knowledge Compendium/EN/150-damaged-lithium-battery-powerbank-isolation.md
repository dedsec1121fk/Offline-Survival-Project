# Damaged Lithium-Ion Batteries and Power Banks: Stop-Use and Isolation Decisions

Portable batteries are valuable during outages, but a swollen, overheating, smoking, hissing, leaking, recalled, crushed, or water-damaged lithium-ion device should not be treated as a reduced-capacity battery. It may be a fire hazard whose condition can worsen after the initial damage.

## Recognize a stop-use condition
Remove a suspect device from charging and ordinary service when it shows abnormal swelling, heat, odor, smoke, popping/hissing, damaged casing, exposed cells, or a relevant safety recall. Do not puncture, squeeze, dismantle, or repeatedly “test charge” it. Keep people away and follow local fire/emergency guidance if there is active heating, smoke, or fire.

## Do not hide it in normal waste
CPSC warnings for defective lithium-ion power banks instruct users not to put hazardous batteries in household trash or ordinary recycling streams; disposal should follow the process for damaged/defective batteries available locally. Before transport, ask the receiving hazardous-waste/recycling service how they accept damaged lithium batteries rather than placing one loosely in a bag with metal objects.

## Protect the outage reserve from one failing pack
Store active backup packs so one overheating device does not sit against medicines, documents, bedding, fuel, or all other batteries. Log model/serial information and recalls. Rotate batteries by condition and tested capacity, not merely by purchase date.
