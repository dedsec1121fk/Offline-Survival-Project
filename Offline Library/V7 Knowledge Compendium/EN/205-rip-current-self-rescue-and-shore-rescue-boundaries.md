# Rip Currents: Self-Rescue and Shore-Rescue Boundaries

Rip currents move water away from shore and can rapidly exhaust a swimmer who tries to fight directly against the current. Prevention is stronger than rescue: use guarded beaches, obey flags/signs and stay out when conditions exceed your ability.

## If caught
Stay as calm as possible and conserve energy. Do not continuously fight straight toward shore against the current. Signal for help. Follow current local lifeguard/weather guidance for escaping the flow—often moving parallel to the shoreline or otherwise out of the narrow current before returning toward shore. If unable to escape, float/tread water and keep signaling rather than exhausting yourself.

## If you see someone in trouble
Call/alert the lifeguard or emergency services. From shore, throw or extend flotation when possible. Do not enter hazardous surf without training/equipment; untrained rescuers can become additional victims. Keep visual contact and point rescuers toward the person.

## After rescue
A person who inhaled water, lost consciousness or has persistent coughing/breathing problems needs medical assessment. Keep them warm and monitor while professional help is arranged.
