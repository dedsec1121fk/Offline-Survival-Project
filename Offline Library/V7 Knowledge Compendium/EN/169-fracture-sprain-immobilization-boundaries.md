# Suspected Fracture or Severe Sprain: Immobilization and Transport Boundaries

Falls, debris, stair evacuation, vehicle incidents, and cleanup work can cause limb injuries when professional care is delayed. The household objective is to prevent further injury and arrange appropriate assessment—not to force a diagnosis or “set” a bone.

## Start with the whole person
Severe bleeding, breathing problems, altered consciousness, major head/neck/back injury, crush injury, or signs of shock take priority over the limb. If the mechanism was high energy or the person cannot be moved safely, call emergency services and avoid unnecessary movement.

## Protect the injured position
For an obviously injured arm or leg, support it in the position found and limit movement. Remove rings, watches, or tight items early if swelling is developing and they can be removed without manipulating the injury. A trained first aider may use a splint, but do not straighten a visibly deformed limb or push exposed bone back into a wound.

## Check circulation and sensation
Before and after any support/splinting, note skin color/temperature, ability to move fingers/toes if appropriate, sensation, and the presence of circulation signs. Increasing numbness, a cold/pale/blue extremity, severe worsening pain, or loss of circulation is urgent.

## Open injury or contamination
Cover open wounds with a clean dressing and control bleeding without pressing directly on protruding bone. Floodwater, soil, or dirty debris contamination increases infection/tetanus concerns and should be reported to the clinician.

## Transport planning
A person who cannot bear weight safely may need assisted evacuation or professional transport. Do not turn an unstable staircase, motorcycle, or overloaded car into the second injury. Record time, mechanism, visible changes, and any first aid provided.
