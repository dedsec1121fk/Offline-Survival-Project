# Cooling or warming center route plan: make public sheltering reachable before it is needed

## A listed center is not automatically usable for your household
Verify opening status, hours, accessibility, transport, pet/service-animal policy and whether registration or identification is needed. Official centers can change during an event; use current authority information rather than an old saved address alone.

## Build a primary and backup
Choose at least two plausible destinations in different directions when geography permits. Record offline directions and approximate travel time. If one route passes through a flood-prone underpass, wildfire corridor or steep icy road, identify the alternative beforehand.

## Plan the last kilometre
A bus stop several blocks away may be impractical for an older adult, wheelchair user or person carrying medical equipment in extreme weather. Include the door-to-door segment, lift/elevator dependency and who can assist. Arrange a buddy or accessible transport contact early.

## Carry the arrival module
Bring identification as required, essential medicines, medical/accessibility information, charging cable, drinking water/snack if appropriate, and a small comfort layer. Do not move the entire household stock when the center is intended as temporary temperature refuge.

## Keep departure triggers conservative
Do not wait until someone is already ill from heat/cold to begin a difficult trip. Use forecasts, official warnings, indoor temperature trend and the person’s care plan to decide earlier. If emergency symptoms are present, seek emergency medical help rather than treating the center as a clinic.

## Reconfirm before travel
Call/check official channels when possible to verify the facility is still operating. Record the time of confirmation. During a widespread event, capacity or location may change.
