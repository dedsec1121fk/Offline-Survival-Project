# Flash Floods: Underpasses, Canyons, and Rapid-Rise Escape

Flash floods can develop faster than a household can move equipment. Low crossings, urban underpasses, drainage channels and narrow valleys concentrate water and should be treated as route hazards, not convenient shortcuts.

## Move early to higher ground
When a flash-flood warning or obvious rapid rise affects your location, move away from the flood path promptly. Do not go into a basement/underground garage to save a vehicle when water is entering. In a canyon or dry wash, rain can create dangerous flow even when the storm is not directly overhead.

## Never test floodwater with a vehicle or feet
Moving water can hide missing pavement, open drains, debris and electrical hazards. Turn around rather than using another vehicle's successful crossing as proof. Pedestrians should not wade into moving water to retrieve objects or animals; find a safer route or call rescue services.

## Plan urban escape vertically and horizontally
Know which nearby roads climb rather than descend into tunnels or river corridors. In a building, identify higher safe levels while preserving a route for rescue and avoiding roofs unless necessary for immediate life safety. Carry a light because power can fail in garages/stairs.
