# Utility shutoff map: know where controls are without practicing hazardous manipulation

## Identification comes before operation
Mark the location of the main water shutoff, electrical service/distribution area and any fuel/gas controls relevant to the property. Record who is authorized/competent to operate each and the utility/emergency contact. The map is for rapid identification; it is not a substitute for training or local rules.

## Learn normal indicators
Photograph labels and panel directories in normal conditions. Keep access clear. If a valve or breaker is already damaged, corroded, inaccessible or unlabeled, correct that during routine maintenance rather than discovering it during a flood or fire.

## Do not improvise on gas or damaged electrical systems
If there is suspected gas leak, fire, floodwater around electrical equipment, arcing, damaged service conductors or other serious hazard, evacuate/keep clear and contact emergency services or the utility according to local guidance. Do not enter a hazardous area merely to reach a shutoff.

## Water shutoff can limit secondary damage
Where it is safe and the household has been shown the correct control, stopping water after a major internal pipe failure may reduce damage. Keep the proper non-specialized tool accessible if the installation requires one, but never force a stuck valve during an emergency.

## Label the recovery side too
After any emergency shutoff, record who is allowed to restore service. Some systems require inspection or utility action before re-energizing/reopening. “I turned it off, so I can turn it back on” is not a universal rule.

## Drill by pointing, not by creating an outage
A household exercise can have each person identify the controls, exits and contacts without actually interrupting gas/electricity or life-support equipment. This builds orientation without introducing unnecessary hazard.
