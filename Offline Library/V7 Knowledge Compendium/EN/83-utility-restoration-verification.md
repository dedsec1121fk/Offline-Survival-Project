# Utility restoration: verify stability before rebuilding normal operations

## “Power is back” is not the end state
Electricity, water or communications may return intermittently after a major disruption. Bring critical systems back in stages. Observe for unusual smell, heat, leaks, low water pressure, repeated trips or other abnormal conditions rather than reconnecting every load immediately.

## Electricity: start with safety-critical checks
Keep wet or damaged electrical equipment out of service until evaluated. Reconnect essential low-risk loads first and watch the source. Large appliances and temporary extension systems should not all be restarted simultaneously. If protection devices trip repeatedly, stop and seek qualified help rather than resetting continuously.

## Water: confirm the advisory status
Restored pressure does not necessarily mean water is approved for drinking. Check the utility/public-health notice and follow any boil-water or do-not-use instruction. Flush, clean or treat only according to the current guidance for the event; keep emergency drinking water separate until clearance is explicit.

## Communications: expect congestion and stale information
When mobile data returns, cached pages and delayed messages can appear in confusing order. Verify timestamps and official sources. Use the restoration window to send concise status updates and download essential current instructions, not to exhaust batteries on nonessential traffic.

## Reset temporary systems deliberately
Close generator or battery arrangements according to their safe procedures, remove temporary cords, clean sanitation backups and move supplies back to normal storage only after the primary utility is dependable. A temporary system left half-connected becomes a future hazard.

## Record the real restoration time
Log when each service became stable—not merely the first moment it flickered on. This helps calculate food cold-chain exposure, battery usage and actual outage duration for after-action review.
