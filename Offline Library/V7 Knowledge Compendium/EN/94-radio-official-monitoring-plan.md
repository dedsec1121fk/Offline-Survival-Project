# Radio and official monitoring plan: listen on purpose instead of chasing every signal

## Define the sources before the incident
Record which national/local broadcasters, Civil Protection channels, municipality pages or emergency frequencies/services are authoritative for your area. A radio can receive information without mobile data, but the household still needs to know which station is credible.

## Schedule listening windows
Continuous listening consumes batteries and attention. Check at planned intervals, then increase frequency when warnings or rapidly changing hazards justify it. Log the time and source of important instructions so later messages can be compared accurately.

## Separate instruction from commentary
News discussion, callers and social posts may repeat unverified reports. Mark official instructions separately in the incident log. Do not change evacuation or shelter action based on a rumor when an authoritative channel is available.

## Prepare reception and power
Test the radio in the actual shelter area, not only outdoors on a clear day. Keep compatible batteries or charging method and know how to tune/store stations without internet. A hand-crank feature is a backup, not an excuse to ignore battery planning.

## Share one concise household update
After monitoring, the information lead should give the group the action, source and time—not a stream of alarming details. This reduces contradictory interpretations and conserves everyone’s phone batteries.

## Preserve the last confirmed instruction
If the signal disappears, keep the last verified instruction and its timestamp visible while seeking an update. Do not silently replace it with speculation. In Greece, 112 alerts and Civil Protection information are key official pathways during dangerous events.
