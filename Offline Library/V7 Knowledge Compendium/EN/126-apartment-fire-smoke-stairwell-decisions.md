# Apartment fire and smoke: make the exit decision from conditions and official building guidance

## Fire behavior in multi-unit buildings is not a simple house-fire copy
A fire may be inside your unit, elsewhere in the building, in a corridor or below you. Smoke in the common route can make an otherwise familiar staircase dangerous. Know the building’s fire instructions, alarm system and designated escape routes before an incident. Local fire-service instructions override a generic checklist.

## If the fire is in your dwelling, escape comes first
Alert others, use the planned exit if it is tenable and call emergency services from safety. Close doors behind you when possible to limit smoke/fire spread. Do not delay for property. Do not use an elevator unless the building/fire authority specifically provides an evacuation lift and instructs its use.

## Test the route without entering smoke
Before opening a door into a common area, look/listen/smell for signs of fire and follow recognized fire-safety guidance for checking whether the route is tenable. If the corridor or stairwell is smoke-filled or the route is unsafe, do not push through blindly. Use the building’s alternative/protected strategy and communicate your location to emergency services.

## Keep fire doors functional
Doors designed to limit smoke/fire spread must not be propped open for convenience during an outage or evacuation. Storage in corridors and stairs can narrow escape routes. Keep mobility aids positioned so they are accessible without obstructing others.

## Meeting and accountability
The household should have a meeting point outside and away from fire-service access. Report missing people; do not re-enter to search. In a larger building, a neighbor check should never delay a required evacuation or place someone into smoke.

## Recovery starts after clearance
Do not return because visible flames are gone. Wait for fire-service/building clearance, then consider smoke contamination, water damage, electrical damage and alarm replacement. Photograph losses only after the area is safe.
