# Portable Fuel Stove/Canister: Storage, Leaks, and Indoor Boundaries

Camping stoves and fuel canisters are useful equipment outdoors, but using them as improvised indoor kitchen/heating systems can create fire, explosion and carbon-monoxide hazards. Equipment labels and local fire rules define where a device may operate.

## Store fuel separately and cool
Keep canisters/cylinders away from heat, flame, direct sun and vehicle interiors that can become extremely hot. Protect valves from impact. Do not store leaking or heavily corroded containers with normal supplies.

## Check the connection before ignition
Use only the fuel type and connector intended by the manufacturer. Inspect seals/threads and stop if you smell fuel or hear leakage. Move away from ignition sources and ventilate according to product/emergency guidance. Never search for a leak with a flame.

## Outdoor means outdoor
A tent, closed balcony, garage with the door partly open, vehicle or small shelter may still trap combustion gases. Do not use outdoor-only fuel-burning appliances inside. Cooking in an outage should be organized around a genuinely safe outdoor location or equipment explicitly rated for the intended indoor use.

## Fire control
Keep the stove stable and clear of dry vegetation, fabric and children. Have an escape/fire-response plan. Do not move a burning fuel appliance unless its instructions and conditions make that safe.
