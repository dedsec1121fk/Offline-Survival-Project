# Marine Stings and Coastal First-Response Boundaries

A coastal emergency can combine heat, crowding, poor communications, and marine injury. Jellyfish, venomous fish/spines, sea urchins, or other organisms require different care, so the offline rule is to remove the person from immediate danger, prevent drowning, avoid unsupported universal remedies, and obtain local medical guidance for significant symptoms.

## Water safety comes first
Get the person to a safe place without creating another drowning victim. A rescuer should not enter dangerous surf, rocks, or current without appropriate skill/equipment. If the person has breathing difficulty, collapse, severe allergic symptoms, or altered consciousness, activate emergency services immediately.

## Do not treat every sting the same
Different marine organisms respond differently to heat, water type, spine removal, or topical treatments. Avoid applying an unverified mixture simply because it is popular online. If tentacles/spines are visible, avoid bare-hand contact and follow species/local medical guidance where known. Do not dig deeply for embedded spines in the field.

## Watch the whole person
Severe pain, rapidly spreading swelling, chest symptoms, breathing difficulty, vomiting with weakness, fainting, confusion, or extensive exposure needs urgent evaluation. A person with a history of anaphylaxis follows their prescribed emergency plan while emergency care is activated.

## Document the exposure
Record beach/location, approximate time, water conditions, visible organism description if known, and any first aid already used. In a mass-sting event, notify lifeguards/authorities so others can be warned rather than repeatedly treating isolated cases.

## Greece-specific preparation
On islands or remote beaches, know the nearest lifeguarded area, access point for ambulance/boat, coordinates, and the route back to road access before swimming. Keep footwear for rocky exits and a phone/radio in a dry container.

## Verification anchors
- Local lifeguard/emergency medical guidance and regional poison information.
- CDC/NIOSH envenomation principles: avoid destructive first-aid myths and seek prompt care for systemic symptoms.
