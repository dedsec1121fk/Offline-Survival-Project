# Animals in emergencies: pets, service animals, and small livestock

## If animals cannot go, the evacuation plan is incomplete
Pre-identify transport for every animal: carrier, crate, harness, lead, vehicle space and a destination that will accept the species. Keep identification, veterinary/medication information, recent photo, food, water, sanitation supplies and handling equipment together. Practice loading before an alarm; a frightened animal may behave very differently.

## Service animals are functional equipment and partners
Plan for the animal’s food, water, relief and veterinary needs while maintaining the handler’s access to the animal wherever legally and safely possible. Do not treat a service animal as ordinary luggage or separate it merely for convenience. Carry a backup method for a critical task when feasible because the animal can also become injured or stressed.

## Containment after structural damage
Earthquakes, fire and storms can break fences, doors and windows. Before releasing an animal into a familiar yard, inspect for glass, ash, contaminated water, downed wires, sharp metal and escape gaps. Keep animals away from floodwater and wildfire ash according to veterinary/public-health guidance.

## Small livestock
Know which animals can be moved, which shelter-in-place structure is available and how water/feed will be supplied if roads close. Do not begin an improvised large-animal evacuation after fire or flood has already cut the route. Coordinate with local veterinary, agricultural and civil-protection advice where relevant.

## Recordkeeping
The Dependents module can store species, equipment, responsible person, backup caregiver and essential supplies. Add microchip/registration information only if you are comfortable keeping it locally. During an evacuation, record which vehicle and destination has each animal so separation does not become an accountability problem.
