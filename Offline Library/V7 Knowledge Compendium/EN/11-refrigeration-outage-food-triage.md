# Refrigeration outage food triage

## Start the clock and keep doors closed
A power outage becomes a food-safety event as soon as reliable refrigeration is lost. Record when power failed and avoid opening the refrigerator or freezer except for a planned retrieval. CDC guidance states that an unopened refrigerator keeps food safe for about four hours, while a full freezer can hold a safe temperature for about 48 hours and a half-full freezer about 24 hours if the door remains closed. These are planning limits, not permission to ignore actual temperatures or local public-health advice.

## Use temperature history, not smell
Pathogenic bacteria may grow without changing a food’s appearance, smell, or taste. Do not taste questionable food to test it. A refrigerator or freezer thermometer gives much better evidence than intuition. If perishable food has been above the safe cold range for too long or the history is unknown, follow official discard guidance. Keep a separate “uncertain” area rather than returning a questionable package to normal stock.

## Prioritise opening strategy
If the outage is expected to be short, the best action may be doing nothing. If it will be longer, retrieve a planned group of items at once. Move ice packs or frozen bottles only if doing so does not require repeated door opening. If you have a cooler and a verified source of ice, use it for the highest-priority perishables; do not spend scarce fuel driving repeatedly for ice unless the overall situation justifies it.

## Special foods deserve explicit decisions
Infant food, medically necessary nutrition, allergy-safe items, and foods for immunocompromised household members may require stricter handling. Do not substitute a product with an unknown allergen profile simply because the safe option spoiled. Record these items separately in Food Operations so the household sees them before general calories.

## After power returns
Power restoration does not automatically prove that all food is safe. Review the outage duration, thermometer records, ice crystals in frozen items, and the current official food-safety guidance. Clean spills promptly and label any food that remains under review. If floodwater entered the appliance, use the separate flood-exposure decision process rather than ordinary outage rules.
