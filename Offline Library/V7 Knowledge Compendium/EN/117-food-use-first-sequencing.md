# Food use-first sequencing: turn a mixed pantry into a controlled consumption plan

## The first meal after an outage should be a decision, not a habit
Inventory food by risk and by dependency. Some items depend on refrigeration, some on freezer temperature, some on clean water, and some on cooking fuel. Eating shelf-stable food immediately while perishable food is approaching a safety limit can waste the most time-sensitive resource.

## Create four practical groups
Mark foods as: cold-chain priority, shelf-stable ready-to-eat, shelf-stable requiring water/heat, and special-diet/medical or infant food. Within the cold-chain group, use only items that remain within current food-safety guidance. “Use first” never means “eat questionable food before it spoils further.”

## Consider water and fuel as ingredients
Dry staples may look abundant but can consume significant safe water and cooking energy. A plan for pasta, rice or dried legumes must include the water and fuel required to prepare them safely. During a simultaneous water/power failure, reserve no-cook foods for periods when cooking would create an unacceptable fuel or ventilation burden.

## Protect restricted supplies
Allergy-safe, infant, medically appropriate or texture-modified foods should be labeled and separated from general use. Count them against the needs of the person who depends on them rather than total household calories. If a substitute is not clinically or nutritionally appropriate, “we have plenty of food” is not an adequate continuity statement.

## Sequence by evidence
Use recorded appliance temperatures and outage duration to decide cold-chain items. Then choose opened shelf-stable packages before sealed long-storage stock when safe. Keep an unopened strategic reserve for a later phase of the incident instead of emptying every package during the first day.

## Close the loop
Update quantities after each meal cycle. Record discarded food separately so it is not accidentally counted as stock. When resupply occurs, refill the consumed category and rotate dates. A good food ledger tells the next shift what is genuinely available, not what the household purchased last month.
