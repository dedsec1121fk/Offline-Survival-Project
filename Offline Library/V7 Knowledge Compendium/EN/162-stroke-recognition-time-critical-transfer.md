# Stroke Recognition and Time-Critical Transfer

Stroke is a time-critical emergency. The household task is not to determine the stroke type; it is to recognize sudden neurological change, record when the person was last known well, call emergency medical services, and avoid delays caused by waiting for symptoms to improve.

## Sudden changes matter
CDC lists sudden weakness or numbness of the face, arm, or leg—especially on one side—along with sudden speech/confusion, vision difficulty, balance/walking problems, or a severe unexplained headache as stroke warning signs. A transient episode can still be a medical emergency; there is no safe household method for distinguishing a temporary ischemic event from a major stroke at the beginning.

## Record the clock, not a guess
Write down the exact time symptoms were first noticed and, if different, the last time the person was known to be normal. If symptoms are discovered after sleep, record when the person was last seen well. This is more useful to clinicians than an estimate created later under stress.

## Call rather than self-triage
Use the emergency medical system. Do not wait for a relative, routine appointment, or online search. Do not give food, drink, or unprescribed medicine to “test” whether the person improves. If the person has a prescribed emergency plan for another condition, tell dispatch/responders, but do not assume familiar symptoms explain away a possible stroke.

## Prepare the handoff
Bring identification, a concise medication/allergy list, known medical history, and the recorded onset/last-known-well time if these can be obtained without delaying care. Note whether the person uses anticoagulants or has had recent surgery/bleeding only if already known; do not search the home for paperwork while transport is waiting.

## Offline readiness
Keep a printed medical card and emergency contact sheet where another person can find them. A person living alone should include a plan for unlocking/access, pets, and medication information so responders are not delayed by avoidable logistics.
