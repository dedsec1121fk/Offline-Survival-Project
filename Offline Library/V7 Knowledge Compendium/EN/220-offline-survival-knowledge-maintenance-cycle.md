# Offline Survival Knowledge Maintenance Cycle

An offline guide becomes dangerous if nobody notices that phone numbers, medication plans, maps, equipment manuals or official safety guidance have changed. Treat the knowledge base as maintained equipment, not a book written once.

## Separate timeless principles from update-sensitive facts
Principles such as avoiding floodwater or keeping generators outside are relatively stable; evacuation zones, contact numbers, local shelters, medicine instructions, road maps, device firmware/manuals and legal requirements may change. Mark update-sensitive records with the date/source last verified.

## Quarterly household review
Open the project without internet and attempt the real tasks: find the emergency card, use the default phone browser, retrieve a route, open the medicine list, export a brief, locate the water chapter and read a Library document. Fix anything that depends on a forgotten password, missing app, dead battery or cloud-only file.

## Annual authoritative refresh
When normal internet is available, re-check the source catalog against official civil-protection, public-health, weather, utility and medical/equipment sources. Replace superseded instructions rather than appending contradictory notes. Run translation and duplicate audits after every content update.

## Preserve multiple forms
Keep the project on the phone, another local storage device and a small printed core for the few procedures needed when electronics fail. Test backups by restoring them; a backup that has never been opened is an assumption.

## Release rule
A larger guide is not automatically a better guide. Reject content that cannot name the decision it improves, repeats another chapter, invents clinical/engineering detail, or cannot be maintained in both languages.
