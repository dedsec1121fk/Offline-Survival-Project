# High-rise utility outage: plan for stairs, water pressure and vertical dependency

## Height changes ordinary preparedness
In a tower or multi-storey apartment building, loss of power can stop lifts, pumps, access systems, corridor lighting and sometimes water delivery to upper floors. A household that can manage a three-day outage at ground level may be unable to carry the same water, equipment or dependent person up many flights of stairs.

## Know the building systems before failure
Identify stairwells, alternative exits, refuge/accessibility arrangements, fire doors, emergency lighting, building management contacts and where official notices are posted. Never assume a lift will be available for evacuation. Residents who cannot use stairs need a building-specific assistance plan developed before the emergency, consistent with local fire and accessibility rules.

## Water is a vertical logistics problem
Stored water is heavy. Keep a sensible reserve inside the dwelling before an outage instead of planning repeated stair carries after pumps fail. Divide the reserve into containers that can be safely handled. If building management provides temporary distribution at ground level, assign carriers, protect stairs from spills and do not exceed individual lifting ability.

## Entry systems can fail
Electronic locks, intercoms and garage gates may behave differently without power. Know the lawful/manual emergency procedures and keep required physical keys accessible. Do not wedge fire doors open to make movement easier; that can defeat smoke compartmentation.

## Reduce unnecessary trips
Coordinate errands between households where appropriate. One descent for verified information or supplies is safer than repeated independent journeys in dark stairs. Keep the mobile phone/radio reserve for essential communication and use the building check-in system to identify residents needing assistance.

## Decide early when the building is no longer workable
Dangerous indoor temperature, loss of safe water, smoke, repeated aftershocks/structural concerns, fire-system failure or inability to support power-dependent care may justify relocation under official guidance. The correct decision can be leaving before stairs become harder, not proving the household can remain indefinitely.
