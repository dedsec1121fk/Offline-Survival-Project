# Apartment water storage: balance litres, lifting, floor space and contamination control

## The largest container is rarely the best answer
Water is heavy and apartment storage is constrained by floor space, stairs and the ability of household members to move it. Build the reserve from multiple manageable containers instead of one tank nobody can relocate. Keep the total distributed in sensible locations rather than concentrated on a weak shelf or unstable furniture.

## Separate daily access from strategic reserve
Keep a small working supply in the kitchen and a larger sealed reserve in a protected location. This reduces repeated opening of the long-term stock. If the apartment has several levels, consider where water will actually be needed when lifts or pumps fail.

## Protect from household contaminants
Do not store drinking water beside fuels, solvents, pesticides, cleaning chemicals or flood-prone floor drains. Keep containers closed, labeled and away from direct sun/excess heat. A leak should not be able to reach electrical equipment or destroy documents.

## Plan carrying before the outage
If resupply will come from a building lobby or public point, determine who can carry which container and how many stair trips are realistic. A 20-litre container may be an excellent storage unit for one household and unusable for another. Include a small trolley only if stairs/terrain permit it; do not rely on an elevator during a power failure.

## Track potable and utility water separately
Water collected for toilet flushing or cleaning must never become indistinguishable from drinking water. Use different containers or prominent labels. The Command Center water-batch ledger can record source and treatment but physical separation in the home remains essential.

## Review the reserve after a real drill
Carry one day’s planned volume through the actual route, open the containers, dispense water and replace them. Note leaks, grip problems and inaccessible storage. A theoretically sufficient number of litres is not preparedness if nobody can safely use them.
