# Tools and spare parts: store the failures you can safely fix, not a fantasy workshop

## Start from actual household systems
List the equipment whose failure would materially affect water, shelter, transport, communications or power. Record the model and the small consumables that normally fail: fuses specified by the manufacturer, flashlight cells, bicycle tubes, filter elements, approved charging cables, fasteners, tape, seals or hand tools already used for routine maintenance.

## Separate repair from hazardous work
Owning a tool does not make electrical panels, gas systems, damaged structures, pressurized equipment or contaminated machinery safe to work on. Mark tasks that require a licensed/qualified professional and keep the relevant service contact with the equipment record. Emergency urgency is not permission to bypass safety systems.

## Test compatibility before the incident
A spare part with the wrong connector, thread, voltage or size is dead inventory. Whenever practical, fit or test a spare during normal maintenance, then return a fresh/verified replacement to stock. Label parts with the device they belong to instead of relying on memory.

## Keep a field repair module small
For evacuation, carry only tools that support likely route/transport problems and can be used competently. A heavy box of general tools can displace water, medication and weather protection. The larger home tool set can remain organized by system.

## Track consumption
When a repair uses the last fuse, patch, filter or fastener, update the inventory immediately. Reorder before the item disappears from memory. Some spares age even when unused, so include inspection dates for adhesives, rubber, batteries and other degradable items.

## Preserve manuals offline
Save the manufacturer instructions for critical equipment locally. A spare part is more useful when the torque, installation, safety and troubleshooting instructions are available without internet access. Do not reproduce proprietary service procedures inaccurately inside the survival guide; keep the official manual alongside the equipment.
