# Food allergies in shared sheltering: prevent accidental exposure in a high-noise environment

## Identification must be specific
A statement such as “food allergy” is not enough for food distribution. Record the allergen, the person, usual emergency action plan and any prescribed rescue medication according to their clinician’s plan. Do not improvise medication instructions inside a shelter guide.

## Preserve labels
Keep original ingredient/allergen labels with packaged foods. Do not transfer unknown food into unmarked containers. Home-cooked or donated dishes with uncertain ingredients should be treated as unknown for an allergic person rather than accepted on reassurance alone.

## Separate preparation and utensils where needed
Cross-contact can occur through cutting boards, serving utensils, hands and shared spreads/condiments. Establish a protected preparation area or sealed meal route for people with serious allergies. Clean surfaces appropriately before use and store the safe food where it will not be consumed accidentally by others.

## Communicate without publicizing medical history
The people serving food need to know which meal belongs to whom; the entire shelter does not need the person’s full medical details. Use a discreet identifier agreed with the individual/family and keep the emergency plan with the caregiver or designated coordinator.

## Never pressure someone to “eat around” an uncertain ingredient
Scarcity does not make an unsafe food acceptable. Plan reserve foods that the person already tolerates and include them in evacuation supplies. If safe food becomes unavailable, escalate early to shelter staff, health services or supply coordinators rather than waiting until the next meal.

## Treat a reaction as a medical emergency according to the person’s plan
Follow the prescribed action plan and seek emergency help. A preparedness manual should support recognition, communication and logistics; it should not replace individualized medical instructions.
