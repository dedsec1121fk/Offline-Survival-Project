# CPR and AED Readiness: Recognition, Action, and Responder Handoff

A survival library cannot replace hands-on CPR training, but it can help a household prevent the most common preparedness failure: having an AED nearby, emergency numbers available, and nobody who knows where the equipment is or what to do when a person suddenly becomes unresponsive.

## Prepare before the emergency
Know where the nearest AEDs are in places you use repeatedly: apartment common areas, workplaces, gyms, schools, ferries, hotels, community buildings, or shelters. Keep first-aid/CPR training current for more than one household member. If a home AED is present, record its model, pad expiry dates, battery status, storage location, and the people trained to use it. Do not bury it beneath other supplies.

## If someone becomes unresponsive
First make sure the scene is safe. Check responsiveness and normal breathing according to the CPR training you have received. Activate emergency medical services immediately and have someone bring an AED if available. If the person is not breathing normally, begin CPR according to your training. The American Red Cross teaches adult chest compressions at 100–120 per minute and emphasizes firm, continuous compressions with full chest recoil; trained rescuers should use the current technique appropriate to the person's age and condition.

## Let the AED guide the shock decision
Turn on the AED and follow its spoken/visual prompts. The device analyzes the rhythm and decides whether a shock is advised. Keep people clear during analysis and shock delivery. Resume CPR when the device instructs you. Do not delay basic resuscitation while searching for obscure settings or trying to diagnose the rhythm yourself.

## Build a useful responder handoff
One person should track the approximate collapse time, when CPR started, whether an AED shock was delivered, known medical conditions, medications/allergies if readily available, and changes observed. Hand this information to responders without interrupting lifesaving care.

## What this chapter deliberately does not do
It does not substitute for certified CPR/AED training, pediatric-specific instruction, professional dispatch guidance, or emergency medical care. Practice with a training manikin/AED trainer if possible; reading alone is not sufficient skill preparation.
