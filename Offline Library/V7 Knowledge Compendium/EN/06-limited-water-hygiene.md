# Hygiene when clean water is limited

## Prioritise the health-critical uses
A water shortage is not simply a drinking problem. Hand hygiene, food preparation, oral care, wound care, toilet cleaning, infant care, and some medical equipment can all depend on safe water. Make a use hierarchy before the shortage becomes severe. Protect drinking and medically necessary water first, then allocate other water according to the actual contamination risk of each task rather than splitting the reserve evenly.

## Create clean and dirty work zones
In a small apartment or shelter, designate one surface for clean water containers and prepared food, and another for waste, used washing water, dirty shoes, pet equipment, and cleaning tools. The physical separation can be only a few metres, but it should be obvious. Colour tape, labels, or different containers reduce the chance that a person under stress fills a clean bottle from the wrong bucket.

## Hand hygiene under constraint
Use safe running water and soap when available. Where water is limited, follow current public-health guidance on alcohol-based hand sanitizer for appropriate situations, while remembering that sanitizer does not remove every type of contamination and is not a replacement when hands are visibly dirty or contaminated by certain hazards. Preserve enough clean water for washing after toilet use, before food handling, and for caregiving tasks where contamination consequences are highest.

## Reuse grey water cautiously
Water used for washing may have limited secondary uses, but only where local health guidance allows it and where it cannot be confused with drinking water. Never store grey water in a container normally used for drinking without unmistakable labelling. Avoid using contaminated wash water where aerosols, splashing, or contact with food surfaces can spread pathogens.

## Monitor depletion by task
Instead of recording only “litres remaining,” note the major uses each day. A sudden increase may reveal a leak, diarrhoeal illness, heat stress, or sanitation failure. That makes the water log an early-warning tool rather than a passive inventory.
