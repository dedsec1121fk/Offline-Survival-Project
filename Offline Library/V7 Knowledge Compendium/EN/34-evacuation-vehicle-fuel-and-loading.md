# Evacuation vehicle: range, loading, and departure readiness

## The vehicle is part of the evacuation system
A car that starts is not automatically evacuation-ready. Track fuel or charge, tyre condition, coolant/fluids where applicable, lights, spare/repair equipment, seasonal needs and whether every required person, child restraint, mobility device and animal carrier can actually fit.

## Protect range
Set a minimum fuel/charge threshold before an incident. During a threat, avoid unnecessary idling and trips that consume the reserve needed to leave. Route planning must include congestion, detours and the possibility that the closest fuel/charging point has no power or supply. Electric vehicles need the same discipline: a nominal range estimate is not a guarantee under heat, cold, traffic, elevation or heavy load.

## Load in priority order
First load people-specific life-safety items: medicines, mobility/communication equipment, identification and essential animal equipment. Next load water, compact food, communications, lighting and weather protection. Bulky replaceable belongings should not displace safety equipment or obstruct visibility and exits. Secure heavy objects so a sudden stop does not turn them into projectiles.

## Two-minute departure check
Account for every person and animal; confirm keys, destination, primary/alternate route, fuel/charge, phones/radio and one contact outside the immediate area. Record which vehicle is carrying critical medicines or equipment if a group uses multiple cars. Keep vehicles together only when safe; rigid convoy behaviour can create delays at closures.

## If the vehicle fails
Have a transfer plan: another household vehicle, neighbour/mutual-aid contact, accessible transport, taxi/public transport if authorities say it is operating, or an emergency-service notification for someone who cannot self-evacuate. A plan that assumes the only car never fails has one obvious point of failure.
