# Greece: household readiness for wildfire season

## Start before the high-risk day
Greek wildfire preparedness begins with the area around the property, access for responders, reliable contact details and awareness of daily restrictions or risk conditions. Follow the current Ministry for Climate Crisis and Civil Protection, Fire Service and municipal instructions for vegetation management, prohibited activities and local prevention measures; requirements can change by season and location.

## Address and access
Make sure every household member can describe the property location clearly, including settlement, road, nearby landmark and any access complication. Rural homes should consider whether a fire engine can pass the gate/road and whether parked vehicles, branches or stored material obstruct the route. Keep house numbers or location markings visible where practical.

## Evacuation destinations in two directions
Many Greek settlements have limited road networks. Choose at least two destinations that do not depend on the same narrow road or forest corridor. An island or mountainous community may have a port or main highway that is itself affected by smoke, fire or congestion. Record a nearby local refuge option as well as a farther relocation option, following official directions during the event.

## Heat and smoke together
Wildfire evacuation often occurs during extreme heat. Add drinking water, hats/light clothing, prescribed respiratory medicines and support for older adults to the vehicle plan. A person with respiratory disease may need to leave smoke earlier even before the fire front threatens the property; discuss individual thresholds with their clinician.

## 112 discipline
Keep emergency alerts enabled and read the entire 112 instruction. An alert can specify a geographic area, direction of movement or protective action. Do not treat a social-media map as more authoritative than a newer official message. If instructed to evacuate, act promptly rather than waiting for visual confirmation of flames.
