# Home Fire Extinguisher Decision: Escape Route First

A fire extinguisher is not permission to fight a growing fire. U.S. Fire Administration guidance places escape conditions ahead of extinguisher use: people should already be alerted, emergency services contacted as appropriate, the fire must be small/contained enough for the extinguisher, and the user needs a clear escape route.

## Make the exit non-negotiable
Never position yourself with the fire between you and the exit. If smoke is building, flames are spreading, the correct extinguisher is unavailable, the user is unsure, or one discharge does not control the fire, leave and close doors if this can be done safely. Do not re-enter for possessions or to “check whether it went out.”

## Prepare before there is a fire
Know where extinguishers are, their intended fire classes, inspection status, and how the manufacturer says to operate them. Keep them accessible without placing them directly next to the most likely fire source, where flames could prevent access. Everyone still needs the escape plan and meeting point; an extinguisher is secondary equipment.

## After discharge
Even a small controlled fire can leave hidden heat, electrical damage, smoke contamination, or structural damage. Contact the appropriate fire/utility/professional service before returning affected equipment or areas to normal use.
