# Home Fire: Smoke Escape, Door Check, and Meeting Point

A home fire can become unsurvivable quickly. The household plan should prioritize immediate escape, closed doors where appropriate, crawling low when smoke conditions require it, and a fixed meeting point outside—not fighting the fire.

## Know two ways out when possible
Keep exits, keys and stairs usable. Practice an alternate route from bedrooms and identify who assists children, older adults, disabled people or animals without delaying everyone. Security bars/locks must have a safe emergency-release plan consistent with local fire code.

## Treat smoke as the main route problem
If smoke blocks the normal route, use the alternate if it is safe. Stay low beneath smoke as taught by fire-safety authorities. Before opening a closed door on an escape route, check it according to fire-service guidance; if heat/smoke indicates fire on the other side, use the alternate route and keep the door closed.

## Once outside, stay outside
Meet at the preselected point so responders know whether someone is missing. Call emergency services from outside. Do not re-enter for property or animals. Tell firefighters exactly who may be inside and the last known location.
