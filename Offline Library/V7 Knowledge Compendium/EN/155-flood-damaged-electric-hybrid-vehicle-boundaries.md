# Flood-Damaged Electric and Hybrid Vehicles: High-Voltage Boundaries

A flooded electric or hybrid vehicle is not just a wet car. High-voltage batteries and components can create shock and fire hazards, and damage may not be visible immediately. NHTSA advises owners to contact the dealer and/or emergency services when flood exposure may have damaged an EV battery.

## Do not treat the vehicle as emergency power
After significant flood exposure, do not plug in, charge, open the high-voltage system, or use vehicle-to-load functions until the vehicle has been evaluated under manufacturer/professional guidance. Keep people away from damaged battery areas and note smoke, unusual heat, odor, popping sounds, warning messages, or fluid leakage for responders.

## Parking location matters
NHTSA flood-damaged vehicle guidance warns that a damaged lithium-ion vehicle can pose a fire risk. Follow current official/manufacturer instructions about where the vehicle should be stored or towed; do not move it into an attached garage merely to protect it from weather. Tell tow/recovery personnel that it is an EV/hybrid with flood exposure.

## Document before recovery
Photograph water level, exterior damage, VIN/vehicle identity, dashboard warnings if visible without entering an unsafe vehicle, and the time/location of flooding. Keep repair, towing, insurer, and manufacturer communications together so a later shift does not attempt to start or charge a vehicle that another person already isolated.
