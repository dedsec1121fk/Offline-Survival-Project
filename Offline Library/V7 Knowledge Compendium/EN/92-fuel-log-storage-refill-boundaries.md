# Emergency fuel log: track quantity, compatibility, storage and safe refill decisions

## Fuel is a hazardous resource, not only an energy number
Record fuel type, approved container, quantity, storage location, purchase/rotation date and the equipment it serves. Gasoline, diesel, LPG and other fuels are not interchangeable. Follow local law, fire code, building rules and manufacturer instructions for storage limits and locations.

## Keep fuel out of occupied/storage-sensitive areas
Do not store fuel with food, drinking water, medicines or inside living/sleeping spaces. Containers must be suitable, closed and protected from heat/ignition. Apartment buildings and shared properties may have stricter restrictions; those rules override a generic preparedness plan.

## Separate consumption from reserve
Log each refill into the generator/vehicle/tool and the remaining amount. Define a protected reserve for evacuation or critical use rather than letting comfort loads consume it unnoticed. Fuel estimates should include realistic engine/generator consumption, not the label’s best-case figure alone.

## Refuel only under safe conditions
Follow equipment instructions for shutdown, cooling and refueling. Never refuel a hot/running generator or create vapors near ignition sources. Spills require appropriate cleanup and ventilation; do not wash fuel into drains or soil.

## Rotate without stockpiling indefinitely
Fuel properties and container seals change with time. Use a rotation schedule suitable for the fuel/product and local guidance. Remove leaking, swollen, corroded or damaged containers from service safely rather than transferring risk to a new location.

## Reconcile after the incident
Compare actual fuel use with the power/vehicle logs. If the reserve disappeared faster than expected, identify the load or travel that consumed it. Improve the demand plan before simply increasing stored quantity.
