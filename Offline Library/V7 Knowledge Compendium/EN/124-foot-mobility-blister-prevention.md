# Feet and walking mobility: protect the evacuation system you carry on your body

## Foot problems become transport problems
A small hot spot, wet sock or poorly fitting shoe can become a reason someone cannot continue walking. During evacuation, inspect feet at planned stops rather than waiting for pain to become severe. People with diabetes, circulation problems or reduced sensation should follow their clinician’s foot-care plan and avoid self-treatment that could create injury.

## Start with tested footwear
Emergency footwear should already fit with the socks normally used. A brand-new pair stored untouched in a go-bag may create problems on the first long walk. Keep laces, closures and soles in good condition and include an alternative if one person depends on specialized footwear or orthotics.

## Keep feet dry and manage friction early
Change wet socks when possible, air feet during safe rests and remove grit from shoes. Address a friction “hot spot” before the skin breaks using a familiar protective method suitable for the person. Do not cut, drain or aggressively treat a blister when you cannot maintain clean wound care.

## Load affects the feet
An overloaded pack changes gait and increases fatigue. Remove unnecessary weight before trying to solve the resulting pain with more padding. Distribute shared water/equipment according to people’s actual carrying ability and update the route plan if pace falls below what the evacuation window requires.

## Build rest into the route
Choose stops where people can sit safely, inspect footwear, drink and reassess. Long continuous walking without a check can hide a developing problem until the group is far from alternatives. Record anyone whose mobility is deteriorating and move them earlier to available transport if appropriate.

## Escalate injuries
Deep wounds, signs of infection, inability to bear weight, major swelling, loss of sensation or significant trauma need professional medical evaluation. The operational objective is to preserve mobility without turning minor discomfort into a more serious injury.
