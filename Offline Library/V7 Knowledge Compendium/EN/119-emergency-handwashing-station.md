# Emergency handwashing station: create a clean flow without wasting safe water

## Handwashing is an infrastructure function
When taps fail, simply storing water does not create a usable hygiene system. People need a way to dispense water without contaminating the container, collect runoff, keep soap dry, and know which water is approved for the task. Place the station near sanitation/food areas but far enough away that wastewater cannot splash clean supplies.

## Use controlled dispensing
A container with a tap or other hands-free/low-touch dispensing arrangement conserves water better than dipping cups into an open bucket. Keep the drinking-water supply separate unless that is the only approved safe source. Label any nonpotable hygiene water clearly and follow local public-health instructions about what water quality is acceptable for handwashing during the incident.

## Build a clean-to-dirty sequence
Approach with dirty hands, operate the dispenser without reaching into stored water, use soap, rinse, dry with a clean method and leave without touching contaminated handles where possible. Provide a waste bin for used towels. If the station serves a sick-room or sewage-cleanup area, it should not become the household’s food-preparation wash point.

## Manage the greywater
Collect or direct runoff so it does not create slippery floors, standing water, insect breeding or contamination near stored supplies. In apartments, never discharge improvised greywater where it can leak to lower units or damage electrical areas. Disposal must follow local sewer/environmental restrictions.

## Maintain the station
Refill from a known source, clean the outlet and catch basin, replenish soap and inspect for leaks. Assign someone to this task during a prolonged disruption. A dry soap bottle beside an empty water container is not a functioning handwashing station.

## Keep sanitizer as a complement, not a universal replacement
Alcohol-based hand sanitizer can be useful when soap and water are not immediately available, but visibly dirty hands and some contamination situations still call for washing. Follow current CDC/public-health guidance and product labels.
