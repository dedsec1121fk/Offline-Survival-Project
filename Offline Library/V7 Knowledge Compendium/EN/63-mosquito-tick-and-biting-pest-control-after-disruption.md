# Mosquito, tick and biting-pest control after disruption

Flooding, damaged screens, outdoor sleeping and interrupted waste collection can increase contact with mosquitoes, ticks and other pests. The goal is to reduce breeding places and human exposure without creating new chemical or fire hazards.

## Remove avoidable breeding water

When it is safe, empty or cover small containers that hold stagnant water: buckets, trays, discarded packaging and uncovered storage. Do not empty contaminated water into food, play or drinking-water areas. Larger drainage or flood problems may require municipal action rather than household improvisation.

## Build a physical barrier

Repair screens, use intact bed nets where appropriate, close sleeping areas and choose clothing that reduces exposed skin when conditions allow. Inspect people and animals after travel through vegetation according to local health guidance. Keep sleeping equipment away from refuse, standing water and animal waste.

## Use repellents and pesticides correctly

Follow the product label and local health authority. More product is not automatically more protection. Keep chemicals away from food, drinking-water containers and children, and never burn or mix products unless the label specifically instructs it. For pets, use only products approved for that species; some substances safe for people or dogs can harm other animals.

## Track illness patterns

Note fever, rash or other concerning symptoms and recent bites/travel for a clinician if illness develops. Do not attempt to diagnose a vector-borne disease from a bite mark alone. During an outbreak, official public-health instructions override household routines.

**Source anchors:** CDC vector/disaster health guidance and local public-health/pest-control instructions.
