# Shelf-stable meals when electricity and cooking fuel are limited

## Design meals around what does not need rescue
During a prolonged outage, the most reliable food is food that remains safe without refrigeration and does not require scarce fuel or unsafe indoor combustion. Build several “no-cook” meal combinations from foods your household already tolerates: shelf-stable protein, grains or crackers, fruit or vegetables, and a safe drink. Avoid stockpiling foods that require large amounts of water or long boiling if water and fuel are likely to be the limiting resources.

## Open-package size matters
A large container may be economical in normal life but inefficient during an outage if it requires refrigeration after opening. Smaller units can reduce waste, especially for milk substitutes, sauces, spreads, and medically important foods. Record whether a product becomes perishable after opening and plan the meal so the whole package is used safely.

## Food safety does not pause during a disaster
Keep clean utensils and hands separate from waste and raw foods. Do not leave prepared shelf-stable food sitting for long periods in heat merely because the ingredients began at room temperature. Follow normal safe-food principles and local health guidance. If water for washing is scarce, choose meals that create fewer dirty utensils rather than lowering hygiene standards.

## Nutrition planning is broader than calories
Calories help estimate endurance, but an emergency menu also needs protein sources, appropriate foods for children, fibre, familiar foods that reduce stress, allergy-safe options, and any medically directed diet. The project’s food-energy calculator intentionally does not prescribe a universal daily intake. Use it as stock arithmetic, then build the real menu around the people present.

## Build a three-layer pantry
Keep a rapid-access layer for the first day, a routine shelf-stable layer for several days, and a reserve layer that is not casually opened. Rotate normal household consumption through the stock so emergency food is not a forgotten box of expired items. Include a manual can opener wherever cans are stored.
