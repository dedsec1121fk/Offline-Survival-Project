# Lithium Battery Everyday Charging and Storage Safety

Rechargeable lithium batteries make phones, power banks, radios and tools extremely useful during outages, but normal charging practices matter long before a battery is visibly damaged. Treat heat, swelling, unusual odor, hissing, leaking, smoke or a sudden change in behavior as stop-use signs rather than something to “test one more time.”

## Set up a safer charging area
Charge on a stable, nonflammable surface with airflow around the device. Keep charging equipment away from bedding, paper piles, curtains and direct sun. Use a charger/cable intended for the device and retire connectors that are crushed, loose, scorched or intermittently arcing. Do not cover a charging device to keep it warm or hide its light.

## Watch temperature and behavior
Some warmth during charging can be normal; rapidly increasing heat or behavior that is new for that device is not a reason to keep charging unattended. Disconnect from power if it can be done safely. A swollen battery should not be punctured, compressed, bent or forced back into a case. Follow local hazardous-battery disposal guidance rather than putting damaged lithium cells into ordinary household waste.

## Store for continuity, not neglect
Rotate power banks through periodic function checks. Record capacity, last test date and which critical device each one supports. Avoid storing packs where summer vehicle temperatures or freezing conditions are routine. Separate spare batteries from loose conductive metal that could short terminals.

## Incident boundary
If a battery is smoking, venting or on fire, prioritize people, evacuation and emergency services. Do not create a second injury by handling a hot or unstable cell. A battery incident can re-ignite; do not assume that visible flame stopping means the hazard is over.
