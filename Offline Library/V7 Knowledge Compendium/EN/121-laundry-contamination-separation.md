# Laundry after contamination or utility loss: keep dirty textiles from spreading the problem

## Decide whether washing is appropriate
Ordinary worn clothing, flood-soiled textiles, sewage-contaminated fabric and chemically contaminated clothing are not the same problem. Some material should not be handled or washed at home. Follow public-health, hazardous-material or remediation instructions for the incident before putting contaminated items into a normal washing machine.

## Establish a dirty textile boundary
Use a labeled bag/bin near the place clothing is removed. Do not shake heavily contaminated textiles because this can spread dust, mold or other material. Keep them away from bedding, towels used for hygiene and food areas. If bags leak, place them in a secondary container.

## Conserve water without cross-contamination
When water is limited, prioritize underwear, towels used for personal hygiene and clothing needed for safe temperature control. Rewearing an uncontaminated outer layer may be preferable to spending safe water on unnecessary washing. Do not use chemically suspect water for hand-laundering clothing that will contact skin.

## Drying is part of cleaning
Damp textiles stored in a pile can develop odor and microbial growth. Create airflow and drying space without blocking exits, heaters or electrical equipment. In wet disaster recovery, reducing indoor humidity and drying materials promptly can be as important as the wash step.

## Protect the person doing the work
Wash hands after handling dirty laundry and before touching clean items. If the incident requires gloves or other protective equipment, remove them without contaminating the clean side. People with respiratory disease, weakened immunity or other vulnerability should avoid mold/sewage cleanup tasks when public-health guidance recommends it.

## Reopen normal laundry deliberately
After water/power returns, inspect the machine and surrounding area for flood/sewage exposure before use. Clean contaminated work surfaces as appropriate and run only after the utility is safe. Document textile losses that matter for recovery claims instead of mixing them back into the household inventory.
